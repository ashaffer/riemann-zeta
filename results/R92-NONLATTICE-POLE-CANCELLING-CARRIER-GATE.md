# R92 nonlattice pole-cancelling carrier gate

Status: an exact nonlattice scalar carrier survives.  A continuous Pareto
average of real dilations has a nonnegative, bounded floor-discrepancy
density and multiplier

```text
(s-1)/(s(s+1)).
```

Its only multiplier zero in `Re(s)>0` is the pole-cancelling zero at `s=1`.
Consequently its Mellin zeros in that half-plane are exactly the zeta zeros.
This removes the artificial-zero obstruction of R91, but does not prove a
zero-free strip: nonvanishing of the surviving positive transform is now
literally equivalent to zeta nonvanishing.  Discrete real dilations,
Beatty/Sturmian sequences, shifted lattices, and normally convergent signed
Dirichlet multipliers do not give the same escape.

Date: 2026-08-07.

## 1. Verdict

The recurrence theorem in R91 is sharp with respect to discreteness.  Put

```text
dmu(q)=2q^(-3)dq,                  1<=q<infinity,
N=floor(x),
A(x)=floor(x)-integral_[1,infinity) q floor(x/q)dmu(q).
```

Then the integral can be evaluated exactly:

```text
A(x)=N(N+1-x)/x,                  N<=x<N+1.             (1.1)
```

In particular,

```text
0<=A(x)<=1.                                                (1.2)
```

The positive measure `A(x)dx/x`, or equivalently the positive logarithmic
density `A(exp(u))du`, has Mellin/Laplace transform

```text
integral_1^infinity A(x)x^(-s-1)dx
   =[(s-1)/(s(s+1))]zeta(s),             Re(s)>0.       (1.3)
```

The factor

```text
P_*(s)=(s-1)/(s(s+1))                                  (1.4)
```

has no zero in `Re(s)>0` other than `s=1`.  At `s=1` its zero cancels the
zeta pole and the value of (1.3) is `1/2`.  Therefore

```text
integral_1^infinity A(x)x^(-s-1)dx=0, Re(s)>0
       if and only if
zeta(s)=0.                                               (1.5)
```

This is the scalar carrier that the discrete eta construction could not
produce.  Its delay law is genuinely nonlattice: if `q=exp(V)`, then
`V` has the continuous exponential density `2exp(-2v)dv`.  Its Fourier
transform decays instead of recurring.

There is also a precise cost.  The density in (1.1) decreases from `1` to
`0` between consecutive integers and then jumps back to `1`.  It is not
monotone, not a cumulative positive counting measure, and not generated by
an ordinary Dirichlet multiplier.  Since (1.3) already vanishes at every
nontrivial zeta zero, positivity alone cannot prove its nonvanishing.  A
successful next argument has to exploit more than scalar positivity -- for
example, a signed joint estimate tied to the exact sawtooth geometry.

## 2. Continuous dilation calculus

Let `mu` be a probability measure on `[1,infinity)`.  Define

```text
D_mu(s)=1-integral q^(1-s)dmu(q),                       (2.1)

A_mu(x)=floor(x)-integral q floor(x/q)dmu(q).           (2.2)
```

The floor in the second term vanishes for `q>x`, so (2.2) is pointwise
meaningful without any moment hypothesis.  For `Re(s)>1`, Tonelli and the
substitution `x=qy` give

```text
s integral_1^infinity floor(x)x^(-s-1)dx=zeta(s),       (2.3)

s integral_1^infinity q floor(x/q)x^(-s-1)dx
   =q^(1-s)zeta(s).                                     (2.4)
```

It follows that

```text
s integral_1^infinity A_mu(x)x^(-s-1)dx
   =D_mu(s)zeta(s).                                     (2.5)
```

This identity does not assert that `A_mu` is nonnegative.  That is the
additional gate.  It also makes the discrete/continuous distinction exact.
Writing `q=exp(v)` and letting `nu` be the law of `v`,

```text
D_mu(1+z)=1-phi(z),
phi(z)=integral_0^infinity exp(-zv)dnu(v).              (2.6)
```

Thus the artificial multiplier zeros are precisely the solutions of
`phi(z)=1`.

## 3. Why a continuous delay can avoid recurrence

The following theorem separates the R91 obstruction from the surviving
case.

**Theorem 3.1 (continuous nonlattice delays have an artificial zero-free
band).**  Suppose

```text
dnu(v)=h(v)dv,
h>=0,
integral h(v)dv=1,
integral exp(eta_0 v)h(v)dv<infinity                  (3.1)
```

for some `eta_0>0`, and suppose the law is not concentrated at `v=0`.
Then there is a `delta>0` such that

```text
1-phi(z)!=0
```

throughout `Re(z)>=-delta`, apart from its simple zero at `z=0`.

### Proof

The exponential moment makes `phi` holomorphic in
`Re(z)>-eta_0`.  Its zero at the origin is simple because

```text
(1-phi)'(0)=integral v h(v)dv>0.                       (3.2)
```

For real `t!=0`, equality in

```text
abs(phi(it))<=integral h(v)dv=1                       (3.3)
```

would require `exp(-itv)` to be constant almost everywhere on a set of
positive Lebesgue measure.  Hence `phi(it)!=1` away from `t=0`.

There is also no recurrence at high frequency.  For each fixed
`0<delta_0<eta_0`, the family

```text
{exp(-sigma v)h(v):-delta_0<=sigma<=0}                 (3.4)
```

is compact in `L^1`, because it is the continuous image of a compact
interval.  The Riemann--Lebesgue lemma, uniformly on this compact family,
gives

```text
sup_(-delta_0<=sigma<=0)|phi(sigma+it)| -> 0
                                      as abs(t)->infinity. (3.5)
```

Near zero, (3.2) isolates the simple zero.  On a compact part of the
imaginary axis separated from zero, (3.3) gives a positive minimum of
`abs(1-phi(it))`.  Continuity preserves that minimum after moving a small
fixed distance to the left.  Equation (3.5) handles the remaining large
heights.  Combining the three regions proves the theorem.  QED.

This is the exact opposite of a normally convergent discrete delay law.
For a discrete law its Fourier transform is almost periodic and returns
arbitrarily close to `1`; for an absolutely continuous law its Fourier
transform decays to `0`.

The exponential law makes the distinction explicit.  If

```text
h_lambda(v)=lambda exp(-lambda v),
```

then

```text
phi(z)=lambda/(lambda+z),
1-phi(z)=z/(lambda+z).                                  (3.6)
```

There are no recurrent zeros at all.  The choice `lambda=2` is special
because it also makes the floor discrepancy (2.2) nonnegative in closed
form.

## 4. The exact Pareto carrier

Take

```text
dmu(q)=2q^(-3)dq.                                      (4.1)
```

This is a probability measure.  Its logarithmic image is the exponential
law in (3.6) with `lambda=2`, and therefore

```text
integral_1^infinity q^(1-s)dmu(q)
   =2 integral_1^infinity q^(-s-2)dq
   =2/(s+1),                         Re(s)>-1.          (4.2)
```

Consequently

```text
D_mu(s)=(s-1)/(s+1).                                   (4.3)
```

It remains to verify positivity rather than assume it.  Put `N=floor(x)`.
Using

```text
floor(x/q)=sum_(1<=n<=N) 1_(q<=x/n),                   (4.4)
```

one gets

```text
integral q floor(x/q)dmu(q)
 =2 sum_(n<=N) integral_1^(x/n) q^(-2)dq
 =2 sum_(n<=N)(1-n/x)
 =2N-N(N+1)/x.                                         (4.5)
```

Subtracting this from `N` proves

```text
A(x)=N(N+1-x)/x.                                       (4.6)
```

For `N<=x<N+1`, both factors in the numerator are nonnegative and

```text
A(x)<=(N/x)*1<=1.                                      (4.7)
```

Thus its Mellin integral converges and is holomorphic for `Re(s)>0`.
Equation (2.5), initially valid for `Re(s)>1`, and analytic continuation
now give

```text
integral_1^infinity A(x)x^(-s-1)dx
 =[(s-1)/(s(s+1))]zeta(s),             Re(s)>0.        (4.8)
```

There is no hidden singularity at `s=1`; the value is

```text
lim_(s->1) [(s-1)/(s(s+1))]zeta(s)=1/2.                (4.9)
```

Equations (4.6)--(4.9) prove all claims in Section 1.

The shape is worth recording.  On every open interval `(N,N+1)`,

```text
dA/dx=-N(N+1)/x^2<0,                                   (4.10)
```

while

```text
A(N)=1,             lim_(x->(N+1)-)A(x)=0.             (4.11)
```

So the density performs one full downward sweep per integer and then jumps
up.  In terms of the fractional part, away from integers,

```text
A(x)=(1-{x})(1-{x}/x).                                 (4.12)
```

It approaches the first Bernoulli sawtooth `1-{x}`, not a monotone
function of `x` or of `log(x)`.

## 5. What the survivor does and does not prove

Let

```text
C(u)=A(exp(u)).                                        (5.1)
```

Then `0<=C<=1` and

```text
integral_0^infinity C(u)exp(-su)du
 =[(s-1)/(s(s+1))]zeta(s).                             (5.2)
```

This is an explicit positive Laplace transform having every nontrivial
zeta zero.  It therefore supplies a particularly clean reality check:

```text
generic positivity of a Laplace density cannot imply nonvanishing
in the right half-plane.                               (5.3)
```

The monotone-density theorem of R91 cannot be used, because (4.10)--(4.11)
give infinitely many upward jumps.  Regular convolution cannot remove the
zeta zeros: it only multiplies (5.2) by the transform of the smoothing
kernel.  Likewise, any theorem declaring this sawtooth transform nonzero
in `Re(s)>1-eta` would already prove the same zero-free strip for zeta.

The carrier nevertheless improves the formulation.  It removes all
eta-type artificial zeros without a vector norm, and it leaves a fully
explicit bounded positive density.  Any new mechanism can now be tested
directly against the local pieces

```text
C(u)=N(N+1-exp(u))/exp(u),
log(N)<=u<log(N+1).                                    (5.4)
```

The missing input is cancellation or variation control across the integer
jumps, not construction of a pole-cancelling scalar factor.

## 6. A single noninteger dilation loses positivity

For a fixed real `q>1`, consider the formally identical carrier

```text
A_q(x)=floor(x)-q floor(x/q).                          (6.1)
```

Its transform is exact:

```text
s integral_1^infinity A_q(x)x^(-s-1)dx
  =(1-q^(1-s))zeta(s),                 Re(s)>1,        (6.2)
```

and by continuation wherever the left side converges.  However, positivity
is rigid.

**Proposition 6.1.**  `A_q(x)>=0` for almost every `x>=1` if and only if
`q` is an integer.

### Proof

If `q` is an integer, then

```text
A_q(x)=floor(x) mod q,
```

so it lies in `{0,...,q-1}`.  If `q` is not an integer, take

```text
q<=x<ceil(q).
```

Throughout this interval `floor(x/q)=1` and `floor(x)=floor(q)`.  Hence

```text
A_q(x)=floor(q)-q<0                                    (6.3)
```

on a set of positive length.  QED.

Thus replacing the integer eta dilation by one irrational or rational
noninteger dilation removes exact periodicity only by destroying the
positive staircase.  The continuous average in Section 4 is not a
notational version of that failed step: it averages the negative pieces in
exactly the Pareto proportion needed to obtain (4.6).

## 7. Every discrete delay still recurs

Noninteger and shifted delays do not help if the delay law remains
discrete.

**Theorem 7.1 (discrete-delay recurrence).**  Let

```text
nu=sum_(j>=1)p_j delta_(v_j),
p_j>0,
sum p_j=1,
v_j>0,
sum p_j exp(eta_0 v_j)<infinity                        (7.1)
```

for some `eta_0>0`.  Put

```text
P(z)=1-sum_j p_j exp(-zv_j).                           (7.2)
```

Then `P` has zeros `z_n` with

```text
abs(Im(z_n))->infinity,
Re(z_n)->0,
Re(z_n)<=0.                                           (7.3)
```

If the support is not contained in a common lattice `h Z`, the recurrent
zeros can be chosen with `Re(z_n)<0`.

### Proof

The exponential moment gives normal convergence in
`Re(z)>-eta_0`.  Simultaneous Diophantine approximation on any fixed
finite subset of the frequencies `v_j`, followed by control of the
normally small tail, produces `tau_n->infinity` for which

```text
P(z+i tau_n)->P(z)                                     (7.4)
```

uniformly on a small disc about zero.  The zero at zero is simple because

```text
P'(0)=sum_j p_jv_j>0.                                  (7.5)
```

Rouche's theorem therefore copies it to zeros
`i tau_n+o(1)`.  There are no zeros in `Re(z)>0`, since there

```text
abs(sum_j p_j exp(-zv_j))<1.                           (7.6)
```

On the imaginary axis, equality to `1` requires
`exp(-itv_j)=1` for every `j`.  A nonzero such `t` exists exactly when the
support lies in one common lattice.  This proves the last assertion.  QED.

A shifted lattice `v_j=v_0+jh` is therefore no escape.  If `v_0/h` is
rational, it is still a common lattice and has exact boundary repeats.  If
`v_0/h` is irrational, simultaneous approximation of `v_0` and `h`
creates recurrent zeros just to the left.  A finite collection of
incommensurable real dilations behaves the same way.

The essential feature of Section 4 is not merely an irrational ratio.  It
is the continuous delay spectrum and the resulting Riemann--Lebesgue
decay.

## 8. Beatty and Sturmian carriers

Beatty geometry does provide positive pole-cancelled transforms, but not
an independently holomorphic zeta divisor.

Let `alpha>1` be irrational and define the upper mechanical word

```text
w_alpha(n)=ceil(n/alpha)-ceil((n-1)/alpha) in {0,1}.   (8.1)
```

Its ones form a shifted Beatty sequence.  The partial sums satisfy

```text
sum_(n<=N)w_alpha(n)=ceil(N/alpha),                    (8.2)

0<ceil(N/alpha)-N/alpha<1.                             (8.3)
```

Therefore the pole-cancelled Dirichlet series

```text
D_alpha(s)=sum_(n>=1)(w_alpha(n)-1/alpha)n^(-s)        (8.4)
```

converges and is holomorphic in `Re(s)>0`.  If

```text
B_alpha(x)=ceil(floor(x)/alpha)-floor(x)/alpha,
```

then `0<B_alpha(x)<1` and Abel summation gives the exact positive
representation

```text
D_alpha(s)/s
 =integral_1^infinity B_alpha(x)x^(-s-1)dx,
                                      Re(s)>0.         (8.5)
```

This looks promising, but it is a different factorization.  In
`Re(s)>1` one may of course write

```text
D_alpha(s)=zeta(s)Q_alpha(s),
Q_alpha(s)=D_alpha(s)/zeta(s).                         (8.6)
```

The quotient is not an independently continued multiplier.  At every zeta
zero `rho`, holomorphy of `Q_alpha` would require

```text
D_alpha(rho)=0.                                       (8.7)
```

Establishing (8.7) throughout a fixed strip is already a joint zeta--Beatty
zero statement, not a consequence of (8.3).

There is an elementary finite-factor obstruction.  If

```text
Q(s)=sum_(d<=D)c_d d^(-s),
```

then the coefficient of `n^(-s)` in `zeta(s)Q(s)` is

```text
sum_(d|n,d<=D)c_d,                                     (8.8)
```

a periodic function of `n` with period dividing
`lcm{d:c_d!=0}`.  The irrational mechanical word is aperiodic: a periodic
binary word has rational density, while its density here is `1/alpha`.
Thus (8.4) cannot equal zeta times a finite ordinary Dirichlet multiplier.

For rational `alpha=a/b` in lowest terms, the word becomes periodic of
period `a`, and

```text
sum_n w_alpha(n)n^(-s)
 =a^(-s)sum_(r=1)^a w_alpha(r)zeta(s,r/a).             (8.9)
```

Subtracting `(b/a)zeta(s)` removes the principal mean, but leaves the
nonzero additive Fourier modes of the residue pattern.  It produces a
combination of Hurwitz zeta or root-of-unity polylogarithms, not a scalar
factor known independently to divide zeta.

Complementary Beatty sequences do not alter the conclusion.  Their two
Dirichlet series sum to zeta exactly, but a residue-cancelling signed
difference retains the aperiodic discrepancy series (8.4).  The exact
identity is vector/additive, not a zero-free scalar multiplier.

## 9. Shifted lattices and uniqueness of exponents

A genuinely shifted counting lattice leads naturally to Hurwitz zeta:

```text
sum_(n>=0)(n+a)^(-s)=zeta(s,a).                        (9.1)
```

If `a` is irrational, a pole-cancelled combination involving (9.1) cannot
equal `zeta(s)Q(s)` with `Q` an ordinary absolutely convergent Dirichlet
series in any right half-plane.  Indeed, the product on the right is an
ordinary Dirichlet series supported at integer bases, whereas (9.1) is a
generalized Dirichlet series supported at the distinct noninteger bases
`n+a`.  Uniqueness of absolutely convergent generalized Dirichlet series
forces all coefficients at those noninteger bases to vanish, a
contradiction.

For rational `a=r/q`, scaling converts (9.1) to a residue-class series:

```text
zeta(s,r/q)=q^s sum_(m congruent r mod q)m^(-s).       (9.2)
```

The pole-cancelled part again consists of nonprincipal residue modes.  One
can formally divide any ordinary residue-class series by zeta in
`Re(s)>1`, but continuing that quotient across zeta zeros is precisely the
same common-zero problem as (8.7).

So shifts have two outcomes:

```text
irrational shift   loses the ordinary integer Dirichlet spectrum;
rational shift     retains extra Hurwitz/character modes.              (9.3)
```

Neither outcome yields the clean explicit divisor (1.4).

## 10. Signed Dirichlet multipliers

Allowing signs does not evade recurrence when the advertised fixed margin
comes from normal convergence.  If

```text
Q(s)=sum_(n>=1)b_n n^(-s),
sum_(n>=1)|b_n|n^(-1+eta_0)<infinity,
Q(1)=0,                                                (10.1)
```

then Theorem 6.2 of R91 copies the zero at `1` to zeros

```text
s_j=1+i tau_j+o(1),             abs(tau_j)->infinity. (10.2)
```

The proof is the same normal-convergence and Rouche argument as Theorem
7.1, now with frequencies `log(n)`.  Coefficient signs never enter it.

The multiplier in the surviving carrier is genuinely outside this class.
For example,

```text
(s-1)/(s(s+1))=1/s-2/(s+1).                            (10.3)
```

It decays only polynomially on the positive real axis.  If it were an
ordinary Dirichlet series absolutely convergent at some real `sigma_0`,
then its limit as `sigma->infinity` would be its first coefficient.  That
coefficient would be zero, and the remaining tail would satisfy

```text
abs(sum_(n>=2)b_n n^(-sigma))
 <=2^(-(sigma-sigma_0))
      sum_(n>=2)|b_n|n^(-sigma_0)
 =O(2^(-sigma)),                                        (10.4)
```

contradicting the `1/sigma` decay in (10.3).  Similarly, the Abel-normalized
factor `(s-1)/(s+1)` approaches `1` with a `1/sigma` correction, whereas a
normally convergent Dirichlet series approaches its first coefficient
exponentially fast.

Conditional ordinary multipliers remain logically possible, but they do
not give a free strip.  The model example is

```text
1/zeta(s)=sum_(n>=1)mu(n)n^(-s),                       (10.5)
```

whose continuation and regularity in a fixed strip are equivalent to the
desired zeta zero-free statement.  Thus there are exactly two ways out of
the recurrence theorem:

```text
continuous/non-Dirichlet spectrum   -- explicit, as in Section 4;
conditional arithmetic spectrum    -- strip-equivalent, as in (10.5).   (10.6)
```

## 12. Exact next target

The carrier-construction problem is solved at the scalar level by (4.8).
The next target should not be another search for a pole-cancelling factor.
It is the following concrete theorem.

**Sawtooth strip target.**  Prove that for some fixed `eta>0`,

```text
integral_0^infinity
  A(exp(u))exp(-(sigma+it)u)du !=0                    (12.1)
```

uniformly for

```text
sigma>1-eta,               t in R,                    (12.2)
```

where `A` is the explicit function (4.6).

By (4.8), (12.1)--(12.2) are equivalent to the desired fixed zero-free
strip.  The useful new feature is that there are no artificial multiplier
zeros and no vector common-zero condition.  The hard feature is equally
clear: the unit-interval downward sweeps must be controlled jointly across
all integer jumps.  Any proposed positivity, variation-diminishing,
renewal, or cancellation theorem can now be falsified directly on this
single explicit density before it is imported into the zeta argument.

## 13. Bottom line

The discrete recurrence obstruction is not universal.  A genuinely
nonlattice continuum of dilation delays gives the exact positive identity

```text
integral_1^infinity
  [floor(x)(floor(x)+1-x)/x] x^(-s-1)dx
   =[(s-1)/(s(s+1))]zeta(s),             Re(s)>0.      (13.1)
```

It has no eta-type zeros to work around.  This is a real simplification,
but not a proof of a strip: the transform's remaining zeros are exactly the
zeta zeros.  Beatty, shifted-lattice, and normally convergent arithmetic
variants either restore recurrence, lose positivity, or hide the zeta
problem in the continuation of a quotient.  The R92 survivor therefore
changes the next question from

```text
how do we remove the scalar carrier's artificial zeros?
```

to the sharper question

```text
what zeta-specific property forbids cancellation among the explicit
descending floor sawteeth in (13.1)?
```
