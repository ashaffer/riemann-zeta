## 3. FP1: compact-filtration localization

### 3.1 Abstract theorem

Let `I subset R` be an interval, let `H` and `G` be Hilbert spaces, let
`J:H->G` be compact, and let `(E_t)_{t in I}` be an increasing family of
closed subspaces of `H`.  Write `P_t` for the `H`-orthogonal projection onto
`E_t`.  At every point where the indicated side exists, assume

\[
 \overline{\bigcup_{s\in I,\ s<t}E_s}=E_t,
 \qquad
 \bigcap_{s\in I,\ s>t}E_s=E_t.                    \tag{3.1}
\]

At a left or right endpoint only the available one-sided identity is
required.  All continuity below is in the relative topology of `I`.

Then `t -> J P_t J^*` is operator-norm continuous.  On every nonempty compact
parameter interval `K subset I`, the optimal resolvent modulus

\[
 \omega_K(\delta)=
 \sup_{s,t\in K,\ |s-t|\le\delta}
       \|J(P_s-P_t)J^*\|_{G\to G}                   \tag{3.2}
\]

satisfies `omega_K(delta) -> 0` as `delta` decreases to zero, where (3.2) is
defined for `delta>=0`.

In particular, if `s,t in K` and `s<t`, and

\[
 w\in E_t\cap E_s^{\perp_H},
\]

then, more precisely,

\[
 \sup_{0\ne w\in E_t\cap E_s^{\perp_H}}
 {\|Jw\|_G^2\over\|w\|_H^2}
 =\|J(P_t-P_s)J^*\|
 \le \omega_K(t-s).                                \tag{3.3}
\]

If `E_t cap E_s^perp={0}`, the supremum in (3.3) is defined to be zero,
matching the operator norm.

### 3.2 Proof

The monotone-subspace identities (3.1) imply strong continuity of `P_t` from
both sides.  If `t_n -> t`, then `P_{t_n}-P_t -> 0` strongly; these
differences are self-adjoint and uniformly bounded.  For a finite-rank `F`,
strong convergence on its finite-dimensional adjoint range gives

\[
 \|F(P_{t_n}-P_t)\|\longrightarrow0.
\]

Approximate the compact `J` in norm by `F` to obtain norm convergence of
`J(P_{t_n}-P_t)` and hence of its product with `J^*`.  For nested
projections, `P_t-P_s` is itself the orthogonal projection onto
`E_t cap E_s^perp`; therefore

\[
 J(P_t-P_s)J^*=(J(P_t-P_s))(J(P_t-P_s))^*,
\]

which proves the exact norm formula (3.3).  Continuity on compact `K` is
uniformly continuous, proving (3.2).

This argument is not a finite-codimensional compression of a noncompact
Hankel operator.  Hartman/AAK essential-norm obstructions therefore do not
apply: the compact map is the form-domain embedding `J`, and the small
operator is `J(P_t-P_s)`.

### 3.3 Logarithmic Weil form domain

Fix a bounded support window `I_B`.  Give its logarithmic form domain the
reference norm

\[
 \|f\|_{\mathfrak h}^{2}
 =\|f\|_2^2+{1\over2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
   |\widehat f(\tau)|^2 {1\over2}\log(1+4\tau^2)\,d\tau.       \tag{3.4}
\]

The inclusion

\[
 J_B:H_B^{\mathfrak h}\longrightarrow L^2(I_B)       \tag{3.5}
\]

is compact.  Indeed, fixed support gives physical tightness; the multiplier
in (3.4) tends to infinity and gives uniform Fourier tails; and

\[
 \|f(\cdot+y)-f\|_2^2
 ={1\over2\pi}\int |e^{i\tau y}-1|^2|\widehat f(\tau)|^2\,d\tau
\]

is uniformly small after splitting at a large frequency.  The
Riesz--Frechet--Kolmogorov criterion then gives compactness.

Let `E_L` be either the full form domain supported in `I_L`, or its joint
kernel under a fixed finite family of bounded moments whose restrictions have
the required constant rank locally in `L>0`.  These are closed increasing
subspaces of `H_B^h`.  The decreasing intersection identity is immediate
from support.  For increasing density, inward dilations converge in the
weighted Fourier norm.  In the moment-relative case, at each limiting support
`L>0` choose fixed smooth interior correctors `psi_1,...,psi_m` in a smaller
common core, valid throughout a neighborhood of that `L`, with invertible
moment matrix, and subtract the vanishing moment error.  On a compact positive
slab one common core works uniformly; no core uniform down to `L=0` is
claimed.  This preserves strict interior support and tends to zero in
`H_B^h`.  Thus (3.1) holds, including for the two-moment relative spaces used
in the semilocal branch; the two exponential moments have the required rank.
Typing is important here: the relative `L2` ambient space is the closed joint
moment kernel, and the relative form domain is dense in that space, not in the
full `L2(I_L)`.

In the full-space case, if `T_L` is the positive self-adjoint operator in
`L2(I_L)` associated with the restricted reference form and `i_L` is zero
extension, its embedded resolvent is exactly

\[
 K_L=i_LT_L^{-1}i_L^*=J_BP_L^{\mathfrak h}J_B^*.    \tag{3.5a}
\]

For a moment-relative `E_L`, the associated operator instead lives on
`H_L^{rel}=L2(I_L) cap ker ell`; (3.5a) remains true with all embeddings,
adjoints, and projections taken in the relative ambient spaces.

Hence for `L<L'`,

\[
 K_{L'}-K_L=J_B(P_{L'}^{\mathfrak h}-P_L^{\mathfrak h})J_B^*\ge0,
\]

and its norm is the supremum in (3.3).  The same identity holds in the
correctly typed relative ambient space.

Consequently the exact relative reference-localization statement needed for
propagation is

\[
 \|w\|_2^2\le\omega_B(L'-L)\,\mathfrak h(w,w),
 \qquad \omega_B(\delta)\downarrow0,                \tag{3.6}
\]

for every `h`-harmonic new direction
`w in E_{L'} cap E_L^{perp_h}`.  No `1/log(1/delta)` rate is required.

The Weil application in this report uses real Hilbert spaces, matching the
repository form.  Every statement has the usual complex Hermitian analogue;
whenever positivity is used to kill a cross coefficient, one tests both real
and imaginary scalar phases.

## 4. FP2: the Fredholm--Ward kernel criterion

### Theorem

Let `H` be a real Hilbert space, let `G` be a real normed space, and

\[
 q(u,v)=\langle (I+K)u,v\rangle_H,
\]

where `K` is compact self-adjoint and `q>=0`.  Let `N=ker(I+K)`.  Let
`r(u,w)` be a bilinear cross pairing satisfying

\[
 |r(u,w)|\le M\|u\|_H\|w\|_G.                      \tag{4.1}
\]

Then the following are equivalent:

1. `r(n,w)=0` for every `n in N` and every `w`;
2. there is `C<infinity` such that
   \[
   |r(u,w)|^2\le C q(u,u)\|w\|_G^2                 \tag{4.2}
   \]
   for every `u,w`.

The implication `2 => 1` is immediate.  Conversely, `I+K` is Fredholm,
`N` is finite-dimensional, and its restriction to `N^perp` has a positive
spectral floor `lambda`.  After discarding the kernel component of `u`,

\[
 |r(u,w)|^2\le M^2\|u\|_H^2\|w\|_G^2
 \le {M^2\over\lambda}q(u,u)\|w\|_G^2.             \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the dangerous inverse is not infinite-dimensional at a fixed support:
it is exactly the finite-dimensional nullspace obstruction.
The complex version is identical with a Hermitian form and sesquilinear
cross pairing.

### Weil application

On a bounded support slab, polarize the Weil form as

\[
 Q=\mathfrak h+R.                                   \tag{4.4}
\]

The digamma multiplier minus the logarithmic reference multiplier is
bounded.  The pole term is finite rank, and only finitely many prime-power
translations are active.  Hence

\[
 |R(f,g)|\le M_B\|f\|_2\|g\|_2.                    \tag{4.5}
\]

Since `J_B` is compact, the operator representing `R` in the `h` norm is
compact.  Therefore `Q=I+compact` in that norm.  If `Q_L>=0`, FP2 shows that
the fixed-support harmonic-residual Ward estimate on a specified new
harmonic space is equivalent to the single condition

\[
 \boxed{
 R(n,w)=0
 \quad(n\in\ker Q_L,\quad
       w\in E_{L'}\cap E_L^{\perp_{\mathfrak h}}).}
                                                               \tag{KNC}
\]

This is **kernel null-charge**, abbreviated `KNC`.  It is necessary for any
positive enlargement: a positive semidefinite quadratic form cannot have a
nonzero cross term out of a zero-energy vector.

Equivalently, `KNC` says that each `n in ker Q_L` remains an
Euler--Lagrange nullvector on the enlarged domain:

\[
 Q(n,v)=0\qquad(v\in E_{L'}).                       \tag{KNC'}
\]

Indeed, the radical identity already gives this on `E_L`, while
`E_{L'}=E_L\oplus_{\mathfrak h}(E_{L'}\cap E_L^{\perp_{\mathfrak h}})` and
`h(n,w)=0` on the second summand.  Thus the open arithmetic input is persistence of a
first-contact nullstate through an arbitrarily thin support germ.

## 5. FP3: qualitative, gap-free local extension

Suppose `Q_L>=0`, exact nested-support cancellation identifies the old block
at `L'` with `Q_L`, and `KNC` holds.  Let

\[
 f=u+w,
 \qquad u\in E_L,
 \qquad w\in E_{L'}\cap E_L^{\perp_{\mathfrak h}}.
\]

Put

\[
 A=Q_L(u,u),\qquad S=\mathfrak h(w,w).
\]

FP1, FP2, and (4.5) give, for locally finite constants `C_L,M_B`,

\[
 \|w\|_2^2\le\omega_B(L'-L)S,                       \tag{5.1}
\]

\[
 |R(u,w)|^2\le C_L A\|w\|_2^2,                     \tag{5.2}
\]

\[
 R(w,w)\ge-M_B\|w\|_2^2.                           \tag{5.3}
\]

Writing `omega=omega_B(L'-L)` and completing a square yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_{L'}(f,f)
 &\ge A-2\sqrt{C_L\omega AS}+(1-M_B\omega)S\\
 &=\bigl(\sqrt A-\sqrt{C_L\omega S}\bigr)^2
   +\bigl(1-(M_B+C_L)\omega\bigr)S.                \tag{5.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Since `omega_B(delta)->0`, every semidefinite support satisfying `KNC` has a
nonempty right neighborhood of semidefinite supports.  This uses neither a
uniform old spectral floor nor an explicit localization rate.

## 6. FP4: charged-nullstate first contact and removal of non-Zeno

### Abstract propagation theorem

Let `(E_L)_{L>=L_0}` and `Q_L` be compatible nested families such that, for
every finite outer support `B>L_0`, their restrictions to `L in [L_0,B]`
satisfy FP1--FP3 in one fixed outer ambient space.  No single compact
embedding on the unbounded-support union is assumed.  Assume:

1. `Q_{L_0}>=0`;
2. positivity is inherited by smaller supports;
3. for every `L>L_0`, `union_{L_0<=L'<L}E_{L'}` is form-dense in `E_L`;
4. every singular semidefinite `Q_L` satisfies `KNC` along a sequence of
   support enlargements decreasing to `L`.

Then `Q_L>=0` for every finite `L>=L_0`.

Proof.  Let `S={L>=L_0:Q_L>=0}`.  It is nonempty and downward closed.  If its
supremum `L_*` were finite, then `Q_{L_*}>=0` follows directly from the base
hypothesis when `L_*=L_0`, and from left density and form continuity when
`L_*>L_0`.  FP3 would then extend positivity to some support strictly larger
than `L_*` (with kernel charge automatic if the kernel is zero and supplied by
assumption 4 otherwise), a contradiction.  Thus `S` is unbounded and, being
downward closed, contains every finite support.

The argument works with arbitrarily shrinking local neighborhoods.  There is
no event mesh to sum and therefore no separate non-Zeno hypothesis.

### First-contact equivalence

In the compactly embedded Weil filtration, the following statements are
equivalent, relative to the standard explicit-formula and Weil-criterion
bridges already isolated in the project:

1. global Weil positivity;
2. starting at the certified base, every singular semidefinite support has
   kernel null-charge along arbitrarily thin reference-harmonic enlargements;
3. no first loss of positivity occurs through a **charged nullstate**.

The nontrivial direction is `2 => 1`, proved above.  For `1 => 2`, take a
nullvector `n` and a new direction `w`.  Positivity of
`Q(n+t w,n+t w)` for every real `t` forces the cross coefficient to vanish.

For the full form spaces, global positivity implies RH by the formalized
classical Weil interface.  A two-moment relative version removes the pole
channel and has the same propagation proof.  Here is the normalization
adapter, rather than an appeal to the words "two moments."  For

\[
 \widetilde g(s)=\int_0^\infty g(u)u^s\,{du\over u},
 \qquad f(x)=e^{x/2}g(e^x),                         \tag{6.1}
\]

one has exactly

\[
 \ell_-(f)=\widetilde g(0),\qquad
 \ell_+(f)=\widetilde g(1).                         \tag{6.2}
\]

With the normalization-matched project Weil form, the sign translation is

\[
 q_{\rm project}(f)
 =-\sum_v {\cal W}_v(g*\overline g^{\,\sharp}).     \tag{6.3}
\]

Thus project nonnegativity is precisely the nonpositivity convention in
Connes--Consani, *Selecta Mathematica* 27 (2021), Appendix C, Proposition C.1,
with the finite vanishing set `{0,1}`.  That proposition is global/cofinal; it
does not make positivity at one fixed support RH-equivalent.

The last-mile quantifier audit strengthens and qualifies this criterion.  At
a fixed semidefinite support `a`, the following are equivalent: `KNC` at one
larger support, on one right neighborhood, or along an arbitrarily thin
sequence.  Nesting proves the collapse.  Because `ker Q_a` is finite
dimensional, nullvector-dependent germs also have one common subgerm.
Moreover, under FP1--FP3, germ `KNC` at `a` is equivalent to positivity
extending to any larger support near `a`.

Consequently **universal completed-zeta `KNC` is RH-equivalent**, relative to
the already accepted Weil and support-continuity interfaces.  It is a sharper
normal form for the hard arithmetic, not an independently weaker global
conjecture.  Its source radical is finite-dimensional at each contact, but it
must annihilate an infinite-dimensional harmonic collar germ.  Thus it is a
finite family of functional identities, not a finite list of scalar checks.
The dedicated last-mile audit also gives an exact warning: logarithmic
Dirichlet ground states are charged into every larger collar by weak unique
continuation.  The zeta proof must therefore establish cancellation of that
reference antilocal flux by the **combined** pole, archimedean, and prime
source; principal-symbol regularity or compact support alone points the wrong
way.

## 7. FP5: arbitrary singular-value-envelope Ward catastrophe

The previous all-Schatten countermodel can be strengthened.  Let `r` be a
finite constraint budget, let `eta_k>0` be any nonincreasing sequence with
`eta_k->0`, and let `M_n>=0` with `M_n->infinity` be arbitrary.  There is a genuine unitary
projection defect with multiplicity `r+1` such that, after every codimension
at-most-`r` constraint:

- the favorable leakage and visible Ward blocks have singular values bounded
  by the prescribed envelope `eta_k` (after harmless reindexing);
- the reverse leakage retains essential norm one;
- the selected Ward quotient is at least `M_n`.

Choose defect eigenvalues

\[
 0<\delta_n\le {1\over4}\eta_{(r+1)n}^{2},
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_n={\delta_n\over1+M_n^2}.              \tag{7.1}
\]

Mix a `+delta_n` vector with the `-1` reservoir exactly as in the earlier
projection-defect construction.  Then

\[
 A_n=\varepsilon_n,
 \qquad
 |B_n|^2=(1+\varepsilon_n)(\delta_n-\varepsilon_n), \tag{7.2}
\]

and

\[
 {|B_n|\over\sqrt{A_n}}\ge M_n.                    \tag{7.3}
\]

At the same time `|B_n|<=sqrt(2 delta_n)<=eta_n`, with the multiplicity
choice defeating the finite constraints.  Hence **no separate spectral-ideal
membership or prescribed compact singular-value decay can by itself imply
Ward stability**.  A valid positive conclusion needs additional relative or
joint structure; factorization, a reserve theorem, and a `KNC`-type condition
are three such mechanisms.  The envelope must be strictly positive; an
eventually zero envelope would demand finite rank and is a different
statement.

## 8. FP6: sharp multichannel reserve budget

Let a cross term decompose as `B=sum_i B_i` over a finite channel set.  Assume
`A,S,C_i,G_i,omega_i,M_i>=0` and suppose

\[
 |B_i(u,w)|^2\le C_i A(u)G_i(w),
 \qquad
 G_i(w)\le\omega_i S(w),                            \tag{8.1}
\]

and the collar diagonal satisfies

\[
 D(w)\ge S(w)-\sum_iM_iG_i(w).                      \tag{8.2}
\]

Set

\[
 \Gamma=\left(\sum_i\sqrt{C_i\omega_i}\right)^2,
 \qquad
 \mathsf M=\sum_iM_i\omega_i.                      \tag{8.3}
\]

Then

\[
 A+2\operatorname{Re}B+D
 \ge(\sqrt A-\sqrt{\Gamma S})^2
      +(1-\mathsf M-\Gamma)S.                      \tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the condition $\Gamma+\mathsf M\le1$ suffices even when `A` is only
semidefinite.  The proof is
the triangle inequality followed by (8.1) and one square completion.  Aligned
one-dimensional channels attain every inequality, so this budget is sharp
under componentwise hypotheses alone.  Any better zeta estimate must use
additional joint structure, for example cancellation in the combined source,
not merely improve the same componentwise bookkeeping.

## 9. FP7 and FP12: regulator and anomaly closure

### Absolute-tail regulator universality

Use normalized Haar measure `dmu=dtheta/(2 pi)` on the circle.  For the
interior singular Hardy anomaly, let `Sigma` be a nonempty closed null set,
let `u` be measurable, unimodular, and locally `W^{1,infinity}` off `Sigma`,
and let bounded `g` obey, with the local essential derivative envelope
`Lambda_u` and `delta(x)=dist(x,Sigma)`,

\[
 |g|(1+\Lambda_u+\delta^{-1})\in L^1.              \tag{9.0}
\]

The empty-set case is the separate ordinary smooth theorem.  Under (9.0),
spatial positivity and evenness of the convolution kernels are unnecessary.
It is enough that convolution operators `T_j` with kernels `h_j` are trace
class and that those kernels obey

\[
 \int h_j\,d\mu=1,
 \qquad \sup_j\|h_j\|_1<\infty,
 \qquad
 \int_{d(r,0)>\eta}|h_j(r)|\,d\mu(r)\to0
 \quad(j\to\infty)                                  \tag{9.1}
\]

for every `eta>0`.  Signed and complex approximate identities are allowed.
The proof uses the same removable-diagonal limit and replaces positivity by
the absolute `L1` domination in (9.1).  A common square-root sandwich still
requires the convolution operators to be spectrally positive so that the
square roots are Hilbert--Schmidt.

Thus the finite-singular-set theorem extends to any nonempty closed null set
under the displayed domination.  Separately, if there are finitely many
jumps, `u` is piecewise `C1` up to them, and bounded `g` is piecewise
continuous with one-sided traces, every even normalized uniformly-`L1`
absolute-tail kernel whose convolution operator is trace class gives the same
boundary term

\[
 {1\over4\pi}
 \left[-ig_+(1-\overline{u_+}u_-)
       +ig_-(1-\overline{u_-}u_+)\right].           \tag{9.2}
\]

In the convolution orientation of the singular report, if the positive- and
negative-side masses tend to `m_+` and `m_-`, respectively, the coefficients
of the displayed `g_+` and `g_-` terms become `m_-/(2 pi)` and `m_+/(2 pi)`;
the masses satisfy `m_++m_-=1`, and the even case has `m_+=m_-=1/2`.
Without those limits
only subsequential boundary formulas, and possibly no full trace limit, are
asserted.  These statements concern the total regulated defect, not the four
individual Hardy blocks.

There is no power-only corollary for an arbitrary closed null set: nullity
does not control the rate at which its metric neighborhoods shrink.  For a
nonempty closed null set `Sigma`, put `delta(x)=dist(x,Sigma)`.  If

\[
 \mu\{x:\operatorname{dist}(x,\Sigma)<r\}=O(r^\kappa)
 \qquad(0<\kappa\le1),                              \tag{9.3}
\]

and, near `Sigma`,
`|u'|<=C delta^{-alpha}(1+|log delta|)^k` and
`|g|<=C_g delta^beta`, where `alpha,k>=0`, then the domination hypothesis
follows from

\[
 \beta>\max(\alpha-\kappa,1-\kappa).               \tag{9.4}
\]

This is a dyadic-shell calculation.  A finite singular set has `kappa=1`,
recovering `beta>max(alpha-1,0)`.  The thickness exponent is necessary in the
statement even though no optimality claim is made for every fractal set.

### Iterated anomaly chain rule

For bounded operators `U,W_1,...,W_m` and `W=W_1...W_m`,

\[
 [W,U]=\sum_{j=1}^m
 W_1\cdots W_{j-1}[W_j,U]W_{j+1}\cdots W_m.         \tag{9.5}
\]

Equation (9.5) is a bounded-operator algebra identity.  It may be substituted
into the exact projection-defect trace ledger in finite dimension.  In
infinite dimension, every product both before and after each cyclic rotation
must be trace class (it suffices that each cyclic word have a designated
trace-class factor and all remaining factors be bounded), or one must invoke
a separately proved common-regulator theorem that licenses the cyclic moves
and the limiting passage.  Trace class of only the final displayed
commutator summands is not sufficient.  Under these hypotheses the rule
compresses finite multiregulator boundary budgets; it creates no new zeta
positivity input.

## 10. FP8: provenance moduli and data processing

For metric maps `U:X->Y` and `T:X->Z`, with nonempty `X`, and for `r>=0`,
define the extended-valued modulus

\[
 \omega_{T\mid U}(r)=
 \sup\{d_Z(Tx,Tx'):d_Y(Ux,Ux')\le r\}.              \tag{10.1}
\]

Its values lie in `[0,infinity]`; finiteness is an additional conclusion, not
part of the definition.

Then:

- `T` descends set-theoretically through `U` exactly when
  `omega_{T|U}(0)=0`;
- the induced decoder is uniformly continuous exactly when
  `omega_{T|U}(r)->0`;
- its optimal Lipschitz constant is
  `sup_{r>0} omega_{T|U}(r)/r`.

If `V` is `L`-Lipschitz with `L>0`,

\[
 \omega_{T\mid U}(r/L)\le\omega_{T\mid VU}(r).      \tag{10.2}
\]

If `V|_{U(X)}` is injective and its inverse
`V(U(X))->U(X)` is `M`-Lipschitz, with `M>=0`,

\[
 \omega_{T\mid VU}(r)\le\omega_{T\mid U}(Mr).      \tag{10.3}
\]

Finally, if a decoder `D:Y->Z` has uniform target error at most `epsilon`,
write `omega_D` for its ordinary modulus restricted to the observed set
`U(X)`.  Then

\[
 \omega_{T\mid U}(r)\le2\varepsilon+\omega_D(r).   \tag{10.4}
\]

This turns the qualitative same-fiber obstruction into a condition-number
ledger.  An invertible change of representation can move instability but
cannot erase it; any claimed gain must appear either in the transported norm
or in the Lipschitz constants of the adapter and its inverse.

## 11. FP9: infinite equicontinuous certificate banks still collapse

Let `K` be a compact metric space, let `A subset K` be the admissible set,
let `a_n in A` and forbidden `b_n in K` satisfy `d(a_n,b_n)->0`, and let the
nonempty family `F subset C(K,R)` be equicontinuous.  Require every
certificate to be
globally valid on the entire admissible class:

\[
 f(a)\le0\qquad(f\in F,\ a\in A).
\]

Then

\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{f\in F}f(b_n)\le0.       \tag{11.1}
\]

Indeed, a common modulus `rho` gives
`f(b_n)<=rho(d(a_n,b_n))`.  Thus not only a finite list, but any infinite or
adaptive **equicontinuous** continuous certificate bank fails to separate the
two classes with a fixed margin.  On compact `K`, a finite `delta`-net also
gives

\[
 \sup_K f\le\max_{x\text{ in the net}}f(x)+\rho(\delta).       \tag{11.2}
\]

This strictly strengthens the earlier finite-certificate lemma.  It applies
to any finite-window `DPA/LTRAD` diagnostic, low-order moment certificate, or
bounded-bandwidth separation attempt **after** the compact model, globally
valid common bank, and accumulating admissible/forbidden pair sequence have
been exhibited.  The recorded prime-density pseudonode models meet those
hypotheses only at their licensed model fidelity; this is not an actual-prime
impossibility theorem.  Within its scope, an escaping family must lose a
common equicontinuity modulus.  In concrete certificate classes this may
appear as diverging bandwidth, derivative/Hölder norm, depth, or condition
number.  Non-equicontinuous spike families show that the added hypothesis is
necessary.

## 12. FP10: observation--synthesis polarity and atomic gauges

For a bounded linear map `U:X->Y` of normed spaces, a scalar target
`t in X^*`, and `C>=0`, the stable observation estimate

\[
 |t(x)|\le C\|Ux\|\quad(x\in X)                    \tag{12.0}
\]

holds exactly when `t=U^*y^*` for some `y^* in Y^*` with
`||y^*||<=C`.  The least constants agree.  Define the functional on
`ran U` by `Ux -> t(x)` and apply Hahn--Banach.  This identifies scalar
stable descent with minimum-cost dual synthesis; mere kernel inclusion gives
only algebraic descent.

Let `S:E->X` be a bounded linear surjection of Banach spaces.  Define the
minimum synthesis cost

\[
 \gamma_S(x)=\inf_{Se=x}\|e\|_E.                   \tag{12.1}
\]

Hahn--Banach gives the polar formula

\[
 \gamma_S(x)=
 \sup\{|\phi(x)|:\|S^*\phi\|_{E^*}\le1\}.         \tag{12.2}
\]

This is the intrinsic quotient norm induced by `E/ker S`; by the open mapping
theorem it is equivalent to the given Banach norm on `X`.  If `E'` is Banach
and `R:E'->E` is a bounded linear isomorphism and the norm
is transported, `gamma_{SR}=gamma_S` exactly.  With fixed native norms, the
division-free comparison (also valid for zero Banach spaces) is

\[
 \gamma_S(x)\le\|R\|\gamma_{SR}(x),
 \qquad
 \gamma_{SR}(x)\le\|R^{-1}\|\gamma_S(x).           \tag{12.3}
\]

For a closed convex resource set `C` containing zero, replace the unit ball
by `C` and use the possibly extended-valued gauge/polar of `closure(S(C))`.
A fixed-mass simplex does not contain zero: its feasibility theory uses
support-function separation, or one first homogenizes it, rather than
applying the gauge statement literally.
Exact invariance requires transporting the **entire** body to `R^{-1}C`;
transporting a norm while changing positivity, support, or normalization
changes the theorem.

Consequences:

- the natural-mask atomic lower bound is a dual quotient certificate;
- finite-moment correction cost is the norm of a right inverse;
- superoscillatory or affine reparameterization gains are paid by
  conditioning unless target-specific cancellation changes the synthesis
  map itself.

This package does not by itself subsume R71's exact signed recombination or
spectral restitution; those retain additional problem-specific algebra.

## 13. FP11: two cross-branch firewalls

### Cone-to-linear firewall

A bound on rank-one positive inputs need not give dimension-free control on
the ambient operator.  On real symmetric `n by n` matrices with Frobenius
norm, let `L(X)=tr(X)`.  For every unit `u`,

\[
 |L(uu^*)|=\|uu^*\|_F=1,
\]

including for entrywise nonnegative `u`, whereas

\[
 \|L\|_{F\to\mathbb R}=\sqrt n.                    \tag{13.1}
\]

Therefore the stable-descent/Douglas package cannot promote FC's restricted
coefficient/XDC estimate to unrestricted Hilbert control without an explicit
cone-to-linear polarization theorem and its dimensional loss.

### All-cut nontransfer and odd-channel polarization

For every tensor order `k`,

\[
 \lambda^{\otimes k}-\eta^{\otimes k}
\]

has matrix rank at most two under **every** flattening, simply because it is a
difference of two rank-one tensors.  Thus the formal all-cut secant condition
alone supplies no rigidity for CA4 mask differences.  The existing SR2PF
rigidity comes from the additional distinct-prime entries, positive
decomposable approximation at scale `B^{-1}`, and integrality.

Writing `s=lambda+eta` and `delta=lambda-eta`, and setting
`v_j^J=delta` for `j in J` and `v_j^J=s` otherwise, gives the exact
position-preserving polarization

\[
 \lambda^{\otimes k}-\eta^{\otimes k}
 =2^{1-k}\sum_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\\|J|\text{ odd}}}
   \bigotimes_{j=1}^k v_j^J.                       \tag{13.2}
\]

Order two is exceptionally a two-channel identity.  At order at least three,
the universal formal expansion contains higher odd-mark channels (individual
terms can of course vanish for special `lambda,eta`).  This blocks an
unjustified extension of the CA4 product-flux decomposition to arbitrary
tensor order.

## 14. Reapplication to the project decision tree

### 14.1 Weil branch

The old chain was

\[
 \text{base positivity}+NR_{\log}+HRW+\text{non-Zeno}
 \Longrightarrow\text{global positivity}.          \tag{14.1}
\]

The fixed-point chain is

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{base positivity}+KNC\text{ at semidefinite contacts}
 \Longrightarrow\text{global positivity}\Longrightarrow RH.} \tag{14.2}
\]

Compact form embedding supplies qualitative localization; Fredholmness
supplies Ward off the kernel; maximal-support continuation supplies
cofinality.  `NR_log` is demoted from a required theorem to a possible
quantitative refinement.  Uniform compact-slab `HRW` remains open, but its
role in qualitative continuation is replaced by the exact finite-kernel
condition `KNC`.  Non-Zeno is removed.  This replacement is a simplification
of the proof architecture, not of the global logical strength: universal
completed-zeta `KNC` is equivalent to RH under these interfaces.

The best next analytic target is consequently not another global floor or a
sharper collar rate.  It is a source-specific classification of the finite
kernel of `Q_L` and its residual boundary charge.  A useful theorem would
either prove `ker Q_L={0}` at every semidefinite support or prove `KNC`
directly from the completed-zeta source.

### 14.2 QP/Turan strip branch

No theorem in the recursion supplies `DPA_P(.019)` or
`LTRAD_P(.0189,.001)`.  FP8 and FP9 sharpen the negative information inside
the recorded compact prime-density pseudonode models: a fixed-resolution or
equicontinuous test family cannot certify their required uniform separation.
This does not promote a pseudonode model to an actual-prime counterexample.
A successful route must introduce genuinely arithmetic non-equicontinuity
while retaining controlled cost.  The uniform strip remains open.

### 14.3 FC/CA4/four-cycle branch

FP11 closes two tempting but invalid shortcuts.  All-cut rank-two secancy of
a pure-tensor difference is automatic, and restricted positive-cone control
does not linearize dimension-freely.  The sharp four-cycle theorem still
requires coefficient-sensitive cancellation on its actual restricted range.
It is not a proved strip gate.

### 14.4 R71/R87/R89 and mask routes

FP8 and FP10 show that coordinate changes, affine serialization, moment
repair, and atomic masks share one quotient-conditioning ledger.  They do not
remove the target-specific signed recombination contacts.  The prior closed
statuses therefore remain closed; no apparent preconditioning gain is
promoted without paying the inverse adapter norm.

### 14.5 Semilocal trace branch

FP7 enlarges the regulator class for the total anomaly; its presently proved
semilocal application is the triangular-hat finite span (or a separately
stated smooth class with the same decay), not the entire logarithmic form
domain.  FP12 handles iterated regulators under its stated trace-ideal or
common-regularization hypothesis.  FP5 simultaneously prevents any argument from
inferring Ward stability from compactness or spectral-ideal decay of visible
Hardy blocks.  The valid bridge to positivity is the kernel-charge criterion,
not separate block trace convergence.

## 17. Final minimal research program

The recursion leaves two honest high-level programs, with no claimed adapter
between them:

1. **Weil/RH:** classify semidefinite compact-support Weil nullspaces and
   prove they carry no residual charge into the reference-harmonic support
   germ.  This is `KNC`.  Qualitative localization, Ward away from the
   kernel, and global continuation then follow automatically.  Universal
   success is exactly RH-strength; the value of this formulation is that it
   specifies the required completed-source exterior cancellation.
2. **Uniform strip:** obtain a genuinely arithmetic, resolution-uniform
   separation mechanism strong enough for `DPA/LTRAD`.  FP8--FP9 say exactly
   why bounded-complexity continuous certificates cannot provide it.

The corrected basis is now certified relative to the frozen grammar and V4
snapshot.  Further consolidation by the same operator returns the same basis;
progress requires new arithmetic input or a genuinely new proof language, not
another renaming pass.
