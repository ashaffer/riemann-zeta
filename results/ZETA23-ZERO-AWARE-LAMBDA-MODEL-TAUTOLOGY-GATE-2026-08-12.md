# The zero-aware `Lambda` model is an exact residue projection, not a strip engine

Date: 2026-08-12  
Status: focused theorem/gate report; no canonical publication files changed

## 0. Verdict

The zero-aware model

\[
 \widetilde\Lambda_{X,\varepsilon}(n)
 =1-\sum_{\substack{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\\
          \beta\geq 1-10\varepsilon\\ |\gamma|\leq X^{1.1}}}
        n^{\rho-1}
\]

does **not** combine with its power-saving approximation theorem, candidate-conditioned
phase geometry, or known zero density to exclude even one zero with
`beta > 1-delta`.  There is an exact obstruction, stronger than a mere
failure of present estimates:

1. every zero included in the model is canceled residue-for-residue in the
   Dirichlet series of `Lambda - Lambda_tilde`;
2. every zero omitted by the horizontal cutoff has, on the actual carrier,
   size below the theorem's `X^(-8 epsilon)` remainder;
3. a zero omitted by the vertical cutoff cannot be resonantly tested in the
   theorem's frequency range;
4. horizontal zero density permits one zero, and the power-saving exceptional
   set measures intervals, not new zero ordinates.

Thus this machinery is excellent for proving short-interval distribution
*despite* possible near-one zeros.  That is exactly why it cannot by itself
prove that those zeros do not exist.

The strongest reusable outputs are the residue-annihilation lemma in Section 2
and the coverage--remainder lemma in Section 4.

## 1. Exact imported statement and a normalization warning

The source is Joni Teräväinen,
[*Almost All Alternating Groups are Invariably Generated by Two Elements of
Prime Order*](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnac354), especially Definition 5.2,
Lemma 5.3, Proposition 5.4, and Lemma 5.6.

Throughout its Section 5, `epsilon > 0` is a sufficiently small absolute
constant.  In the proof of Lemma 5.3 it is harmlessly reduced to, for example,
`epsilon <= 10^(-8)`; Lemma 5.6 also assumes `epsilon <= 1/110` for one error
estimate.  Zeros are counted with multiplicity, as usual in an explicit-formula
sum.

Set

\[
 \mathcal Z_{X,\varepsilon}
 :=\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma:\ \beta\geq1-10\varepsilon,
                         \ |\gamma|\leq X^{1.1}\}.
\]

The two bounds relevant here are:

\[
 |\widetilde\Lambda_{X,\varepsilon}(n)-1|
 \ll \exp\!\bigl(- (\log X)^{0.33}\bigr),
 \qquad X^{0.1}\leq n\leq2X,                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

and, with

\[
 Q_1=\frac{X}{2h^{1-\varepsilon}},\qquad
 Q_2=\frac{X}{h^{1-2\varepsilon}},\qquad
 (\log X)^C\leq h\leq X^{1/9},                                   \tag{1.2}
\]

\[
 \left|\sum_{Q_1\leq n\leq Q_2}
  (\Lambda-\widetilde\Lambda)(n)n^{-1-it}\right|
 \ll |t|^{-1}+X^{-8\varepsilon},
 \qquad 2\leq |t|\leq X.                                         \tag{1.3}
\]

### Apparent one-unit typo in the displayed explicit formula

Exact normalization matters here.  The source's displayed explicit formula
inside the proof of Lemma 5.6 prints the zero denominator as `rho-it` (and the
main numerator with `Q^(it)`).  For the printed left side
`sum Lambda(n)n^(-1-it)`, Stieltjes integration gives instead

\[
 \sum_{Q_1\leq n\leq Q_2}\Lambda(n)n^{-1-it}
 =-\frac{Q_2^{-it}-Q_1^{-it}}{it}
  -\sum_{|\gamma|\leq X^{1.1}}
   \frac{Q_2^{\rho-1-it}-Q_1^{\rho-1-it}}
        {\rho-1-it}
  +\text{truncation error}.                                       \tag{1.4}
\]

The immediately following Perron calculation in the source has precisely the
required denominator `xi-1-it`, so (1.4) is also the normalization needed for
the stated cancellation and for Lemma 5.6.  The omission appears typographical;
it does not damage the power bound, because for omitted zeros
`1-beta >= 10 epsilon` and the extra reciprocal factor costs only a constant
depending on `epsilon` (plus logarithms).  All formulas below use (1.4).

## 2. Exact residue-annihilation lemma

### Lemma 2.1 (the discrepancy deletes every modeled zero)

For `Re(s)>1`, define

\[
 D_{X,\varepsilon}(s)
 :=\sum_{n\geq1}
   \frac{\Lambda(n)-\widetilde\Lambda_{X,\varepsilon}(n)}{n^s}.
\]

Then

\[
 D_{X,\varepsilon}(s)
 =-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(s)-\zeta(s)
  +\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z_{X,\varepsilon}}
      \zeta(s+1-\rho).                                            \tag{2.1}
\]

The meromorphic continuation of the right side is holomorphic at `s=1` and
at every `rho_0 in Z_(X,epsilon)`.  If `rho_0` has multiplicity `m`, the residue
calculation at `s=rho_0` is

\[
 \operatorname*{res}_{s=\rho_0}\left(-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta(s)\right)
 =-m,
 \qquad
 \operatorname*{res}_{s=\rho_0}
 \sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z_{X,\varepsilon}}\zeta(s+1-\rho)=+m.       \tag{2.2}
\]

At `s=1`, the residues of `-zeta'/zeta` and `-zeta` are `+1` and `-1`.

#### Proof

For `Re(s)>1`, use

\[
 \sum_{n\geq1}\Lambda(n)n^{-s}=-\frac{\zeta'}\zeta(s),\quad
 \sum_{n\geq1}n^{-s}=\zeta(s),\quad
 \sum_{n\geq1}n^{\rho-1-s}=\zeta(s+1-\rho).
\]

This gives (2.1).  The residue statements follow from the local logarithmic
derivative of `zeta` and from the residue `1` of `zeta` at `1`.  QED.

### Corollary 2.2 (candidate-conditioned linear geometry still cancels)

For every finitely supported complex weight `(a_n)`, including a weight chosen
after being told a hypothetical zero `rho_0`, one has the coefficientwise
identity

\[
 \sum_n a_n(\Lambda(n)-1)
 =-\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z_{X,\varepsilon}}
      \sum_n a_n n^{\rho-1}
  +\sum_n a_n(\Lambda-\widetilde\Lambda)(n).                       \tag{2.3}
\]

Choosing `a_n` to phase-match `rho_0` enlarges the `rho_0` term on the first
right-hand side.  It does not turn that term into an error or a contradiction;
it makes the prime statistic follow the modeled zero more visibly.

This is independent of smoothing, short-interval shape, multiple blocks,
Turan weights, or a Mellin/Fourier isolator.  All are linear functionals applied
to (2.3).

### Interpretation

`Lambda -> Lambda-Lambda_tilde` is a finite-rank polar subtraction.  Asking a
bound on its image to rule out a pole in its kernel is information-theoretically
backwards.  One must also bound the subtracted zero model independently.

## 3. The strongest candidate-conditioned test

Let a hypothetical zero be

\[
 \rho_0=1-\delta+iT,\qquad 0<\delta<0.01,\qquad T\geq2.           \tag{3.1}
\]

Take `t=T` in (1.4).  The phase is removed exactly.  The zero's individual
explicit-formula contribution has magnitude

\[
 \mathcal C_{\rho_0}(Q_1,Q_2)
 =\frac{Q_1^{-\delta}-Q_2^{-\delta}}{\delta}
 =Q_1^{-\delta}\frac{1-(Q_2/Q_1)^{-\delta}}{\delta}.               \tag{3.2}
\]

Since

\[
 \frac{Q_2}{Q_1}=2h^\varepsilon,
\]

\[
 |\mathcal C_{\rho_0}|
 \leq Q_1^{-\delta}\log(2h^\varepsilon)
 =X^{-q\delta+o(1)},                                               \tag{3.3}
\]

where `q=(log Q_1)/(log X)`.  From `h<=X^(1/9)`,

\[
 Q_1\geq\frac12X^{8/9+\varepsilon/9},
 \qquad q\geq 8/9+\varepsilon/9-o(1).                            \tag{3.4}
\]

This is the optimal elementary resonance in the actual Lemma 5.6 geometry.
For fixed `delta`, and with `delta log(2h^epsilon)` bounded below, (3.2) also
gives the matching power-size lower bound up to a constant depending on
`delta`.

No unmodulated short interval can improve this.  The modulation `n^(-iT)`
has already removed all vertical oscillation of the candidate.

## 4. Coverage--remainder lemma

### Lemma 4.1 (specific parameters)

At the resonant frequency `t=T`, exactly one of the following occurs.

#### Case A: `delta <= 10 epsilon`

Then `beta >= 1-10 epsilon`.  If `T<=X^(1.1)`, the zero is in
`Z_(X,epsilon)`, and its contribution (3.2) is canceled term-for-term by
`Lambda_tilde`.  This is Lemma 2.1 at finite carrier level.

#### Case B: `delta > 10 epsilon`

Then the zero is omitted horizontally, but (3.3)--(3.4) give

\[
 |\mathcal C_{\rho_0}|
 \leq X^{-(8/9+\varepsilon/9)\delta+o(1)}
 \leq X^{-80\varepsilon/9+o(1)}
 =o(X^{-8\varepsilon}).                                          \tag{4.1}
\]

Thus even perfect phase matching leaves the omitted candidate strictly below
the power remainder in (1.3).  The slack is `8 epsilon/9` in the exponent.

This covers every hypothetical zero with `beta>.99`, despite the source taking
`epsilon` extremely small: the extraordinarily near-one zeros are modeled;
all others are much smaller than the deliberately relaxed remainder.

### Lemma 4.2 (general form)

Suppose a zero-aware model on a carrier `n asymp X^q` includes all zeros with
`1-beta <= Delta_0` and omits the rest.  At a phase-matched zero, the first
omitted mode has natural size

\[
 X^{-q\Delta_0+o(1)}.                                              \tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently a correct uniform approximation after deleting only the included
zeros cannot, without a new theorem excluding omitted zeros, have a remainder
strictly smaller than (4.2).  A candidate is therefore either:

- included, hence canceled; or
- omitted, hence no larger than the honest residual floor.

Changing the carrier from `X^(8/9)` to a smaller power does not evade this
lemma.  It makes omitted zeros larger, but it raises the unavoidable residual
by exactly the same scale.

### Vertical cutoff

There is no vertical-cutoff loophole.  The pointwise theorem tests
`|t|<=X`, while its zero model reaches `|gamma|<=X^(1.1)`.

- To resonate with a zero at height `T`, one needs `t=T`, hence `X>=T`; then
  automatically `T<=X^(1.1)` and the zero is vertically included.
- When a zero first enters as `X^(1.1)` crosses `T`, every permitted
  `|t|<=X` is a distance `asymp T` below it.  Its explicit-formula term has
  the extra denominator `|rho-1-it|^(-1) << T^(-1)` and is nonresonant.

So comparing models just below and just above the vertical cutoff cannot expose
a hidden resonant jump.

### Varying `epsilon` across the horizontal cutoff

Nor can one compare two `epsilon` values.  If `epsilon_- < delta/10`, the
candidate is omitted from the first model, but

\[
 q\delta>\frac89(10\varepsilon_-)
           =\frac{80}{9}\varepsilon_->8\varepsilon_-.             \tag{4.3}
\]

Hence its entire jump is smaller than that model's `X^(-8 epsilon_-)` error.
For `epsilon_+ >= delta/10`, it is exactly canceled.  Even granting identical
carriers and constants, the triangle inequality cannot see the jump.

## 5. Why the independent model bound and zero density do not rescue the route

### 5.1 Lemma 5.3 is subpower, while one fixed-depth zero is power-small

The model itself obeys (1.1), not a fixed power bound.  A fixed modeled
candidate at depth `delta>0`, evaluated anywhere in the stated range, has

\[
 |n^{\rho_0-1}|=n^{-\delta}
 \leq X^{-0.1\delta}
 =o\!\left(\exp\bigl(- (\log X)^{0.33}\bigr)\right).               \tag{5.1}
\]

Thus even an oracle that perfectly isolated the candidate frequency would
produce a lower bound far below the available upper bound.  Turan, Nazarov,
large-sieve, or logarithmic-Fourier isolation cannot repair this ordering.

The source proves (1.1) from a Vinogradov--Korobov zero-free region plus a
near-one zero-density estimate.  The exponents are intentionally arranged so
that every zero still permitted by the zero-free region is individually below
the coarser `0.33` upper scale.

### 5.2 Modern horizontal density still allows one

Guth--Maynard's current global estimate is

\[
 N(\sigma,T)\leq
 T^{\frac{30}{13}(1-\sigma)+o(1)}.                                \tag{5.2}
\]

See [*New large value estimates for Dirichlet polynomials*](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2026.203.2.6).
At `sigma=1-delta`, the right side is

\[
 T^{(30/13)\delta+o(1)},                                          \tag{5.3}
\]

a positive power for every fixed `delta>0`.  It can make the candidate set
sparse; it cannot make its cardinality less than one.

A candidate creates one resonant peak near `t=T`.  Varying the interval center
or the arithmetic scale creates many observations of the **same** peak, not
new zeta zeros.  Hence interval-phase alignment cannot be fed back into (5.2)
as zero replication.

### 5.3 The pointwise model bound already used density in the wrong direction

Zero density enters the proof of (1.1) by the absolute upper bound

\[
 \left|\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}n^{\rho-1}\right|
 \leq\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}n^{\beta-1}.                          \tag{5.4}
\]

This forgets target-conditioned signs and can never yield a lower bound from
one candidate.  Trying to reintroduce an isolator after (5.4) cannot recover
information discarded by the triangle inequality; and, by (5.1), even perfect
recovery would be too small.

## 6. Why the power-saving exceptional set does not turn one zero into a contradiction

Proposition 5.4 bounds a variance of a convolution containing

\[
 \Lambda(n_1)(\Lambda-\widetilde\Lambda)(n_2).                    \tag{6.1}
\]

The hypothetical near-one zero is in `Lambda_tilde` whenever it is large
enough to threaten this residual estimate.  Therefore the variance theorem
says that the prime convolution tracks the zero-aware prediction on almost all
intervals.  A favorable phase of the zero gives a favorable phase of the prime
statistic, not a violation of the estimate.

The final zero-blind semiprime asymptotic has an error of logarithmic relative
size.  For `n_2` on the actual carrier, one fixed-depth candidate contributes
at most

\[
 Q_1^{-\delta}\ll X^{-(8/9+o(1))\delta}=o((\log X)^{-A})          \tag{6.2}
\]

for every fixed `A`.  So it is also swallowed by that final error.

The exceptional set is a set of interval centers `x`.  Known zero density is a
count of ordinates `gamma`.  A deterministic wave from one zero may have a
chosen sign on a positive proportion of centers, but those good centers merely
verify the modeled wave.  There is no map from them to distinct zero ordinates.

## 7. Positivity and mean-killing have an exact dichotomy

Could one use positivity of `Lambda` or of semiprime counts after matching the
candidate phase?

- With nonnegative weights, the main term is positive and of size equal to the
  total weight.  The candidate changes it by only the relative factor
  `X^(-q delta)`, so it cannot force a negative count.
- To delete the main term, a nonzero weight must change sign (or be complex).
  Then coefficient positivity supplies no one-sided inequality.

Squaring a centered statistic restores nonnegativity but turns the candidate
into a compatible positive energy.  No independent power-small upper bound for
that energy is supplied by the zero-aware theorem.

This closes the direct positivity/phase-geometry escape without assuming any
randomness of the primes.

## 8. A finite-scale countermodel for arguments using only the imported estimates

The set `Z_(X,epsilon)` is closed under complex conjugation, so
`Lambda_tilde(n)` is real.  By (1.1), for all sufficiently large `X`,

\[
 \widetilde\Lambda(n)>0
 \qquad (X^{0.1}\leq n\leq2X).                                    \tag{8.1}
\]

On this finite range set

\[
 a_X(n):=\widetilde\Lambda(n).                                    \tag{8.2}
\]

Then `a_X` is positive, has main value `1` up to the stated subpower error,
and has **zero** discrepancy from the zero-aware model, while retaining every
mode in `Z_(X,epsilon)`.  Therefore no argument that uses only

- positivity,
- the main value `1`,
- short-interval linear statistics, and
- a power bound for `a_X-Lambda_tilde`

can logically exclude a modeled zero.

This countermodel does not have prime support.  Exploiting exact prime-power
support in a genuinely nonlinear way would be extra input, not a consequence
of the zero-aware approximation or zero density.  The Teräväinen convolution
estimate itself does not supply such a one-zero contradiction.

## 9. What would actually be sufficient

One of the following genuinely new inputs would be needed.

1. **A zero-blind power approximation.**  Approximate `Lambda` by a model whose
   Mellin transform is independently holomorphic through the proposed strip,
   with error smaller than `X^(-q delta)` at the candidate frequency.  This is
   already a near-equivalent form of a zero-free theorem.

2. **A power bound on the zero model itself.**  Prove, with an isolator of
   subpower norm,
   \[
     \left|\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal Z}n^{\rho-1}\right|\ll n^{-\eta}
   \]
   for `eta>delta`.  For a fixed finite zero set, elementary uniqueness of
   exponential polynomials then excludes every mode with
   `Re(rho)>1-eta`; this is again the desired strip information, not an
   available corollary of density.

3. **A per-zero replication theorem.**  Turn one candidate into more than
   `T^((30/13)delta+o(1))` distinct zeros at comparable height.  Reobserving
   the same modeled wave at many interval centers is not replication.

4. **A resonant comparison across model cutoffs with no coverage gap.**  The
   present vertical margin `X^(1.1)` and horizontal exponent inequality
   `(8/9)10 epsilon > 8 epsilon` explicitly prevent this.

5. **A prime-support-specific one-sided centered inequality.**  It must retain
   a sign after deleting the constant mode and must not factor through
   `Lambda-Lambda_tilde`.  No such inequality occurs in the imported theorem.

## 10. Hostile checks

1. **Multiplicity:** the residue cancellation requires the standard convention
   that the zero sum counts multiplicity.  With that convention it is exact.

2. **Printed denominator:** using `rho-it` rather than the algebraically correct
   `rho-1-it` would falsely remove the resonant `1/delta` factor and would fail
   to match the next Perron display.  Restoring the shift changes only
   logarithmic/`epsilon` constants in the gate, not the exponent dichotomy.

3. **The `1/|t|` term:** at a candidate of height `T`, take `X=T`; then
   `T^(-1)=o(T^(-8 epsilon))` for the source's `8 epsilon<1`.  It does not
   create a hidden contradiction.

4. **Very small `delta`:** if `delta<=10 epsilon`, exact modeling applies.  If
   `delta>10 epsilon`, (4.1) applies.  There is no uncovered boundary layer.

5. **A candidate just outside the vertical cutoff:** it is far outside the
   permitted resonant frequencies and pays a denominator of order its height.

6. **Perfect spectral isolation:** it can at best recover
   `X^(-q delta+o(1))`; this is canceled in the discrepancy and is below the
   independent model upper bound.

7. **Latest zero density:** replacing the density input by
   `30(1-sigma)/13` improves global counts in some ranges but never changes a
   positive power count into `N<1`.

---

## 11. Final assessment

The zero-aware short-interval technology does not tighten a uniform zero-free
strip.  It proves a sharper and conceptually opposite statement: after
projecting out every dangerous zero mode, the remaining prime signal has a
power-saving Dirichlet-polynomial bound and a power-saving exceptional set.

The route therefore terminates in the following exact gate:

\[
 \boxed{\text{dangerous zero modeled}\Rightarrow\text{exactly canceled};
 \quad
 \text{dangerous zero unmodeled}\Rightarrow\text{below honest remainder}.}
\]

Any successor should act on the zero model, or replicate an individual zero;
further refinement of the discrepancy estimate alone moves in the wrong
quotient space.
