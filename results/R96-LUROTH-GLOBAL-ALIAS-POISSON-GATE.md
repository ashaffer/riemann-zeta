# R96 Luroth global-alias Poisson and finite-part gate

Status: all Luroth stationary aliases are summed with a global Poisson
decomposition.  Their local Gaussian coefficient matches exactly the
functional-equation gamma multiplier, while their global signed amplitude
is the renormalized finite part `zeta(1-s)`.  The continuum subtraction is
forced by the overlapping high-index aliases and has the same scale as the
raw sum.  A new absolutely convergent dual Euler-defect carrier is derived,
but it has the same integer aliases and the functional-equation iteration is
an exact neutral two-cycle.  There is no residual sign or phase constraint.
No fixed zero-free strip, and no failure of every fixed strip, is proved.

Date: 2026-08-07.

## 1. Verdict

R95 evaluated every fixed stationary window of the Luroth digit sum.  Its
`k`-th leading contribution was

```text
common_factor * k^(s-1).                              (1.1)
```

The question left open was whether summing *all* windows creates a new
global sign.  It does not.  The exact global amplitude is

```text
D(s)=lim_(K->infinity)
 [sum_(k<=K)k^(s-1)-K^s/s]

    =zeta(1-s),                    0<Re(s)<1.         (1.2)
```

The subtraction `K^s/s` is not optional regularization.  It is the continuum
field generated by the densely overlapping aliases with large `k`.  Without
it, the alias sum diverges at size `K^Re(s)`.

Put

```text
P(s)=(s-1)/[s(s+1)],

chi(s)=2^s pi^(s-1)sin(pi s/2)Gamma(1-s).            (1.3)
```

The complete global alias identity is

```text
C(s)=P(s)zeta(s)=P(s)chi(s)D(s).                     (1.4)
```

This audit establishes five sharper facts behind (1.4).

1. A finite Luroth head has an exact Poisson decomposition into its
   zero-frequency continuum, boundary field, positive stationary modes, and
   negative nonstationary modes.
2. For fixed `k`, stationary phase gives the R95 block constant.  Extending
   the Gaussian window to its natural full width produces exactly the
   Stirling leading term of `P(s)chi(s)`, including phase.
3. The global finite part in (1.2) is forced.  It contains no common cone:
   its terms have the flexible phases `k^(it)`, while its counterterm rotates
   with `K`.
4. The finite part can be rewritten as an absolutely convergent series of
   dual Euler defects.  Those defects have one real sign for `0<s<1`, but at
   complex height they pass every alias `K approximately t/(2 pi m)` with
   unit limiting response.
5. Applying the alias/functional-equation transform twice returns to the
   original carrier because `chi(s)chi(1-s)=1`.  There is no contraction,
   gain in real part, or damping to iterate.

Thus the exact global alias picture is now complete.  A nonvanishing theorem
for it is not a consequence of Luroth positivity: it is exactly a
nonvanishing theorem for the complementary zeta value.  R96 closes Poisson,
van der Corput, all-window phase summation, and recursive alias
renormalization as independent mechanisms.

## 2. Exact Poisson decomposition of a finite head

Recall from R95 that

```text
H_N(s)=sum_(n<=N)J_n(s)

 =[(s-1)S_N(s)+N(N+1)^(-s)]/[s(s+1)],               (2.1)

S_N(s)=sum_(n<=N)n^(-s).                             (2.2)
```

For `N>=2`, apply the symmetric finite Poisson formula to

```text
f(x)=x^(-s)1_[1,N](x),                               (2.3)
```

with half weight at both endpoints.  It gives

```text
S_N(s)
 =(1+N^(-s))/2
  +integral_1^N x^(-s)dx
  +lim_(M->infinity)
    sum_(0<abs(k)<=M) I_(k,N)(s),                    (2.4)

I_(k,N)(s)=integral_1^N x^(-s)exp(2 pi i kx)dx.      (2.5)
```

The mode limit is symmetric.  Substitution into (2.1) yields the exact
identity

```text
s(s+1)H_N(s)
 =B_N(s)
  +(s-1)lim_(M->infinity)
    sum_(0<abs(k)<=M)I_(k,N)(s),                     (2.6)

B_N(s)
 =1-N^(1-s)
  +(s-1)(1+N^(-s))/2
  +N(N+1)^(-s).                                      (2.7)
```

This displays the coefficient-specific recombination explicitly.  The
large pieces `-N^(1-s)` and `N(N+1)^(-s)` in (2.7) are the continuous
Euler field and the Luroth endpoint response.  Neither can be bounded
separately at a fixed power.

Assume `t>0`.  The phase of the positive `k` mode is

```text
Phi_k(x)=2 pi kx-t log x,                             (2.8)
```

with stationary point

```text
x_k=t/(2 pi k).                                      (2.9)
```

The negative modes have no stationary point.  If `N` is just above
`t/(2 pi)`, the positive stationary indices are

```text
1<=k<=t/(2 pi).                                      (2.10)
```

This is the promised all-branch partition: small fixed `k` give the
disjoint R95 Gaussian windows, indices of order `sqrt(t)` begin to overlap,
and the large indices merge with the continuum and boundary fields in
(2.7).  Summing only the disjoint windows omits the term that becomes
`-K^s/s` in (1.2).

## 3. The global finite-part alias theorem

Define

```text
D_K(s)=sum_(k=1)^K k^(s-1)-K^s/s.                   (3.1)
```

**Theorem 3.1 (renormalized all-alias sum).**  Locally uniformly in the
open critical strip,

```text
D(s):=lim_(K->infinity)D_K(s)=zeta(1-s).             (3.2)
```

More precisely, Euler--Maclaurin gives

```text
D_K(s)
 =zeta(1-s)+(1/2)K^(s-1)
  +O((1+abs(s))^2 K^(Re(s)-2))                       (3.3)
```

on compact substrips, with further Bernoulli terms available.

### Proof

Apply Euler--Maclaurin to `x^(s-1)`:

```text
sum_(k<=K)k^(s-1)
 =K^s/s+zeta(1-s)+(1/2)K^(s-1)
  +O((1+abs(s))^2K^(Re(s)-2)).                       (3.4)
```

Since `Re(s)-1<0`, every term after `zeta(1-s)` tends to zero.  QED.

The same identity follows directly from the Poisson modes in Section 2.
As a distributional Fourier pair, for `0<Re(s)<1`,

```text
integral_0^infinity x^(-s)exp(+2 pi i kx)dx
 =exp(+i pi(1-s)/2)Gamma(1-s)(2 pi k)^(s-1),

integral_0^infinity x^(-s)exp(-2 pi i kx)dx
 =exp(-i pi(1-s)/2)Gamma(1-s)(2 pi k)^(s-1).         (3.5)
```

Adding the two Fourier orientations gives

```text
2(2 pi)^(s-1)Gamma(1-s)sin(pi s/2) k^(s-1)
 =chi(s)k^(s-1).                                     (3.6)
```

Matching the zero-frequency and endpoint fields in (2.6)--(2.7) subtracts
the continuous integral `K^s/s`.  Hence symmetric Poisson summation gives

```text
zeta(s)=chi(s)D(s),                                  (3.7)
```

and multiplication by `P(s)` gives (1.4).

Equation (3.7) is the functional equation, but (2.4)--(3.6) identify exactly
how it arises from the Luroth resonant head.  In particular, there is no
unaccounted positive mode after the dual finite part has been formed.

## 4. Exact phase match with the local stationary calculation

For fixed `k`, stationary phase in (2.5) gives

```text
I_(k,infinity)(sigma+it)

 approximately
 (2 pi)^(sigma-1/2)t^(1/2-sigma)
 exp(i[t-t log(t/(2 pi))+pi/4])
 k^(sigma-1+it).                                     (4.1)
```

Multiplication by the Luroth prefactor

```text
(s-1)/[s(s+1)] approximately 1/(it)                 (4.2)
```

turns (4.1) into

```text
(2 pi)^(sigma-1/2)t^(-sigma-1/2)
exp(i[t-t log(t/(2 pi))-pi/4])
k^(s-1).                                             (4.3)
```

This is exactly the `c -> infinity` limit of the R95 stationary-window
constant, because

```text
integral_(-infinity)^infinity exp(2 pi^2 i u^2)du
 =exp(i pi/4)/sqrt(2 pi).                            (4.4)
```

There is also an exact multiplier check.

**Theorem 4.1 (gamma-phase closure).**  Uniformly for `sigma` in a compact
real interval and `t` tending to positive infinity,

```text
P(s)chi(s)

 =(2 pi)^(sigma-1/2)t^(-sigma-1/2)
  exp(i[t-t log(t/(2 pi))-pi/4])
  [1+O_sigma(1/t)].                                  (4.5)
```

### Proof

Use Stirling's formula in (1.3), keeping the dominant exponential from the
sine factor, and multiply by

```text
P(s)=1/(it)[1+O(1/t)].                               (4.6)
```

The magnitude is

```text
t^(-1)(t/(2 pi))^(1/2-sigma),                        (4.7)
```

and the combined phase is the one displayed in (4.5).  QED.

Thus the local Gaussian amplitude, the global Poisson Fourier transform,
and the exact functional-equation multiplier agree in both magnitude and
phase.  There is no residual determinant, sector, or boundary term left to
sign after `D(s)` is included.

## 5. Why the continuum subtraction cannot be discarded

The raw positive-length alias amplitudes have total magnitude

```text
sum_(k<=K)abs(k^(s-1))
 =sum_(k<=K)k^(sigma-1)
 asymp K^sigma/sigma.                                (5.1)
```

Their signed sum has the deterministic continuum term

```text
K^s/s,                                               (5.2)
```

of the same fixed-power size.  Only after subtracting (5.2) does the finite
limit (3.2) exist.  Hence all of the following lose the target information:

```text
absolute values of alias blocks;
one common sector for all k;
differentiating the cutoff without its endpoint field;
discarding overlapping high-k windows;
treating K^s/s as a harmless lower-order integral.                  (5.3)
```

The counterterm also has no stable phase.  Increasing the cutoff from `K`
to `lambda K` rotates it by

```text
exp(it log lambda).                                  (5.4)
```

For any fixed `lambda>1`, that phase is dense on the unit circle as `t`
varies.  A cutoff-independent half-plane cannot contain both the alias sum
and its required continuum subtraction.

The R95 finite-prime Kronecker theorem gives the complementary local
obstruction: even before the high-index continuum is reached, finitely many
actual stationary blocks can form arbitrarily small polygons.  Global
Poisson summation does not repair that flexibility; it records its finite
remainder as `zeta(1-s)`.

## 6. An absolutely convergent dual Euler-defect carrier

The finite part admits a second exact representation which is useful for
testing recursive cancellation ideas.  Its increments are

```text
d_K(s):=D_(K+1)(s)-D_K(s)

 =(K+1)^(s-1)-[(K+1)^s-K^s]/s.                      (6.1)
```

Since

```text
d_K(s)=[(s-1)/2]K^(s-2)
       +O_s(K^(Re(s)-3)),                            (6.2)
```

the following series converges normally on compact subsets of
`0<Re(s)<1`.

**Theorem 6.1 (dual defect carrier).**

```text
zeta(1-s)
 =(s-1)/s+sum_(K=1)^infinity d_K(s).                 (6.3)
```

For real `0<s<1`, every term on the right side is strictly negative.

### Proof

The identity telescopes from

```text
D_1(s)=1-1/s=(s-1)/s                                (6.4)
```

and Theorem 3.1.  For real `0<s<1`, the function `x^(s-1)` is strictly
decreasing, and

```text
[(K+1)^s-K^s]/s
 =integral_K^(K+1)x^(s-1)dx
 >(K+1)^(s-1).                                      (6.5)
```

Thus `d_K(s)<0`.  QED.

This real sign does not survive complex height.  The dual defect has the
same aliases as the original Luroth coefficient.

**Theorem 6.2 (unit response at every dual alias).**  Fix `m>=1`, let

```text
s_t=sigma+it,
K_t=nearest_integer(t/(2 pi m)).                     (6.6)
```

Then

```text
K_t^(1-s_t)d_(K_t)(s_t)->1.                          (6.7)
```

### Proof

Factor (6.1) as

```text
K^(1-s)d_K(s)
 =(1+1/K)^(s-1)
  -[K/s][(1+1/K)^s-1].                               (6.8)
```

At (6.6), the first term tends to `exp(2 pi i m)=1`, while the bracket in
the second tends to zero and its first correction is `O(1/K)`.  Since
`K/s` remains bounded, the second term vanishes.  QED.

Therefore the finite-part renormalization has not removed the resonant
mechanism.  It has moved it to another absolutely convergent Euler defect.
Local pairing of the `d_K` again fails at `K=t/(2 pi m)`.

## 7. Recursive Poisson renormalization is a neutral two-cycle

One might now repeat the Luroth or Poisson construction on the dual carrier
`D(s)=zeta(1-s)`.  The exact result is

```text
D(1-s)=zeta(s),                                      (7.1)

zeta(s)=chi(s)D(s),
D(s)=chi(1-s)zeta(s).                                (7.2)
```

The multipliers obey

```text
chi(s)chi(1-s)=1.                                    (7.3)
```

Thus two iterations return exactly to the starting value.  In modulus, the
first step trades the factor

```text
(t/(2 pi))^(1/2-sigma)                               (7.4)
```

for its reciprocal on the return step.  There is no net damping or gain.

The same conclusion holds for the positive complementary carriers from
R93: an off-line zero gives two forward correlations with complementary
tilts, and the smooth positive countermodels there show that the paired
functional equation has no generic sign.  The global alias derivation adds
no third independent coordinate.

## 8. Sharp nonvanishing barrier

The global alias calculation leaves the exact admission test

```text
D(s)!=0

for 1-eta<Re(s)<1 and abs(Im(s))>=t_0.               (8.1)
```

By (1.2), this is

```text
zeta(1-s)!=0                                         (8.2)
```

on the reflected strip `0<Re(1-s)<eta`.  By the functional equation, it is
equivalent to the original fixed zero-free strip.  More quantitatively, any
fixed-power lower bound

```text
abs(D(s))>=abs(t)^(-A)                               (8.3)
```

would immediately give a fixed-power lower bound for the Luroth correlation
after multiplication by the explicit factor (4.5).

The value of this barrier is structural rather than notational.  Sections
2--7 prove that every piece generated by a uniform all-branch Poisson or van
der Corput treatment has already been consumed:

```text
stationary Gaussian magnitude     -> P(s)chi(s),
stationary block phases           -> k^(s-1),
overlapping high-index continuum  -> -K^s/s,
finite signed remainder           -> zeta(1-s),
second renormalization            -> zeta(s).         (8.4)
```

There is no leftover error of fixed sign whose domination would imply
(8.1).  Proving (8.1) now requires a genuinely new global multiplicative
estimate for the finite part itself, not a sharper stationary-phase error.

## 9. Disposition

This report proves the following reusable results.

1. The finite Luroth head has the exact all-mode Poisson decomposition
   (2.6).
2. All stationary aliases sum, after their forced continuum subtraction,
   to the ordinary finite part `zeta(1-s)`.
3. The full Gaussian stationary coefficient matches `P(s)chi(s)` exactly
   through Stirling, including its phase.
4. The high-index continuum is a main term, not an error; removing it or
   taking absolute values costs `K^Re(s)`.
5. The dual finite part has an absolutely convergent Euler-defect carrier
   with one real sign, but every complex integer alias passes with unit
   response.
6. Repeating the global alias transform is an exact neutral two-cycle.

No fixed zero-free strip and no failure of every fixed strip is proved.  The
global Poisson/van der Corput route is complete and closed as an independent
cancellation mechanism.  Its sole remainder is the complementary zeta
value itself.
