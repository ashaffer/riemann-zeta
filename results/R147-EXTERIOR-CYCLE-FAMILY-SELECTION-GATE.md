# R147 exterior-cycle family-selection gate

## Status

R146 found an exact degree-zero Artin mask which looked substantially better
than the preceding bounded-support masks:

```text
p_m=lambda_(-1)(Std)=sum_(j=0)^(m-1)(-1)^j wedge^j Std.
```

This report completes its algebraic, local, conductor, family-counting, and
high-jet ledgers.  It also tests the best fixed-degree specialization, namely
`m=3`, against current unconditional cubic-field counting and Artin/Dirichlet
zero-density theorems.

The conclusions are sharp but negative for the proposed proof mechanism.

1. For every `m>=3`, `p_m` is exactly `m` on `m`-cycles and zero on every
   other conjugacy class.  It has trivial multiplicity one, nonnegative Euler
   logarithm coefficients, and deletes every unramified non-`m`-cycle local
   factor.
2. In a totally real `S_m` field of odd squarefree discriminant, every
   ramified inertia group is generated by a transposition.  The complete
   ramified local factor of `p_m` is then one.  Its signed finite conductor
   and signed archimedean degree are also one and zero, respectively.
3. The absolute hook ledger has degree `2^(m-1)` and discriminant exponent
   `2^(m-2)`.  For fixed `m`, the remote-divisor cost is therefore
   `O_m(log D_K+log(|t|+3))`; signed conductor cancellation does not cancel
   this absolute divisor count.
4. For `m=3`, the virtual Artin function is the exact quotient

   ```text
   F_K(s)=zeta(s)L(s,chi_D)/L(s,Std_K)
         =zeta(s)^2 L(s,chi_D)/zeta_K(s).
   ```

   Current theorems do allow the simultaneous selection of totally real
   squarefree-discriminant cubic fields which delete every inert prime up to
   `X` and for which the dangerous denominator is zero-free in a fixed
   near-one rectangle.  The stronger construction here also avoids zeros of
   the quadratic numerator, although R148 observes that this is unnecessary.
   The first rigorous scale supplied by those theorems is

   ```text
   log D_K=(A+o(1))X,             A>2.
   ```

5. The R138 high-jet comparison needs instead

   ```text
   log D_K=o(X^kappa),            kappa<eta<1/2,
   ```

   where `eta` is the auxiliary zero-free width.  At the width supplied by
   the almost-all Artin theorem one has, conservatively, `eta<1/720`.
   Thus the rigorous family selection misses the analytic budget by a full
   power of `X`, not by a constant.
6. Even an ideal product-density field count would normally require
   `log D_K` of order `X/log X` for fixed `m`.  This still cannot satisfy the
   high-jet budget.  This is a no-go for generic family selection, not an
   unconditional lower bound for every exceptional field.
7. Averaging the head-deleted masks does not turn the remote divisors into
   independent noise.  The average retains the common zeta-zero pole but,
   in `Re(s)>1`, becomes essentially a Chebotarev tail of `-zeta'/zeta`.
   Its high jets are tiny because its regular parts cancel the common pole
   coherently.  A naive square-root-of-family saving is therefore invalid.

## 1. Exact exterior-cycle identity

Let `V=Std` be the `(m-1)`-dimensional standard representation of `S_m`, and
put

```text
p_m=sum_(j=0)^(m-1)(-1)^j wedge^j V.                       (1.1)
```

Its character is

```text
p_m(g)=det(1-g|V).                                          (1.2)
```

If `g` has `c(g)` cycles, then eigenvalue one has multiplicity `c(g)-1` on
`V`.  Hence (1.2) vanishes unless `g` is an `m`-cycle.  For an `m`-cycle the
nontrivial eigenvalues are the nontrivial `m`th roots of unity, so

```text
det(1-g|V)=product_(a=1)^(m-1)(1-zeta_m^a)=m.               (1.3)
```

Therefore

```text
p_m(g)=m 1_(g is an m-cycle).                               (1.4)
```

Since there are `(m-1)!` such cycles,

```text
<p_m,1>=m(m-1)!/m!=1.                                      (1.5)
```

Thus the source zeta factor occurs with multiplicity exactly one.  Every
`wedge^j V` is the irreducible hook representation indexed by
`(m-j,1^j)`, so (1.1) is also the irreducible Artin factorization; there is
no hidden permutation-character enlargement in this ledger.

## 2. Exact unramified Euler support

Let `p` be unramified with natural Frobenius `g_p`.  The logarithm of the
local virtual Artin factor is

```text
log F_(K,p)(s)
 =sum_(v>=1) p_m(g_p^v)/(v p^(vs)).                         (2.1)
```

A power of a permutation cannot merge distinct cycles.  Consequently, if
`g_p` is not an `m`-cycle, then no `g_p^v` is an `m`-cycle, and

```text
F_(K,p)(s)=1.                                               (2.2)
```

If `g_p` is an `m`-cycle, then `g_p^v` is an `m`-cycle exactly when
`(v,m)=1`.  Hence

```text
log F_(K,p)(s)
 =sum_((v,m)=1) m/(v p^(vs)),                               (2.3)

F_(K,p)(s)
 =product_(d|m)(1-p^(-ds))^(-m mu(d)/d).                   (2.4)
```

All logarithmic Euler coefficients are therefore nonnegative and bounded
by `m`.  Deleting the `m`-cycle class at every prime `p<=X` deletes the
complete Euler factor, including all prime powers, at every such prime.

The local forbidden density is only

```text
alpha_m=#(m-cycles)/m!=1/m,                                 (2.5)
```

so this mask is arithmetically much cheaper than forcing bounded support.

## 3. Ramified local factors and the `m=2` exception

Assume `m>=3`, `D_K` is odd and squarefree, and the Galois closure `E` has
group `S_m`.  Every finite ramified inertia group in `E/Q` is then tame and
generated by a transposition `tau`.

Let `sigma` be a Frobenius lift.  The tame relation gives

```text
sigma tau sigma^(-1)=tau^p=tau,                             (3.1)
```

because the ramified rational prime is odd.  Thus `sigma` centralizes
`tau`, and both `sigma^v` and `sigma^v tau` preserve the two-point support of
`tau`.  For `m>=3` neither can be an `m`-cycle.  The inertia-averaged local
trace is consequently

```text
(1/2)[p_m(sigma^v)+p_m(sigma^v tau)]=0                     (3.2)
```

for every `v>=1`.  The complete ramified local factor is exactly one.

This statement fails for `m=2`: the transposition is itself a full cycle.
The signed conductor cancellation below fails there as well.  All
transposition-ramified deletion claims in this report require `m>=3`.

## 4. Exact signed conductor and absolute hook ledger

At a transposition, `V` has eigenvalues

```text
+1 with multiplicity m-2,          -1 with multiplicity 1. (4.1)
```

The tame Artin conductor exponent of `wedge^j V` is therefore

```text
a_j=binom(m-2,j-1),                 1<=j<=m-1.              (4.2)
```

Every ramified prime occurs to exponent one in `D_K`, so

```text
q(wedge^j V)=D_K^binom(m-2,j-1).                            (4.3)
```

The signed conductor exponent is

```text
sum_(j=1)^(m-1)(-1)^j binom(m-2,j-1)
 =-(1-1)^(m-2)=0.                                           (4.4)
```

Thus

```text
q(p_m)=1.                                                   (4.5)
```

Because `K` is totally real, its normal closure is totally real.  Every hook
is even at infinity, while

```text
sum_(j=0)^(m-1)(-1)^j dim(wedge^j V)
 =(1-1)^(m-1)=0.                                            (4.6)
```

The signed Gamma factor cancels as well.

The corresponding absolute quantities do not cancel:

```text
sum_j dim(wedge^j V)=2^(m-1),                               (4.7)

sum_(j=1)^(m-1) a_j=2^(m-2).                               (4.8)
```

For a fixed target height `gamma`, a standard completed-`L` divisor count
therefore has the size

```text
C_m(D_K,gamma)
 <<_m 2^m[1+log D_K+log(|gamma|+3)].                        (4.9)
```

For fixed `m`, this is `O_m(log D_K+log(|gamma|+3))`.  It is
the absolute remote-divisor ledger which survives after the exact signed
functional-equation constants have disappeared.

For reference, tame discriminant theory also gives

```text
D_E=D_K^(m!/2)                                               (4.10)
```

in this squarefree-discriminant setting: each ramified prime has inertia
order two and Galois discriminant exponent `m!(1-1/2)`.

## 5. The cubic specialization

For `m=3`,

```text
p_3=1-Std+sgn.                                               (5.1)
```

If `D=D_K>0` is odd and squarefree and `chi_D` is the quadratic-resolvent
character, then

```text
F_K(s)=L(s,p_3)
      =zeta(s)L(s,chi_D)/L(s,Std_K)                          (5.2)
      =zeta(s)^2 L(s,chi_D)/zeta_K(s).                       (5.3)
```

Here

```text
L(s,Std_K)=zeta_K(s)/zeta(s).                               (5.4)
```

The standard factor has conductor `D`, the sign factor has conductor `D`,
and all three representations are even.  Equations (4.4)--(4.6) are thus
visible directly in (5.2).

At an inert prime,

```text
F_(K,p)(s)=(1-p^(-3s))/(1-p^(-s))^3,                        (5.5)
```

and the logarithmic coefficients are three for powers `p^v` with `3` not
dividing `v`, and zero otherwise.  Split and partially split unramified
primes, and transposition-ramified primes, have factor one.

The standard representation is monomial, so its Artin `L`-function is
entire unconditionally.  A terminology warning matters here: for a totally
real cubic field this two-dimensional representation is **even**.  It is
not the odd holomorphic weight-one representation attached to a
negative-discriminant cubic field.  None of the selection argument below
uses a weight convention; it uses the exact Artin quotient (5.4).

## 6. Exact LOTZ region and why it covers the cubic standard factor

Let `F` be a degree-`n` `S_n` field, ordered by `D_F<=Q`, and let
`\widetilde F` be its normal closure.  Corollary 3.8 of Lemke Oliver,
Thorner, and Zaman states that, for every `epsilon_0>0`, all but
`O_(n,epsilon_0)(Q^epsilon_0)` such fields have

```text
L(s,rho_F)=zeta_F(s)/zeta(s)                                (6.1)
```

holomorphic and nonzero in

```text
Omega_(\widetilde F)(epsilon_0/n!).                         (6.2)
```

For a Galois field `E/Q` of group `G`, the low-height branch of their exact
region `Omega_E(u)` is

```text
1-Re(s)
 <=[u/(10|G|)]
   [log D_E/(log D_E+log(3+|Im(s)|))],                      (6.3)
```

valid when

```text
|Im(s)|<=exp(D_E^(u/(6C_G))).                               (6.4)
```

Above that height their region reverts to a classical discriminant-height
zero-free region.  Only (6.3) is needed here.

For `n=3`, `G=S_3`, and `u=epsilon_0/6`, the asymptotic fixed-height width in
(6.3) is

```text
epsilon_0/360.                                               (6.5)
```

Consequently, for every fixed `T_0` and every

```text
0<eta<epsilon_0/720,                                        (6.6)
```

the rectangle

```text
Re(s)>=1-eta,                 |Im(s)|<=T_0                  (6.7)
```

lies inside (6.2) once `D_F` is sufficiently large.

There is no conductor-order mismatch.  LOTZ order this corollary by the
degree-three field discriminant `D_F`, while the region is expressed using
the closure discriminant.  In the present squarefree cubic family,

```text
D_(\widetilde F)=D_F^3.                                     (6.8)
```

Most importantly, their representation `rho_F` is exactly the faithful
two-dimensional standard representation in (5.4).  Thus the theorem applies
directly, irrespective of whether one describes that representation through
Artin induction, a Hecke character, or automorphic language.

## 7. Cubic head count with current local specifications

Use totally real cubic fields of positive odd fundamental discriminant.
At every prime outside the head, impose the ordinary condition `A'_p` of
Bhargava--Taniguchi--Thorne: the local cubic algebra is not totally ramified.
At an odd prime `p<=X`, also exclude the inert splitting type `(3)`.

From the local-density table in their Theorem 1.3, the allowed fraction at
such a prime, relative to the fundamental-discriminant family, is

```text
u_(3,p)
 =[(2/3)p^2+p]/(p^2+p)
 =(2p+3)/(3(p+1)).                                          (7.1)
```

Therefore

```text
U_3(X)=product_(p<=X)u_(3,p)
 =exp[-log(3/2)pi(X)+O(log log X)].                          (7.2)
```

Each nonordinary head specification is a union of splitting types, so the
same theorem gives local error factor

```text
E_p=p^(2/3),

product_(p<=X)E_p
 =exp[(2/3)theta(X)].                                       (7.3)
```

For a dyadic discriminant interval `Y/2<D_F<=Y`, the main term, secondary
term, and error consequently have the scales

```text
Y U_3(X),

Y^(5/6) exp[O(X/log X)],

Y^(2/3+epsilon) exp[(2/3)X+o(X)],                           (7.4)
```

respectively.  Put

```text
Y=exp(AX).                                                   (7.5)
```

For every fixed `A>2`, one may choose the theorem's `epsilon>0` so that

```text
(1/3-epsilon)A>2/3.                                         (7.6)
```

The main term then dominates both the secondary term and the error.  Hence
there are

```text
Y^(1-o(1))                                                   (7.7)
```

totally real odd-fundamental-discriminant `S_3` fields in the dyadic
interval with no inert prime `p<=X`.

This is a rigorous existence theorem.  It is also an expensive theorem:
the explicit local-specification error forces `log Y>(2+o(1))X`.  The
product-density main term alone would predict the cheaper but still large
scale `log Y` of order `X/log X`.

## 8. Simultaneous auxiliary zero avoidance at that scale

Fix `T_0`, choose `0<epsilon_0<1`, and let `eta` satisfy (6.6).  The head
family in (7.7) has size `Y^(1-o(1))`.

LOTZ remove at most

```text
O(Y^epsilon_0)                                               (8.1)
```

fields for which `L(s,Std_K)` is not holomorphic and nonzero in (6.7).
This is negligible relative to (7.7).

For the sign factor, Jutila's Dirichlet zero-density estimate gives, for
`alpha>=4/5`,

```text
sum_(q<=Y) sum_(chi mod q)^* N(alpha,T_0,chi)
 <<_(epsilon,T_0) Y^(4(1-alpha)+epsilon).                   (8.2)
```

Taking `alpha=1-eta`, the number of bad primitive quadratic characters is
at most

```text
Y^(4eta+epsilon).                                           (8.3)
```

For a positive fundamental discriminant `D`, cubic fields of discriminant
`D` correspond to index-three subgroups of the quadratic class group.  In
particular, their number is at most the quadratic class number.  The crude
but rigorous bound

```text
h(D)<<sqrt(D) log D                                         (8.4)
```

therefore bounds the number of cubic fields lying above all bad quadratic
characters by

```text
Y^(1/2+4eta+epsilon+o(1)).                                  (8.5)
```

Choose `epsilon` so that the exponent in (8.5) is less than one.  This is
automatic for the tiny `eta` in (6.6).  Subtracting (8.1) and (8.5) from
(7.7) proves the following.

### Cubic local-selection and zero-avoidance theorem

Fix `A>2`, `T_0>=1`, and `0<epsilon_0<1`.  If

```text
0<eta<epsilon_0/720,                                        (8.6)
```

then, for every sufficiently large `X`, there is a totally real `S_3`
cubic field `K` with odd fundamental discriminant satisfying

```text
(1/2)exp(AX)<D_K<=exp(AX),                                  (8.7)
```

such that

1. no prime `p<=X` is inert in `K`;
2. `L(s,Std_K)` is holomorphic and nonzero in (6.7); and
3. `L(s,chi_(D_K))` is nonzero in (6.7).

The two zero-density inputs and the class-number fiber bound are used only
to select a member of the already-counted head family.  Thus zero avoidance
itself is not the obstruction in fixed degree three.

## 9. Conditional high-jet closure theorem and its budget

This section isolates exactly what would be needed to convert the mask into
a zeta zero-free strip.

Suppose that

```text
rho=1-delta+i gamma                                         (9.1)
```

is a zeta zero with `0<delta<eta`.  Choose

```text
z_*=1+r+i gamma,
d=|z_*-rho|=r+delta,
d<R<r+eta.                                                  (9.2)
```

Let `lambda>1` satisfy

```text
I(lambda)=lambda-1-log lambda>log(d/r),                     (9.3)
```

and select derivative orders `k` by the usual finite Turan power-sum
lemma.  Set

```text
X_k=exp(lambda k/r).                                        (9.4)
```

Assume there are totally real odd-squarefree-discriminant `S_m` fields
`K_k`, with fixed `m>=3`, such that

1. no unramified prime `p<=X_k` has `m`-cycle Frobenius;
2. every nontrivial hook factor `L(s,wedge^j Std_(K_k))` is holomorphic and
   nonzero in the closed disk `|s-z_*|<=R`; and
3.

   ```text
   2^m[1+log D_(K_k)+log(|gamma|+3)]
      =o((R/d)^k).                                          (9.5)
   ```

Then `rho` cannot exist.

Indeed, for the normalized high jet of `-F'_K/F_K`, complete head deletion
and positivity of the Euler logarithm give

```text
|J_k(z_*)|
 <=m r^(-k+O(1)) exp[-k I(lambda)+o(k)].                    (9.6)
```

The local zero avoidance and Turan selection give a target-divisor lower
bound of size

```text
c d^(-k-1),                                                 (9.7)
```

while all divisors outside the radius `R` contribute at most

```text
O(2^m[1+log D_K+log(|gamma|+3)]R^(-k-1)).                   (9.8)
```

Condition (9.3) makes (9.6) smaller than (9.7), and (9.5) makes (9.8)
smaller than (9.7), a contradiction.

In terms of `X=X_k`, condition (9.5) is

```text
2^m[1+log D_K+log(|gamma|+3)]=o(X^kappa),                   (9.9)

kappa=(r/lambda)log(R/d).                                   (9.10)
```

The geometry in (9.2) gives the strict inequality

```text
kappa<eta-delta<eta.                                        (9.11)
```

Even an ideal auxiliary GRH-width rectangle would have `eta<=1/2` and
therefore `kappa<1/2`.  With the unconditional LOTZ selection above, one
may take only a much smaller fixed `eta` while keeping the exceptional set
smaller than the cubic family.

## 10. The strict scale contradiction

The theorem in Section 8 supplies

```text
log D_K=(A+o(1))X,             A>2.                         (10.1)
```

The analytic closure in Section 9 requires, already for `m=3`,

```text
log D_K=o(X^kappa),            kappa<eta<1/2.               (10.2)
```

Equations (10.1)--(10.2) are incompatible by a full power of `X`.
Accordingly:

```text
current cubic count + current zero density + R138 high jet = NO CLOSURE.
                                                                    (10.3)
```

This is not merely an artifact of the explicit `A>2` error threshold.  For
fixed `m`, an ideal product-density model in a field family of polynomial
size `D^a` leaves the fraction

```text
exp[-c_m pi(X)+O_m(log log X)],
c_m=-log(1-1/m).                                            (10.4)
```

To retain even one typical field, that model needs

```text
log D >=(c_m/a+o(1))X/log X.                                (10.5)
```

The right side of (10.5) is still larger than `X^kappa` for every
`kappa<1`.  Hence perfect error terms in a conventional first-moment family
count would not bridge (10.2).

Letting `m` grow does not balance the exponents.  The local entropy improves
only like `1/m`, while the absolute hook ledger grows like `2^m`.  If the
hook ledger is to remain below `X^kappa`, then `m=O(log X)`, and the modeled
field entropy remains `X/polylog(X)`, again larger than `X^kappa`.

These are family-selection no-go statements.  They do **not** prove a
deterministic lower bound of the form (10.5) for every individual field.
An exceptionally correlated sequence with

```text
log D_K=o(X^kappa)                                          (10.6)
```

is not ruled out unconditionally by current least-prime theorems.

As a reality check, a GRH-quality effective Chebotarev theorem for the
nontrivial Artin factors, together with the ordinary prime number theorem
for the trivial factor, predicts an `m`-cycle prime at

```text
p<<_m (log D_E)^2 polylog(log D_E).                         (10.7)
```

Thus head deletion through `X` would force

```text
log D_K>>_m X^(1/2)/polylog(X),                             (10.8)
```

already incompatible with the strict `kappa<1/2` budget.  This is a
conditional diagnostic, not an unconditional exclusion of (10.6).

## 11. Why ensemble averaging does not remove the remote ledger

There is a tempting final shortcut: average many head-deleted fields and
treat their auxiliary divisors as independent phases.  The exact
Chebotarev average shows why that inference is unsafe.

For uniform `g` in `S_m`, (1.4) gives

```text
E_g p_m(g^v)
 =1,                  (v,m)=1,
 =0,                  (v,m)>1.                             (11.1)
```

Indeed, only an `m`-cycle can have an `m`-cycle power, and it does so
precisely for `(v,m)=1`.  Under ideal independence away from the imposed
head, the family average in `Re(s)>1` is therefore

```text
sum_(p>X) sum_((v,m)=1) log p/p^(vs).                       (11.2)
```

Up to the predictable prime-power correction with `(v,m)>1`, which is
analytic in a neighborhood of `Re(s)=1`, this is the head-deleted tail of
`-zeta'/zeta`.  It is the Euler-prime analogue of

```text
A_X(s)=-zeta'(s)/zeta(s)-sum_(n<=X)Lambda(n)n^(-s).          (11.3)
```

Subtracting the finite head in (11.3) does not remove any zeta-zero pole.
Nevertheless, at `Re(s)>1`, the matched high derivatives of `A_X` are tiny
because only the tail remains.  The regular part cancels the nearby pole
contribution coherently.

Thus an average can simultaneously

1. retain the same source pole at every zeta zero; and
2. have arbitrarily small high jets to the right of one.

This is an explicit countermodel to a naive `1/sqrt(#family)` treatment of
the remote divisor sum.  Those divisors are constrained by the common Euler
tail and need not behave like independent random phases.  A valid averaging
escape would require a **centered signed family theorem** which controls the
joint regular parts after subtracting (11.2), at the derivative order and
discriminant scale in (9.4)--(9.9).  No current Artin or Dedekind zero-density
theorem gives that statement.

## 12. What this branch proves and what remains open

The exterior-cycle construction is algebraically genuine.  It solves all
of the following exact design problems at once:

- degree-zero positive Euler logarithm;
- source zeta multiplicity one;
- complete unramified head deletion on a density `1-1/m` set;
- complete transposition-ramified deletion for `m>=3`;
- signed finite-conductor cancellation; and
- signed archimedean cancellation.

The fixed cubic specialization also shows rigorously that auxiliary
zero-avoidance can be combined with the local head conditions.  What fails
is the quantitative scale needed to prevent the remote regular part from
cancelling the source pole.

The strict conclusion is therefore:

```text
The exterior-cycle/family-selection route does not prove a fixed zeta
zero-free strip with current literature, even after successful auxiliary
zero avoidance.  It also does not prove that such a strip cannot exist.
It kills the generic analytic closure, not exceptional fields below the
family-entropy scale.                                               (12.1)
```

There are only two honest continuations of this branch:

1. prove an exceptional-field theorem producing (10.6) together with the
   local zero-avoidance in Section 9; or
2. replace the absolute remote-divisor ledger by a centered signed joint
   theorem strong enough to exploit cancellation after the deterministic
   Chebotarev tail (11.2) has been removed.

Either result would be substantially beyond the currently imported
number-field counting and zero-density literature.  Without one of them,
the R138 high-jet contradiction cannot be closed.
