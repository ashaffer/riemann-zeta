# Triangular-packet cone no-go

## Status and verdict

This note gives an exact fail-fast answer to the proposed Selberg-packet
lifting step.  Positivity on every modulated interval, even after allowing
every interval length and position inside a fixed window, does **not** imply
positivity of the compressed Hermitian convolution operator.  Equivalently,
the positive cone generated by modulated triangular autocorrelations is a
proper subcone of the full Paley--Wiener autocorrelation cone.

The obstruction already occurs for a `3 x 3` real symmetric Toeplitz matrix,
and dimension three is minimal.  It also has both a continuous prime-atom
model and an entire rank-three convolution-kernel model on an interval.  The
latter shows that the failure is not caused by distributional kernels.  Thus
a lift of the triangular Selberg test to the full Weil form cannot be a
purely convex-density or Toeplitz lemma; it must use extra zeta-specific
relations or a richer family containing coherent superpositions of separated
packets.

## 1. Minimal finite-dimensional countermodel

Let

```text
        [ 1    0   5/4 ]
T =     [ 0    1    0  ].
        [ 5/4  0    1  ]
```

This is a real symmetric Toeplitz matrix.  For every consecutive modulated
box in `C^3`, its quadratic form is positive:

```text
width 1:                         1,
width 2:                         2,
width 3, v_theta=(1,e^{i theta},e^{2i theta}):
        v_theta^* T v_theta = 3+(5/2)cos(2 theta) >= 1/2.
```

Translation of a width-one or width-two box does not change these values.
Nevertheless

```text
w=(1,0,-1),       w^* T w = -1/2.
```

Indeed, the eigenvalues of `T` are `9/4`, `1`, and `-1/4`.

This is minimal.  In dimension one the claim is immediate.  A Hermitian
Toeplitz matrix in dimension two has the form

```text
[ t0       conjugate(t1) ]
[ t1       t0            ].
```

Positivity on `(1,e^{i theta})` for every `theta` says
`2t0-2|t1|>=0`; the width-one test says `t0>=0`.  These are exactly the two
eigenvalue inequalities `t0 +/- |t1|>=0`.

More generally, in every dimension `N>=3`, take `T_N` with diagonal one,
corner diagonals `t_(N-1)=t_(-(N-1))=c`, and all other diagonals zero.  Every
proper consecutive box has value equal to its width, while a full-width box
has value

```text
N+2c cos((N-1)theta).
```

Hence all boxes are nonnegative when `c<=N/2`, whereas `T_N` has eigenvalue
`1-c`.  Every `1<c<=N/2` is a counterexample.

## 2. Continuous Hermitian convolution countermodel

The same mechanism survives without discretization.  Let `0<d<L` with

```text
L/2 < d < L,
```

and choose

```text
1 < c <= L/(2(L-d)).
```

On `L^2(0,L)`, use the even real convolution distribution

```text
k = delta_0 + c(delta_d+delta_(-d))
```

and the compressed quadratic form

```text
Q(f) = integral_0^L |f(x)|^2 dx
       +2c Re integral_0^(L-d) f(x+d) conjugate(f(x)) dx.       (2.1)
```

For any interval `I subset (0,L)` of length `ell` and any real modulation
`tau`, put `f_(I,tau)(x)=1_I(x)e^{i tau x}`.  Directly from (2.1),

```text
Q(f_(I,tau))
  = ell+2c(ell-d)_+ cos(tau d).                                (2.2)
```

The result is independent of the position of `I`.  If `ell<=d`, it equals
`ell`.  If `d<ell<=L`, then

```text
Q(f_(I,tau)) >= ell-2c(ell-d) >= 0,                            (2.3)
```

because `ell/(2(ell-d))` decreases with `ell` and its minimum on this range
is `L/(2(L-d))`.  Thus **every width, position, and modulation passes**.
If the upper inequality for `c` is strict, the stronger uniform estimate is

```text
Q(f_(I,tau)) >= mu ||f_(I,tau)||_2^2,
mu = 1-2c(L-d)/L > 0.                                         (2.4)
```

The operator is nonetheless indefinite.  For nonzero `g in L^2(0,L-d)`,
define

```text
f(x) =  g(x)       for 0<x<L-d,
       -g(x-d)     for d<x<L,
        0           otherwise.                                (2.5)
```

The two occupied intervals are disjoint because `d>L/2`.  Consequently

```text
||f||_2^2 = 2||g||_2^2,
integral_0^(L-d) f(x+d)conjugate(f(x)) dx = -||g||_2^2,
Q(f) = 2(1-c)||g||_2^2 < 0.                                   (2.6)
```

In fact the nontrivial part of the operator is a direct integral of the
matrix `[[1,c],[c,1]]`, so its exact lower spectral edge is `1-c`.
Replacing the two shifted coefficients `c` by `-c` leaves every packet bound
unchanged (the carrier phase absorbs the sign); the negative direction then
uses equal rather than opposite signs on the two blocks.  Thus the example
also has exactly the negative shifted-atom sign of the von Mangoldt comb.

For the especially simple rational choice

```text
L=3/2,   d=1,   c=5/4,
```

equation (2.2) becomes

```text
Q(f_(I,tau)) = ell+(5/2)(ell-1)_+ cos(tau),
```

and every nonempty interval has positive value.  More strongly, its
normalized Rayleigh quotient is at least `1/6` uniformly in `ell` and `tau`,
while (2.6) gives Rayleigh quotient `-1/4` on every antisymmetric two-block
vector.

## 3. Entire finite-rank convolution countermodel

There is also a counterexample with a real-even entire kernel.  This rules
out the possibility that the shifted delta masses in Section 2 are
responsible for the failure.

Put `D=(-L/2,L/2)`, let

```text
eta = pi/(2L),              k_ent(u) = 2 cos(eta u)-1/4,
```

and let `Q_ent` be the compressed convolution form with kernel `k_ent`.
Writing the form as three rank-one terms gives

```text
Q_ent(f)
 = |integral_D f(x)e^(-i eta x) dx|^2
  +|integral_D f(x)e^( i eta x) dx|^2
  -(1/4)|integral_D f(x) dx|^2.                              (3.1)
```

It is therefore bounded and self-adjoint, with rank three.  If `I subset D`
has length `ell`, and

```text
f_(I,tau)(x)=ell^(-1/2)e^(i tau x)1_I(x),
sinc y = sin(y)/y,        sinc 0 = 1,
```

then translation of `I` changes only irrelevant phases in (3.1), and

```text
Q_ent(f_(I,tau)) / ell
 = sinc^2(x-alpha)+sinc^2(x+alpha)-(1/4)sinc^2(x),            (3.2)

x = tau ell/2,             alpha = eta ell/2 in (0,pi/4].
```

The following elementary inequality proves that (3.2) is nonnegative:

```text
sinc^2(x-alpha)+sinc^2(x+alpha) >= (1/4)sinc^2(x)
                       whenever |alpha|<=pi/4.               (3.3)
```

Here is a proof retaining explicit constants.  If `|x|<=pi/4`, then
`|x +/- alpha|<=pi/2`; hence each term on the left is at least
`4/pi^2`, while the right is at most `1/4`.  If `|x|>=pi/4`, then
`|alpha|<=|x|`, so, by continuity at a removable zero if necessary,

```text
sinc^2(x-alpha)+sinc^2(x+alpha)
 >= [sin^2(x-alpha)+sin^2(x+alpha)]/(|x|+|alpha|)^2
 = 2[sin^2(x)cos^2(alpha)+cos^2(x)sin^2(alpha)]
       /(|x|+|alpha|)^2
 >= (1/4)sinc^2(x).
```

The last line uses `cos^2(alpha)>=1/2` and
`|x|+|alpha|<=2|x|`.  This proves packet positivity simultaneously for
every interval length, position, and carrier frequency.

Nevertheless `Q_ent` is indefinite.  Let

```text
V = span{e^(i eta x), e^(-i eta x)} subset L^2(D),
h = projection_(V perp)(1).
```

The three distinct exponentials are linearly independent on `D`, so `h` is
nonzero.  It is orthogonal to the two positive modes, and
`<h,1>=||h||_2^2`.  Consequently

```text
Q_ent(h) = -(1/4)||h||_2^4 < 0.                              (3.4)
```

If desired, the witness can be written without projection notation:

```text
h(x)=1-[4 sqrt(2)/(pi+2)]cos(eta x).
```

Indeed, the constant function is orthogonal to `sin(eta x)`, and the stated
coefficient is `integral_D cos(eta x) dx / integral_D cos^2(eta x) dx`.
This is an analytic, finite-rank counterexample to the proposed implication.

## 4. Fixed full interval versus all subintervals

For a translation-invariant Hermitian kernel `k`, testing only the full
interval of length `L` asks for

```text
Fourier[(L-|u|)_+ k(u)](tau) >= 0        for every tau.       (4.1)
```

Allowing every subinterval asks for the strictly larger family of
conditions

```text
Fourier[(ell-|u|)_+ k(u)](tau) >= 0
                      for every 0<ell<=L and every tau.       (4.2)
```

Interval position adds no condition because both the kernel and the packet
autocorrelation are translation invariant.  In the entire countermodel,
(4.1) is the endpoint `alpha=pi/4` of (3.3), whereas (4.2) is the whole
range `0<alpha<=pi/4`.  Thus the proof does distinguish the two tests and
shows that even the stronger one fails.

The full-window exponentials are themselves a total family in `L^2(D)`.
That does not help: knowing a quadratic form on each member of a total set
does not control the cross terms in coherent linear combinations.  Adding
all shorter boxes still does not supply those cross terms, as (3.4) shows.

## 5. Autocorrelation-cone interpretation

The autocorrelation of a modulated interval is

```text
c_(ell,tau)(u) = e^{-i tau u}(ell-|u|)_+.
```

Equations (2.2)--(2.4) say that `k` pairs nonnegatively with every such
triangle, with a uniform normalized margin in the strict case.  It therefore
pairs nonnegatively with their entire positive cone and with its normalized
closure.  Equation (2.6) supplies a compactly supported
`f` whose autocorrelation pairs negatively with `k`.  By separation, that
autocorrelation is outside the closed triangular-packet cone.

The geometric reason is visible in (2.5).  A single interval that correlates
points distance `d` apart must pay positive diagonal mass throughout the gap
between them.  A coherent superposition of two separated blocks can occupy
only the correlated endpoints, choose their relative phase to be negative,
and avoid the gap.  Varying the interval's width, position, or carrier
frequency never supplies that missing interference degree of freedom.

## 6. Consequence for the signed-prime checkpoint

Selberg's triangular zero kernel remains a useful necessary scalar test, but
there is no general implication

```text
nonnegative on all Selberg triangles
    => nonnegative on all PW_(U/2) autocorrelations.
```

The countermodel uses the same kind of shifted delta atoms that occur in the
prime part of the Weil distribution, so excluding it by a generic
regularity argument is unavailable.  A viable continuation must add at
least one genuinely new ingredient, for example:

1. signed control for cross terms between separated interval packets;
2. a polarization identity backed by estimates on their coherent sums; or
3. an arithmetic relation tying the many prime shifts together in a way the
single-shift countermodel cannot satisfy.

Testing more individual triangles, even at a continuum of scales, does not
close the endpoint inequality on a fixed support window.
