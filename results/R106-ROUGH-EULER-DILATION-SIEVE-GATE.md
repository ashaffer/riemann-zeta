complete-period sieve ledger, full window-span ledger, and the
moving-order/Euler-transfer incompatibility are proved.  The operator is a
genuine rough sieve, but it supplies no fixed-power surplus on the scale on
which it remains a faithful completed detector.  No fixed zero-free strip is

## 1. Verdict

Put

```text
(tau_a F)(R)=F(R+a),
B_p=I-p^(-1/2)tau_(-log p),
B_y=product_(p<=y)B_p,
P_y=product_(p<=y)p.                                  (1.1)
```

This is not the R105 Ramanujan operator.  It has the exact expansion

```text
B_y=sum_(q|P_y)mu(q)q^(-1/2)tau_(-log q),             (1.2)
```

and on a free Vaughan cofactor it is the literal rough-number sieve

```text
B_y sum_(m>=1)f_R(mdb)
 =sum_((m,P_y)=1)f_R(mdb).                            (1.3)
```

On a zero carrier it has the finite reciprocal-Euler multiplier

```text
B_y exp[(rho-1/2)R]
 =E_y(rho)exp[(rho-1/2)R],
E_y(s)=product_(p<=y)(1-p^(-s)).                      (1.4)
```

Thus the idea has real content.  It also has an exact critical cost which
closes it as an automatic fixed-power mechanism.

1. The complete-period energy density in (1.3) is
   `phi(P_y)/P_y asymp 1/log y`, only logarithmic.
2. The translated window in (1.2) has full backward span
   `log P_y=vartheta(y) asymp y`.  A completed zero detector at logarithmic
   scale `R` therefore requires `vartheta(y)+O(hk)<R`.
3. On the regular Vaughan schedule, the whole permitted free cofactor has
   logarithmic size `A R/k+O(k)` for a fixed `A>0`.  Sieving that interval
   down to `m=1` requires

```text
y>=exp[A R/k+O(k)].                                   (1.5)
```

   Combining (1.5) with `vartheta(y)=O(R)` forces
   `k >= (A+o(1))R/log R`.  The proved moving-order/Euler range instead has
   `k^2=O(R)`.  These inequalities are incompatible.
4. Transporting the recorded order-`k` periodic-Euler error through (1.2)
   is stricter still.  Backward translation by `log q` amplifies its
   `x^(-k-1/2)` remainder by `q^k`, so the available absolute transfer costs

```text
sum_(q|P_y)q^k=product_(p<=y)(1+p^k)
              =exp[k vartheta(y)+O(pi(y))].           (1.6)
```

   Retaining a power-small center-evaluation error therefore requires
   `k vartheta(y)+O(k^2)<aR`, hence `y=O(R/k)`.  At that
   scale (1.3) again has only a logarithmic sieve density.
5. If the long filter is applied only to the balanced tail, its exact
   cofactor action remains true but its reciprocal-Euler multiplier does
   not describe a completed detector.  Applying it to the exact completed
   field gives coefficient `mu_y*Lambda` and Dirichlet series

```text
E_y(s)[-zeta'(s)/zeta(s)].                             (1.7)
```

   Since `E_y(rho)!=0` at every nontrivial zero, the full zero pole is still
   present.  The omitted Vaughan heads/boundary shells carry precisely the
   completion that a tail-only rough sieve drops.

The resulting no-go is scale-theoretic, not a claim that the signed
`mu(d)Lambda(b)` sum cannot have further cancellation.  Such a cancellation
estimate would be new arithmetic input.  It is not generated by `B_y`, by
`Q_h`, or by the existing periodic-Euler lemma.

## 2. Exact action on an arbitrary arithmetic field

For a finitely supported coefficient sequence `a`, define

```text
D_(a,V)(R)=sum_(n>=1)a(n)n^(-1/2)V(R-log n).           (2.1)
```

Directly,

```text
p^(-1/2)tau_(-log p)D_(a,V)(R)
 =sum_(p|n)a(n/p)n^(-1/2)V(R-log n).                  (2.2)
```

Consequently, if `epsilon` is the convolution identity and `delta_p` is the
point mass at `p`,

```text
B_pD_(a,V)=D_((epsilon-delta_p)*a,V),                  (2.3)
```

or, coefficientwise,

```text
a_p(n)=a(n)-1_(p|n)a(n/p).                            (2.4)
```

Let

```text
mu_y(q)=mu(q)1_(q|P_y).                               (2.5)
```

The factors commute, so iteration of (2.4) proves

```text
B_yD_(a,V)=D_(mu_y*a,V),
(mu_y*a)(n)=sum_(q|n,q|P_y)mu(q)a(n/q).               (2.6)
```

Equation (1.2) follows at the same time.  All identities are finite; no
Euler-product convergence is being assumed.

For the transformed completed prime field of R102 the continuum moment is
already zero, so `a=Lambda`.  Hence

```text
B_yD_(V_Q)(R)
 =sum_n (mu_y*Lambda)(n)n^(-1/2)V_Q(R-log n).         (2.7)
```

If `y` exceeds every active `n`, then `mu_y(q)=mu(q)` for every divisor in
(2.6), and the convolution identity gives

```text
(mu_y*Lambda)(n)=(mu*Lambda)(n)=-mu(n)log n.          (2.8)
```

Thus the endpoint of the full sieve is a smoothed `mu(n)log n` field, not a
small or positive field.  A power estimate for it is inverse-zeta
information, not a free consequence of inclusion-exclusion.

## 3. Exact rough sieve on the Vaughan cofactor

Use the R104/R105 normalization

```text
f_R(t)=t^(-1/2)V_Q(R-log t),
F_(d,b)(R)=sum_(m>=1)f_R(mdb).                         (3.1)
```

Since

```text
f_(R-log p)(t)=sqrt(p)f_R(pt),                        (3.2)
```

we have

```text
p^(-1/2)tau_(-log p)F_(d,b)(R)
 =sum_(p|m)f_R(mdb).                                  (3.3)
```

Subtracting (3.3) from (3.1), and then iterating over the distinct primes,
proves (1.3).  In particular, if the active cofactor range is `m<=M`, then

```text
y>=M  implies
B_yF_(d,b)(R)=f_R(db).                                (3.4)
```

Conversely, to make (3.4) an identity for every `1<=m<=M`, every prime
`p<=M` must occur in the filter, because the test cofactor `m=p` otherwise
survives.  For the initial-prime filter (1.1), `y>=M` is therefore both
necessary and sufficient for exact collapse of the whole interval.

This cofactor `m` must not be confused with the reduced-denominator variable
called `m` in R104 Section 7.  R104's polylogarithmic `m=1` face is a
projective rational face.  Setting the free Vaughan cofactor in (3.1) equal
to one still leaves the hard balanced `d,b` top box found in R105; it does
not by itself invoke R104's reducible-face estimate.

## 4. Sieve norm and elementary critical bound

On one complete residue period,

```text
(1/P_y)sum_(m mod P_y)1_((m,P_y)=1)^2
 =phi(P_y)/P_y
 =product_(p<=y)(1-1/p)
 asymp exp(-gamma)/log y.                             (4.1)
```

This is simultaneously the mean and the mean square because the rough
weight is an indicator.  It is a logarithmic energy gain.  There is no
coefficient-uniform contraction: a sequence supported on the reduced
residue classes is unchanged by (1.3).

The same endpoint appears directly on a critically bounded completed
field.  Suppose, on all translated scales which occur, that

```text
abs(F(S))<=C exp(S/2)A(S).                             (4.2)
```

Then (1.2) gives

```text
abs(B_yF(R))
 <=C exp(R/2) max_(q|P_y)A(R-log q)
   sum_(q|P_y)1/q
 =C exp(R/2)A_* product_(p<=y)(1+1/p)
 <<C exp(R/2)A_* log(2y).                             (4.3)
```

Thus the operator has only a logarithmic loss at the elementary critical
exponent.  Formula (4.3) is useful, but it is not an exponent improvement.

## 5. Spectral ledger

On an exponential `exp(lambda R)`,

```text
B_p exp(lambda R)
 =[1-p^(-lambda-1/2)]exp(lambda R).                   (5.1)
```

Putting `lambda=rho-1/2` proves (1.4).  For every finite `y` and every
`Re(rho)>0`, each factor is nonzero because `abs(p^(-rho))<1`.

For fixed `rho=beta+i gamma`, `1/2<beta<1`, the prime number theorem and
partial summation give

```text
sum_(p<=y)p^(-rho)
 =y^(1-rho)/[(1-rho)log y]
  +O_rho(y^(1-beta)/log^2 y).                         (5.2)
```

Since `sum_p p^(-2beta)<infinity`, expansion of the finite logarithm yields

```text
log abs(E_y(rho))
 =-Re{y^(1-rho)/[(1-rho)log y]}
  +O_rho(y^(1-beta)/log^2 y+1).                       (5.3)
```

In the completed safe range `y=O(R)`, this is `o(R)`.  Thus `B_y` neither
deletes nor changes the horizontal exponential order of a fixed off-axis
zero there.

On the real Fourier axis its multiplier satisfies

```text
product_(p<=y)(1-p^(-1/2))
 <=abs product_(p<=y)(1-p^(-1/2-it))
 <=product_(p<=y)(1+p^(-1/2)),                        (5.4)
```

The lower endpoint is attained simultaneously by every factor at `t=0`.
Consequently the inverse `L^2(dR)` multiplier norm is exactly

```text
norm(B_y^(-1))_(2->2)
 =product_(p<=y)(1-p^(-1/2))^(-1)
 =exp[(2+o(1))sqrt(y)/log y].                         (5.5)
```

The corresponding upper norm is at most

```text
norm(B_y)_(2->2)<=exp[O(sqrt(y)/log(2y))].            (5.6)
```

For `y=O(R)`, this conditioning is again `exp[o(R)]`.

For a fixed off-axis `beta>1/2`, the possible size in (5.3) is only
`exp[O_rho(y^(1-beta)/log y)]`, whose logarithm is
`o(sqrt(y)/log y)`.  Thus even outside the safe support range, phase-tuning
the finite Euler product cannot beat the coefficient-independent real-axis
conditioning cost of the same operator.  Any R71 energy argument which
inverts or normalizes the filter pays more than the target zero multiplier
can gain.

The center annihilator commutes with every `B_p`.  Its fixed zero multiplier

```text
q_h(rho)
 =[exp(h(rho-1/2))-exp(h/2)]^2                        (5.7)
```

is nonzero at every nontrivial zeta zero.  Therefore the combined
`Q_hB_y` multiplier is `q_h(rho)E_y(rho)` and has exactly the same safe-range
faithfulness.

## 6. The full shift span cannot be replaced by its average

If `V` has compact support `[a,b]`, then (1.2) replaces it by

```text
V_(B,y)(x)
 =sum_(q|P_y)mu(q)q^(-1/2)V(x-log q).                 (6.1)
```

Its support reaches from `a` to

```text
b+log P_y=b+vartheta(y),
vartheta(y)=sum_(p<=y)log p.                           (6.2)
```

The coefficient of the last translate is nonzero, so (6.2) is the actual
support supremum, not a triangle-inequality artifact.  Applying `Q_h` adds
only a fixed `O_h(1)` amount; an order-`k` fixed-step window adds `hk`.

The explicit-formula detector used in the R71/R102/full-field chain needs
its whole window inside the large positive scale region.  At a left block
endpoint this requires, in particular,

```text
vartheta(y)+hk+H <= R-O(1).                            (6.3)
```

If (6.3) fails, (5.1) remains a correct calculation on a pure exponential,
but it is not a lower bound for one residue of the completed arithmetic
field.  The long translated window meets the initial boundary; the
low-scale and trivial-residue terms are no longer negligible and cancel the
apparently enormous finite-Euler multipliers.  Phase-tuning (5.3) at
`y>>R` therefore does not evade the support ledger.

It is true that in the isolated tail (3.1), translates with `q>M` vanish.
That reduces the *tail's* effective span to `log M`.  It does not shorten
(6.2) for the exact heads or the full field.  Dropping those long head
translates changes both (1.7) and the zero multiplier (1.4).

## 7. Regular-schedule incompatibility theorem

The frozen Vaughan schedule used by the proved full-field transfer has

```text
H=R/k,
U=V=exp[(1/2-c/k)R_0],       c>1/4,
hk^2<=(2c-o(1))R_0.                                  (7.1)
```

Across a block of length `H`, its permitted free cofactor obeys

```text
log M <=(2c+1+o(1))R/k+O_h(k).                        (7.2)
```

The precise fixed constant in (7.2) is immaterial below.  The incompatibility
is captured by the following elementary theorem.

### Theorem 7.1 (no regular faithful collapse by the full Euler-product filter)

Fix `A,C>0`.  There do not exist, for all sufficiently large `R`, parameters
`k,y` satisfying simultaneously

```text
k^2<=CR,
y>=exp(A R/k),
vartheta(y)<=CR.                                      (7.3)
```

#### Proof

The prime number theorem gives `vartheta(y)~y`, so the third inequality
implies `y=O(R)`.  The second then gives

```text
A R/k<=log R+O(1),
k>=(A+o(1))R/log R.                                   (7.4)
```

But `(R/log R)^2/R=R/log^2 R` tends to infinity, contradicting the first
inequality.  This proves the theorem.

Taking any fixed `A` below the cofactor exponent in (7.2) shows that the
proved regular schedule cannot both collapse the cofactor interval and
retain a full completed zero detector.  Choosing the formally tempting
`k~R/log R` would make `M` polynomial in `R`, but it violates the
`hk^2=O(R)` support and periodic-Euler condition by a factor tending to
infinity.

For every admissible faithful choice, `y=O(R)` and (4.1) is at best
`1/log R` in energy.  This is `X^o(1)`, not `X^(-eta)`, where `X=exp R`.

## 8. The periodic-Euler defect is an even stronger gate

The uniform order-`k` Euler estimate recorded in the full-field theorem has
the shape

```text
abs(e_k(x))
 <<exp[C_h(k^2+k log(k+2))]
   [U^(k+1)log(2x)+(UV)^(k+1)]x^(-k-1/2).             (8.1)
```

For a translated scale which remains in its validity range,

```text
q^(-1/2)abs(e_k(x/q))
 <<q^k times the right side of (8.1),                 (8.2)
```

with `log(2x)` harmlessly enlarged.  Therefore the direct, currently proved
transfer through (1.2) costs

```text
sum_(q|P_y)q^k
 =product_(p<=y)(1+p^k),                              (8.3)

log product_(p<=y)(1+p^k)
 =k vartheta(y)+O(pi(y)).                             (8.4)
```

If the unfiltered remainder has a fixed negative exponent `-aR` after the
cutoffs are selected, (8.1)--(8.4) retain a fixed negative exponent only if

```text
k vartheta(y)+C_hk^2+O_h(k log k)<=(a-o(1))R.          (8.5)
```

In particular,

```text
vartheta(y)=O(R/k),          y=O(R/k).                 (8.6)
```

This range lies exponentially below the cofactor endpoint
`M=exp[Theta(R/k)]`.  Its rough-sieve density is only
`asymp1/log(R/k)`.

Equation (8.3) is an audit of the existing Euler lemma, not a theorem that
signed cancellation in the transformed exact head is impossible.  To beat
it one would need a new sieve-weighted periodic-Euler theorem.  Section 9
shows why that theorem cannot simply declare the transformed exact head to
be an error.

## 9. Exact completion retains every zero pole

For `Re(s)>1`, (2.6) gives the Dirichlet-series identity

```text
sum_n (mu_y*Lambda)(n)n^(-s)
 =E_y(s)[-zeta'(s)/zeta(s)].                           (9.1)
```

Both sides continue meromorphically wherever the logarithmic derivative
does, because `E_y` is a finite entire Dirichlet polynomial.  If `rho` is a
zeta zero of multiplicity `m_rho`, then

```text
principal_part_(s=rho) (9.1)
 =-m_rho E_y(rho)/(s-rho).                            (9.2)
```

The coefficient is nonzero.  Applying `Q_h` multiplies (9.2) by the nonzero
fixed factor (5.7).

There is no contradiction with annihilation of the evaluated Vaughan
center.  Indeed

```text
B_p exp(R/2)=(1-p^(-1))exp(R/2),                      (9.3)

B_p[R exp(R/2)]
 =[(1-p^(-1))R+p^(-1)log p]exp(R/2).                  (9.4)
```

Thus every `B_p` preserves the rank-two center space, and `Q_h` kills that
space exactly.  The zero pole in (9.2) belongs to the exact arithmetic
completion, not to the rank-two pole center.

Suppose a long `B_y` is applied only to the frozen tail and (3.4) makes that
piece simpler.  Subtracting it from (9.1) leaves the transformed exact heads
and cutoff shells.  Equation (9.2) proves that the sum of all completed
pieces still has the full zero principal part.  Unless the tail estimate
itself already excludes that pole, the complementary pieces must carry it.
Calling them a power-small Euler defect outside the range (8.5) assumes the
missing fixed-power theorem.

At the maximal endpoint `y` beyond the active arithmetic range, (2.8)
makes this especially transparent: the completion is the `-mu(n)log n`
field.  A fixed-power bound for that field is a power Mertens/inverse-zeta
statement of the same zero-sensitive type as the desired strip.
