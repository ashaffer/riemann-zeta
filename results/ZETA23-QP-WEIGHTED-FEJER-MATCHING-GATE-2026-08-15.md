# QP weighted Fejer gate: an actual-node matching defeats absolute covariance

**Date:** 2026-08-15

**Verdict:** Proposition 4.1 of the coefficient-sensitive cross-Gram report
cannot be extended to arbitrary coefficient vectors by replacing the exact
arithmetic progression with effective support plus additive energy.  The
proposed absolute directional estimate

```text
|C_t(a)| <= delta^(1-o(1)) Z_a                         (0.1)
```

is false under every structural input used in that promotion: stable
normalization, `asymp delta^-2` effective support, negative packet moments,
small nonzero scalar difference moments, a scalar one-parameter phase curve,
and the project polynomial aperture.  The counterconfiguration transfers,
with a power-small error, to positive weights on the **actual prime-log
nodes** by Baker--Harman--Pintz mesh interpolation.

The large matching covariance has the **positive** sign and is harmless for
positive QP.  A second exact construction below is sharper: a subcritical
arithmetic-progression block has stable normalization, maximal additive
energy, all scalar difference moments `o(1)`, and a **negative** covariance
larger than `delta Z_a` by an arbitrary fixed power.  Thus even the corrected
one-sided estimate cannot hold for arbitrary measures and coefficient
vectors.

The corrected survivor is

```text
Re C_t(a) >= -delta^(1-o(1)) Z_a,                     (0.2)
```

with dyadic depth factors retained.  Section 3 proves that (0.2) still needs
a coefficient-sensitive theorem special to the natural prime measure; it
does not follow from weighted Freiman structure, KMT-sized scalar moments,
or stable normalization.  Nothing here refutes (0.2) for the natural smooth
von Mangoldt measure itself, and nothing here proves or refutes QP.

## 1. Exact scalar phase construction

Let `0<delta<1`, choose an integer `m` with

```text
16 m delta^2 < 1,                                    (1.1)
```

and take integer frequencies

```text
A={a_1,...,a_m},             T=A union (A+d).         (1.2)
```

They will be chosen in Section 4 so that every nonzero oriented difference
in `A-A` is unique and all required nonzero Fourier support sets below are
disjoint.  On the
circle put

```text
U(x)=sum_j exp(-i a_j x),          W(x)=exp(-i d x).  (1.3)
```

For `b>0`, define the positive density

```text
f_b(x)=|1-b U(x)(1+W(x))|^2
       +b^2 |U(x)(1-W(x))|^2.                         (1.4)
```

The two squares have an exact cancellation:

```text
f_b=1-2b Re[U(1+W)]+4b^2 |U|^2.                      (1.5)
```

Consequently its Haar normalization is

```text
Z_0=integral f_b=1+4m b^2.                            (1.6)
```

Choose the smaller root of

```text
b/(1+4m b^2)=delta,
b=[1-sqrt(1-16m delta^2)]/(8m delta).                 (1.7)
```

Let `mu=f_b dx/(2 pi Z_0)` and

```text
Phi(v)=integral exp(ivx)dmu(x).                       (1.8)
```

Reading the Fourier coefficients in (1.5) gives exactly

```text
Phi(a_j)=Phi(a_j+d)=-delta,                           (1.9)
Phi(d)=0,                                             (1.10)
Phi(a_i-a_j)=rho=4b^2/Z_0=4b delta       (i!=j),      (1.11)
```

and every other nonzero difference generated by `T` has moment zero.  Thus
all selected nonzero difference moments are `O(delta^2)`.  In particular,
they are far smaller than the logarithmic KMT bound.

This is a probability measure on a scalar one-parameter phase curve.  It is
not an abstract sign-vector moment matrix.

## 2. Stable candidate and the exact covariance violation

Take the uniform packet coefficient vector

```text
alpha_t=delta,                  t in T,               (2.1)
D(x)=sum_(t in T) exp(-itx)=U(x)(1+W(x)).             (2.2)
```

The candidate square is `|1+delta D|^2`.  From (1.9)--(1.11),

```text
E_mu D=-2m delta,
E_mu |D|^2=2m+2m(m-1)rho.                             (2.3)
```

Its exact normalization is therefore

```text
Z_a=1-2m delta^2+2m(m-1)rho delta^2.                 (2.4)
```

Now take the legal query frequency `t=d`.  Since `Phi(d)=0`, the product
term in the centered covariance vanishes.  The `m` matched ordered pairs

```text
(a_j+d)-a_j=d                                        (2.5)
```

translate the query exactly to zero.  The other contributing ordered pairs
translate it to a Sidon difference.  Hence

```text
C_d(delta 1_T)
 =delta^2 [m+m(m-1)rho] > 0.                         (2.6)
```

Fix `kappa in (0,1/8)` and take

```text
2m delta^2=kappa+o(1).                               (2.7)
```

Writing `b=x delta+o(delta)`, equation (1.7) becomes

```text
x=1+2 kappa x^2.                                     (2.8)
```

Equations (2.4) and (2.6) tend to

```text
Z_a -> 1-kappa+2x kappa^2,
C_d -> kappa/2+x kappa^2.                            (2.9)
```

Both are fixed positive constants.  Therefore

```text
|C_d|/(delta Z_a) -> infinity.                       (2.10)
```

This disproves (0.1).  Notice the polarity: (2.6) improves a lower antenna
bound.  Replacing (0.1) by the lower estimate (0.2) keeps this contribution
for free.

## 3. A subcritical AP defeats even the one-sided generalization

The critical length `delta^-2` in Proposition 4.1 is indispensable.  A
shorter AP can have maximal additive energy without enough bad mean to force
Fejer concentration.

Let

```text
T={t_0+jq:0<=j<L},             t_0>2Lq,              (3.1)
D(x)=sum_(j<L) exp[-i(t_0+jq)x].                      (3.2)
```

The offset separates `T` from its difference set.  Define the positive Haar
density

```text
g_b(x)=|1-bD(x)|^2,
Z_1=integral g_b=1+Lb^2,                              (3.3)
```

and choose the smaller root

```text
b/(1+Lb^2)=delta,
b=[1-sqrt(1-4Ldelta^2)]/(2Ldelta).                    (3.4)
```

This requires only `4Ldelta^2<1`.  For the resulting probability measure,

```text
Phi(t_0+jq)=-delta,                                   (3.5)
Phi(rq)=b^2(L-|r|)/Z_1,       0<|r|<L.               (3.6)
```

Thus the largest nonzero difference moment is `O(Ldelta^2)`.

Take again the uniform candidate coefficient `alpha_j=delta`.  The exact AP
additive energy and endpoint triple count are

```text
E_4=integral |D|^4=(2L^3+L)/3,
N_0=#{(i,j,l):j=i+l}=L(L+1)/2.                       (3.7)
```

Put

```text
B_D=integral |D|^2 dmu=(L+b^2 E_4)/Z_1.              (3.8)
```

At the endpoint query `t=t_0`, direct Fourier coefficient extraction gives

```text
Z_a=1-2Ldelta^2+delta^2 B_D,
C_(t_0)(delta 1_T)=delta^3(B_D-N_0).                 (3.9)
```

Now write

```text
R=delta^-2,              L=R^theta,
1/2<theta<2/3.                                       (3.10)
```

Then

```text
b=delta(1+o(1)),
max_(r!=0)|Phi(rq)| <<L/R=o(1),
B_D=L+O(L^3/R),
Z_a=1+o(1),
C_(t_0)=-(1/2+o(1))delta^3L^2.                       (3.11)
```

Consequently

```text
C_(t_0)/(delta Z_a)
 =-(1/2+o(1))L^2/R
 =-R^(2theta-1+o(1))/2 ->-infinity.                 (3.12)
```

This is a one-sided failure, not a polarity artifact.  The packet AP has
maximal energy `asymp L^3`, so weighted BSG returns precisely the structure
already displayed.  Fejer does not contradict it because

```text
delta sqrt(L)=sqrt(L/R)->0;                           (3.13)
```

the negative mean lies below the square-root fluctuation scale of this
subcritical block.  Stable normalization remains `1+o(1)`.

The construction also fits the project aperture with every nonzero lag in
the KMT range.  For example, take

```text
q asyp Y^.01,          theta=.6,          c=.019.    (3.14)
```

Then `L=Y^(2ctheta+o(1))` and the frequency top has exponent

```text
.01+2ctheta=.0328<50/33.                             (3.15)
```

All scalar difference moments tend to zero by a fixed power, yet the
negative covariance misses the required bound by
`R^(2theta-1)=Y^.0076`.

## 4. The matching construction fits the project aperture

There is no exponential-frequency Riesz product hidden in the example.  An
explicit polynomial-size Golomb set suffices.  Put

```text
Q=10m^3,
a_j=100m^4+Qj+j^2,                 1<=j<=m,
d=30m^4.                                             (4.1)
```

If `a_i-a_j=a_k-a_l` with `i!=j` and `k!=l`, then

```text
(i-j)[Q+i+j]=(k-l)[Q+k+l].                            (4.2)
```

If `i-j` and `k-l` differed, the `Q` term in (4.2) would have magnitude at
least `10m^3`, while the remaining terms have magnitude below `4m^2`.
Thus the differences agree; then the sums agree, and the ordered pairs are
identical.  The large offsets also make

```text
A, A+d, (A-A)\{0}, d+((A-A)\{0}), d,
2d-((A-A)\{0})                                      (4.3)
```

disjoint whenever the exact Fourier calculation requires them to be.

For the QP depth

```text
delta=Y^(-c),             m asymp delta^-2=Y^(2c),   (4.4)
```

all frequencies in (4.1) have size `Y^(8c+o(1))`.  At `c=.019`,

```text
8c=.152,
.01 < .152 < 50/33.                                  (4.5)
```

Thus `T`, `d`, and every query used in the calculation lie inside the
audited project aperture for all sufficiently large `Y`.

## 5. Transfer to positive weights on actual prime-log nodes

The preceding model can be placed on the prescribed actual support.  Rescale
the circle to `[0,w]`, so the integer character `k` becomes frequency
`2 pi k/w`.  Use the prime nodes

```text
u_p=log(p/Y),                  Y<p<Y exp(w).           (5.1)
```

Baker--Harman--Pintz gives maximal logarithmic mesh

```text
h_Y <<_w Y^(-19/40).                                 (5.2)
```

Partition `[0,w]` into nearest-node Voronoi cells `I_p` and put

```text
lambda_p=integral_(I_p) f_b(u)du / integral_0^w f_b(u)du. (5.3)
```

These are positive probability weights on actual primes, hence on legal
actual prime-power nodes.  For every Lipschitz `g`, nearest-node quadrature
gives

```text
|sum_p lambda_p g(u_p)-integral g dmu|
 <= h_Y ||g'||_infinity.                              (5.4)
```

The largest character frequency in Sections 1--3 is
`K=Y^(8c+o(1))`.  Thus individual moment errors have exponent

```text
8c-19/40=-.323                  at c=.019.            (5.5)
```

For the quadratic covariance integrand,
`||(|D|^2 exp(idu))'||_infinity <<R^2 K`, where
`R=2m=Y^(2c+o(1))`.  Its transfer error has exponent

```text
12c-19/40=-.247                 at c=.019.            (5.6)
```

Both errors are `o(delta^2)`, since `delta^2=Y^(-.038)`.  Therefore the
actual-node measure has

```text
Phi(a_j),Phi(a_j+d)=-delta+o(delta^2),
all selected nonzero difference moments=O(delta^2),
Z_a=1-kappa+2xkappa^2+o(1),
C_d=kappa/2+xkappa^2+o(1).                            (5.7)
```

The same transfer applies to the subcritical AP construction.  With (3.14),
the character error exponent is `.0328-.475=-.4422`; the unscaled quadratic
integrand error exponent is

```text
.01+6c theta-19/40=-.3966.                           (5.8)
```

It is negligible compared with every main term in (3.11)--(3.12).

These transfers are why the examples are actual-prime-**support**
obstructions, not generic moment toys.  The weights (5.3), and their AP
analogue, are deliberately constructed; they are not the natural smooth von
Mangoldt weights.  Accordingly, (5.7) and the transferred AP do not disprove
a coefficient-sensitive theorem special to those natural weights.

## 6. Why weighted BSG/Freiman does not repair the claim

The set `T` has a difference of multiplicity `m`: namely `d`.  Nevertheless
its total additive energy is only `O(m^2)=O(|T|^2)`.  The Golomb property
makes all remaining nontrivial difference multiplicities bounded.  Hence a
weighted Balog--Szemeredi--Gowers theorem sees only the baseline energy and
does not return a long arithmetic progression.

This is the exact gap between Proposition 4.1 and an arbitrary effective-
support statement:

```text
long AP:
  many popular lags coherently force phase concentration;

one matching:
  one popular lag can create a constant translated zero resonance,
  while a second positive square cancels its scalar Fourier moment;

subcritical AP:
  inverse theory succeeds completely, but the Fejer signal-to-noise ratio
  is sqrt(L/R)=o(1), and the negative covariance is power too large.       (6.1)
```

Stable normalization does not distinguish them.  Nor does a scalar KMT
bound, because (1.10)--(1.11) are stronger than KMT.  Additive energy does
distinguish them, but in the wrong direction: the matching has too little
energy for inverse theory even though it already violates the absolute
covariance target.
