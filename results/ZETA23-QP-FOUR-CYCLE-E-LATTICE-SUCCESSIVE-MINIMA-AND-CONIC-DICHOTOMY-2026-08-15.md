# QP four-cycle: `E`-lattice successive-minima and conic dichotomy

**Date:** 2026-08-15  
**Verdict:** the paired-completion invariant admits a rigorous generic versus
tangent dichotomy and now proves a sharp **pointwise** theorem.  For every
fixed all-distinct actual color matrix,

```text
m(C) << D^(1/2) q^o(1).                            (0.1)
```

In the generic branch the short common-level lattice contains only `O(D)`
possible differences.  A new fixed-secant norm reduction shows that each
nonzero `(C,E)` is realized only `q^o(1)` times: its completion conic is an
exact binary quadratic norm equation with nonzero right side.  In the
exceptional branch all completions lie in one rational rank-two plane and
the earlier conic theorem gives the same square-root bound.  The full-integer
translation family shows that the exponent in (0.1) is sharp geometrically.

This does **not** prove the weighted global four-cycle estimate.  Summing the
pointwise estimate over the already affordable `D` determinant layers still
costs an extra factor `D`; the remaining issue is weighted reuse of the
generic secants and exceptional rational planes across colors.

---

## 1. The common-level lattice

Fix an oriented, all-distinct actual-shell color matrix

```text
C=(c11,c12;c21,c22)                                (1.1)
```

having at least one completion.  For two completions `X,X'`, let

```text
E=M(X)-M(X'),       M(X)=(a_i b_j)_ij.             (1.2)
```

At the active asymptotic scale, residual-sum pinning gives the exact equation

```text
c11 E11+c22 E22-c12 E12-c21 E21=0,                (1.3)
```

and residual subtraction gives

```text
||E||_infinity <= K D                              (1.4)
```

for a fixed shell/cutoff constant `K`.

Put

```text
w=(c11,-c12,-c21,c22),
Lambda_C={E in Z^4 : w dot E=0}.                   (1.5)
```

Distinct prime powers in the project shell have distinct prime bases: two
different powers of one prime have ratio at least `2`, whereas the ratio of
any two shell nodes is less than `exp(2w0)<2`.  Hence the four colors are
pairwise coprime and `w` is primitive.  The standard orthogonal-lattice
formula therefore gives

```text
rank Lambda_C=3,          det Lambda_C=||w||_2 asymp q.  (1.6)
```

Let `lambda_1<=lambda_2<=lambda_3` be its Euclidean successive minima.

## 2. Generic short-vector count

For a fixed-rank lattice, the standard successive-minima counting lemma says

```text
#(Lambda intersect [-B,B]^4)
 << product_(i=1)^3 (1+B/lambda_i).                (2.1)
```

This follows, for example, by choosing a reduced lattice basis comparable to
the successive minima and counting its integer coefficients.  Minkowski's
second theorem gives

```text
lambda_1 lambda_2 lambda_3 asymp det Lambda_C asymp q. (2.2)
```

### Theorem 2.1 (generic `E` count)

If

```text
lambda_1 lambda_2 >= c D                           (2.3)
```

for a fixed positive `c`, then

```text
# {E in Lambda_C : ||E||_infinity<=K D}
 <<_(c,K) D+D^3/q.                                 (2.4)
```

Indeed, expand the product in (2.1).  Every term of degree at most two is
`O(D)` because nonzero integral vectors have length at least one and
`lambda_1 lambda_2>>D`; the cubic term is `O(D^3/q)` by (2.2).  At

```text
D=q^(16/33+o(1)),
D^3/q=q^(15/33+o(1))=o(D),                         (2.5)
```

so the right side is `O(D)`.

This counts all short kernel vectors and therefore all realized `E`, but it
does not bound how many ordered completion pairs realize the same nonzero
`E`.

## 3. Two short directions force one rational plane

Suppose instead that

```text
lambda_1 lambda_2 < c D.                           (3.1)
```

Then (2.2) gives

```text
lambda_3 >> q/D.                                   (3.2)
```

Since

```text
q/D^2=q^(1/33-o(1)) -> infinity,                   (3.3)
```

we have `lambda_3>2KD` for all sufficiently large `q`.  Consequently the
group generated by all vectors satisfying (1.4) has rank at most two.  In
particular there is one saturated rational plane `W_C` such that

```text
E in W_C for every pair of completions of C.        (3.4)
```

This is stronger than a decomposition into many local tangent directions:
for each exceptional fixed color matrix, all of its short differences lie
in one rank-two chart.

The example

```text
C=(10009,10039;10069,10099)                        (3.5)
```

shows that this alternative is real at the level of the bare lattice.  Its
kernel contains

```text
E(u,v)=(2v-u,3v-2u;u,v),        det E=2(u-v)^2,     (3.6)
```

and therefore has order `D^2` vectors in a radius-`D` box.  All four entries
of `C` are distinct primes and `det C=-1800`.  Thus covolume alone cannot
remove (3.1).

### 3.1 A weighted warning: exceptional colors alone have polynomial mass

The tempting bound

```text
sum_(C: lambda1(C) lambda2(C)<D)
 |z_c11 z_c12 z_c21 z_c22| << q^o ||z||_2^4       (3.7)
```

is false if the sum ranges over all actual-prime color matrices, even after
requiring all four colors distinct and `0<|det C|<=D`.

Let `L=floor(sqrt(D))/100`.  Partition a fixed positive-length subinterval
of the project shell into integer intervals of length `L`.  The prime number
theorem and pigeonhole principle give one such interval containing

```text
n >> L/log q                                           (3.8)
```

ordinary primes; call this set `A`.  Put `z_p=n^(-1/2)` on `A` and zero
elsewhere.  If `r(s)` counts ordered pairs in `A^2` with sum `s`, then

```text
sum_s r(s)^2 >= n^4/(2L+1).                        (3.9)
```

At most `6n^2` of these additive quadruples have a repeated entry.  Thus,
for large `q`, there are `>>n^4/L` all-distinct tuples

```text
a+d=b+c.                                           (3.10)
```

Write the four primes as `a=M+x1,b=M+x2,c=M+x3,d=M+x4`, with all `xi` in
an interval of length `L`.  For `C=(a,b;c,d)`, (3.10) gives

```text
det C=ad-bc=x1*x4-x2*x3,
0<|det C|<=L^2<=D.                                 (3.11)
```

Nonzero follows because equal sum and equal product would make the two
unordered pairs identical.

For the signed normal `w=(a,-b,-c,d)`, two independent kernel vectors are

```text
e0=(1,1,1,1),
e1=(x4-x2, x4-x1, 0, x2-x1).                      (3.12)
```

The second is nonzero for an all-distinct tuple, `w dot e1=0`, and
`||e1||<<L`.  Hence

```text
lambda1(C) lambda2(C) << L <D.                    (3.13)
```

The contribution of these tuples to the left side of (3.7) is

```text
>> (n^4/L)*n^(-2) >> L/(log q)^2
 =q^(8/33-o(1)),                                   (3.14)
```

which is polynomially larger than `q^o(1)`.

This does **not** refute a sum restricted to color matrices having an actual
carrier completion.  The construction controls only the colors.  It proves
that any exceptional weighted theorem must use the realized product-window
incidence; two short relations and a rank-two discrete Brascamp--Lieb bound
alone cannot establish it.

## 4. Rank-one completion matrices lie on a conic

Fix one completion `X0`, and write `P0=M(X0)`.  By (3.4), every other product
matrix is in

```text
P0+W_C.                                             (4.1)
```

Let `Gamma=W_C intersect Z^4`, choose a reduced basis `V1,V2` of `Gamma`,
and use integral coordinates

```text
P(x,y)=P0+x V1+y V2.                               (4.2)
```

Every completion satisfies

```text
F(x,y)=det P(x,y)=0,                               (4.3)
```

where `F` has degree at most two.  A reduced-basis coordinate estimate and
`||P-P0||_infinity<<D` put all relevant `(x,y)` in a square of side `O(D)`.

The product-matrix map is injective on the actual generic sector.  Indeed,
two completions with the same product matrix have `E=0`; the proved
multiplicative-Sidon/short-residual lemma then forces the ordered carriers to
coincide (the already classified permutation sectors are outside this
generic statement).

There are now two cases.

1. If the degree-two curve (4.3) is absolutely irreducible, Pila's uniform
   integral-point theorem for a degree-`d` plane curve in a square of side
   `N` gives

   ```text
   #F(Z^2) << N^(1/2) (log N)^7                 (d=2). (4.4)
   ```

2. If `F` is reducible or has degree at most one, its zero set is a union of
   at most two affine lines, unless `F` vanishes identically.  No such line
   can contain three actual completions: three roots of
   `det(P+tV)` force that quadratic to vanish identically, hence `det V=0`,
   while the difference of any two distinct actual completions has nonzero
   determinant by the proved Sidon lemma.  Thus there are at most two points
   per line.  If `F` vanishes identically, the same argument permits at most
   one completion.

The geometrically reducible but non-rational-line case has at most the line
intersection's real integral points and is no larger.

### Theorem 4.1 (exceptional-plane square-root theorem)

Under (3.1), the fixed color matrix has

```text
m(C) << D^(1/2) (log D)^7 = D^(1/2) q^o(1).         (4.5)
```

Thus its ordered pair energy is

```text
m(C)^2 << D q^o(1).                                (4.6)
```

The exponent is sharp for the full-integer translation tangent family,
which has `m(C) asymp sqrt(D)`.  That family traces an irreducible parabola
in its affine rank-two chart; it is not a reducible ruling line.

The integral-point input used in (4.4) is J. Pila, *Density of integer points
on plane algebraic curves* (1996), Theorem on pages 1 and 8; for degree two
it gives the displayed uniform `sqrt(N)` polylogarithmic bound.  It refines
the Bombieri--Pila determinant method.

### 4.1 Parabolic inverse refinement

The conic can be classified more sharply.  If its quadratic part has
nonzero discriminant, completing squares reduces it to a nonzero binary
norm equation, so the number of integral points in the polynomial box is
only `q^o(1)`.  Reducible sections contain only constantly many actual
completions.  Hence any fixed color with `m(C)>=q^epsilon` for fixed
`epsilon>0` must lie on a parabolic plane section.

An irreducible rational parabola has one rational point at infinity.  Its
rank-one matrix parametrization factors over `Q[t]` as

```text
M(t)=(u0+t u1)(v0+t v1)^T,                         (4.7)
```

up to a fixed denominator.  The alternative degree splittings would make
all completion differences rank at most one and are excluded.  Primitive
actual carrier factorization introduces only `q^o(1)` gcd classes, since
the gcd of two affine linear forms divides their fixed resultant.  Thus the
power-multiplicity exceptional branch is a union of `q^o(1)` rational
tangent charts.  The full proof is in
`ZETA23-QP-EXCEPTIONAL-CONIC-PARABOLIC-INVERSE-THEOREM-2026-08-15.md`.

### 4.2 Fixed nonzero `E`: exact binary norm reduction

There is a stronger arithmetic theorem for the multiplicity of one fixed
secant.  Use the signed color matrix

```text
K=( c11 -c12; -c21 c22 ),             det K=k,      (4.7)
```

fix a realized nonzero difference `E`, and put

```text
Delta=det E,             M=M(X),       M-E=M(X').   (4.8)
```

In the all-distinct actual sector, `Delta!=0`.  Indeed

```text
Delta=-det(a,a')det(b,b'),                            (4.9)
```

and a vanishing row or column determinant, together with multiplicative
Sidonicity and the sub-unit product interval, makes the two completions
identical.

The equal-level identity is

```text
<K,E>=0,                 <K,M>=L.                    (4.10)
```

Define three integral matrices

```text
A=E*K^T,
T=2*M*adj(E)-Delta*I,
U=A*T-L*Delta*I.                                    (4.11)
```

They satisfy the exact identities

```text
tr A=tr T=tr U=0,
A^2=-k*Delta*I,             T^2=Delta^2*I,
tr(A*T)=2*L*Delta,
A*U+U*A=0,
U^2=Delta^2*(L^2+k*Delta)*I.                        (4.12)
```

For completeness, `tr A=0` is (4.10).  Since both `M` and `M-E` have
determinant zero,

```text
<adj(E)^T,M>=Delta,                                  (4.13)
```

which gives the assertions about `T`.  The identity
`XY+YX=tr(XY)I` for traceless two-by-two matrices then proves the last three
relations in (4.12).

The transformation is injective.  Because `k*Delta!=0`, recover

```text
T=A^(-1)(U+L*Delta*I),
M=(T+Delta*I)E/(2*Delta).                            (4.14)
```

Write

```text
A=(alpha beta; gamma -alpha),
U=(r s;t -r).                                       (4.15)
```

The last two equations of (4.12) become

```text
2*alpha*r+beta*t+gamma*s=0,
r^2+s*t=Delta^2*N,          N=L^2+k*Delta.          (4.16)
```

If `beta!=0`, eliminating `t` gives the integral binary quadratic equation

```text
beta*r^2-2*alpha*r*s-gamma*s^2
  =beta*Delta^2*N,                                  (4.17)
```

whose discriminant is

```text
4*(alpha^2+beta*gamma)=-4*k*Delta!=0.               (4.18)
```

The case `gamma!=0` is symmetric.  If `beta=gamma=0`, then `r=0` and
`s*t=Delta^2*N`, an ordinary divisor equation.

The right side is nonzero in the QP range.  Residual pinning gives

```text
|L| >> |k|q,
```

whereas `|k*Delta|<<D^3=o(q^2)`.  Hence

```text
N=L^2+k*Delta asymp L^2!=0.                          (4.19)
```

We use the following elementary uniform form of the binary norm bound.
If an integral binary quadratic form has nonzero discriminant, its
coefficients, nonzero represented integer, and coordinate box are all at
most `q^O(1)`, then the number of representations in that box is `q^o(1)`.
For square discriminant this follows by factoring two integral linear forms
and applying the divisor bound.  For nonsquare discriminant, map a solution
of `ax^2+bxy+cy^2=n` to

```text
2*a*x+(b+sqrt(b^2-4ac))*y,                          (4.20)
```

whose quadratic norm is `4*a*n`.  Ideal divisors contribute `q^o(1)`
possibilities.  Generators of one ideal differ by units; there are `O(log q)`
such generators in a polynomial-size box in the real case and only finitely
many in the imaginary case.  Imprimitive coefficients are first divided by
their common gcd.  This proof is uniform in the varying form.

All quantities in (4.17) and the image of the QP product box under (4.11)
have polynomial size in `q`.  The injectivity (4.14), together with the
already proved injectivity of the actual completion-to-product-matrix map,
therefore proves:

### Theorem 4.2 (fixed-secant multiplicity)

For every all-distinct actual color matrix and every realized nonzero
difference matrix,

```text
nu(C,E) << q^o(1).                                  (4.21)
```

This improves the generic fixed-`E` Pila bound from `D^(1/2+o(1))` to a
divisor/Pell bound.  The exact matrix identities are replayed in
`qp_four_cycle_pair_energy_audit.py`.

## 5. Rational-slope tangent patches

The local rational-slope generalization can also be proved exactly.  Let
`r,s` be positive coprime integers and consider one affine carrier line

```text
(a(t),b(t))=(a0+r t,b0-s t).                       (5.1)
```

Along it,

```text
a(t)b(t)=V-rs(t-t0)^2                              (5.2)
```

for real constants `V,t0`.  The inverse image of a product interval
`[X-E,X+E]` has at most two connected components.  On either component its
length is at most

```text
2 sqrt(2E/(rs)).                                    (5.3)
```

To see this, put `u=V-X`.  The possible values of `|t-t0|` have squares in

```text
[(u-E)/(rs),(u+E)/(rs)] intersect [0,infinity).     (5.4)
```

If the lower endpoint is nonpositive, the component crosses the vertex and
has twice the positive-side radius, giving (5.3).  If it is positive, the
two signs are separate components; rationalizing the difference of the two
square roots gives the stronger bound without the leading `2`.  Thus one
connected integer cluster has

```text
L_(r,s) << 1+sqrt(D/(rs))                           (5.5)
```

points in the QP product band.

Now take a row progression `a_u=a0+r u`, a column progression
`b_v=b0+s v`, and suppose the color on every retained cell depends only on
`u+v`.  Along a fixed anti-diagonal `u+v=d`, the carrier pair moves in the
direction `(r,-s)`.  Assume that its retained cells lie in one component
from (5.3), and that different anti-diagonals have different colors.  Let

```text
H_uv=|z_(color(u+v))|                               (5.6)
```

on the patch, with missing cells filled by zero.  Each color occurs in at
most `L_(r,s)` matrix entries, so

```text
||H||_F^2 <= L_(r,s) ||z||_2^2.                    (5.7)
```

The absolute four-cycle mass is bounded by the full fourth trace, and hence

```text
Q_patch <= tr((H H*)^2)
        <= ||H||_F^4
        << (1+D/(rs)) ||z||_2^4.                   (5.8)
```

If a color labels at most `mu` anti-diagonals, the right side acquires the
factor `mu^2`.  No balanced-square assumption is needed.

### Theorem 5.1 (rational tangent-patch theorem)

Every injectively colored connected primitive `(r,-s)` tangent patch has
arbitrary-complex weighted four-cycle mass

```text
<< (1+D/(rs)) ||z||_2^4.                           (5.9)
```

This recovers the additive `(1,-1)` Hankel patch at cost `O(D)` and improves
as the primitive direction becomes steeper.  Moreover,

```text
sum_(r,s>=1, rs<=D) 1/(rs) <=(1+log D)^2,           (5.10)
```

so the *directional* budget is only polylogarithmic.  What is not automatic
is the required bounded reuse of colors among parallel components and among
different primitive directions.

### 5.1 Bounded color reuse is false geometrically

There is an exact multilevel full-integer construction with `D=2048L^2`
having `L(L+1)=Theta(D)` fixed-color tangent clusters, all in direction
`(1,-1)`, and `L+1=Theta(sqrt(D))` completions per cluster.  Every triple
satisfies the literal window `|8abc-q^3|<qD`, the induced unordered triple
system is pair-unique, and all eight labels in each displayed rectangle are
distinct.  Nevertheless the same color occurs in all `Theta(D)` clusters.

With flat normalized weights on its `3L+1` colors, its global ordered
off-diagonal completion energy is

```text
L^2(L+1)^2/(3L+1)^2=(1/18432+o(1))D.              (5.11)
```

More decisively, put weight `1/2` on the common color and
`1/(2 sqrt(L))` on each of the other `3L` colors.  This is a unit vector and
gives

```text
P=L^(1/2)(L+1)^2/16=Theta(D^(5/4)).                (5.12)
```

Thus not only bounded reuse, but the global estimate `P<<D q^o(1)` itself
is false for pair-unique full-integer product-window geometry.  The family
uses general integers rather than actual prime powers, so (5.12) is not a
counterexample to the actual QP theorem.  It proves that the missing global
exceptional estimate must use prime-power arithmetic.  The complete proof
and exact formulas are in
`ZETA23-QP-MULTILEVEL-TANGENT-REUSE-OBSTRUCTION-2026-08-15.md`.

## 6. Exact remaining global gate

The dichotomy proves:

```text
generic lambda1*lambda2 >= D:
    at most O(D) possible E values and nu(C,E)<<q^o;

exceptional lambda1*lambda2 < D:
    one rational rank-two chart and m(C)^2<=D q^o.  (6.1)
```

In the generic branch, summing (4.21) over the `O(D)` short differences
gives

```text
m(C)(m(C)-1)<<D q^o(1).                             (6.2)
```

The exceptional branch has the same conclusion by Theorem 4.1.  Therefore
the uniform pointwise theorem (0.1) is proved in both branches.

It does not yet prove

```text
sum_C m(C)^2 |z_c11 z_c12 z_c21 z_c22|
  << D q^o ||z||_2^4.                              (6.3)
```

Pointwise summation would instead give `D^2 q^o`, because the uncompleted
color mass over all nonzero determinant layers is already of size `D`.
Two weighted reuse issues remain, one in each branch:

* in the generic branch, the `O(D)` possible short secants must be summed
  jointly across colors rather than once for every color matrix;
* in the exceptional branch, the pointwise bound (4.6) cannot simply be
  summed over all determinant layers.  The rational planes must be grouped
  by primitive tangent direction and their color reuse controlled by a
  convolution or spacing bound.

What is not proved is that the exceptional completion/color support admits
a bounded- or `q^o`-reuse partition into precisely those patches.  Equation
(5.10) is a budget, not a global decomposition theorem.

```text
generic O(D) short-E value count:                   PROVED;
one-plane exceptional alternative:                 PROVED;
exceptional fixed-C sqrt(D) completion bound:       PROVED;
fixed nonzero (C,E) multiplicity q^o:               PROVED;
all-distinct fixed-C sqrt(D) completion bound:       PROVED;
rational tangent-patch theorem (5.9):               PROVED;
rational tangent harmonic budget:                   PROVED LOCALLY;
global bounded-reuse tangent partition:             FALSE GEOMETRICALLY;
geometry-only global weighted tangent theorem:      FALSE;
prime-power exceptional tangent theorem:            OPEN;
generic weighted secant-reuse theorem:               OPEN;
weighted pair-energy gate (6.3):                    OPEN;
full four-cycle bound:                              OPEN.
```
