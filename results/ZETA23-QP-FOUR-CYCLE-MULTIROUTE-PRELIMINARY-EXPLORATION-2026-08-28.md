# QP four-cycle: preliminary exploration of all credible routes

**Date:** 2026-08-28
**Status:** comparative research audit; **the sharp four-cycle bound is not
proved**.

**Correction after the next-gate audit.**  The rootwise reconstruction and
the one-parameter `S_Q` target originally recorded in Sections 2, 5, 9, and
11 were not valid.  A maximum over roots does not reconstruct the positive
pair mass.  The exact replacement is the signed two-determinant cell theorem,
which is equivalent to the full pair endpoint up to `O(log^2 q)`, not a
strictly weaker lemma.  The corrected analysis is in
`ZETA23-QP-COLLECTIVE-DYADIC-NEXT-GATE-AND-ROUTE-AUDIT-2026-08-28.md`.

## 0. Executive verdict

This pass changed the route ranking.  The main error in the preceding search
was not a bad estimate near the leaves of the proof tree.  It was an earlier
quantifier inflation:

1. the original problem asks for one weighted pair correlation;
2. several attacks replaced it by a uniform theorem for every row, every
   root, every packet, or every two-index completion;
3. those replacements are elegant and useful, but strictly stronger;
4. failure at the stronger target is therefore not evidence against the
   original endpoint.

The closest known formulation remains

```text
sum_C m_C w_z  <<  D q^o(1).                              (E)
```

This is the exact endpoint isolated in
`ZETA23-QP-FOUR-CYCLE-PAIR-COMPLETION-ENERGY-AUDIT-2026-08-15.md`,
equation (0.1).  All routes below should be judged by whether they actually
imply **(E)**, not merely whether they prove a pleasing surrogate.

The leading research program after the corrected audit is:

> retain the pair weights to the last possible stage; peel only certified
> harmless pieces; extract coherent affine/Hankel packets with charges in the
> original energy; then prove mask-stable unconditionality for the
> packet-free residue.

This is a hybrid of the direct pair and peeling/PAC routes.  A rooted or
trace-formula estimate may be used only if its collective reconstruction is
proved as part of the argument.

A skeptical audit gives an important novelty qualifier: dyadic bucketing,
rooting, and capacitary reweighting are standard reformulations.  What is new
in this note is the comparison and corrected route selection, not a new
arithmetic theorem.

## 1. The three levels that must not be conflated

### 1.1 Exact endpoint

In the notation already used throughout the QP reports, the sharp target is

```text
PAIR(q,D):  sum_C m_C w_z  <<  D q^o(1).                  (1.1)
```

The weights and pairs here are those generated by the original four-cycle
problem.  They are not arbitrary test data.

### 1.2 Intermediate targets

Two useful, but stronger, intermediate statements are:

* **Rooted/dynamic large sieve.**  Keep the distinguished root and prove the
  required estimate only after the root and scale have been fixed.  This is
  the corrected target in
  `ZETA23-QP-SELF-ORBIT-DYADIC-BANDPASS-AND-DYNAMIC-PACKET-CLOSEOUT-2026-08-27.md`
  and `ZETA23-QP-DYNAMIC-CF-PACKETS-AND-TRANSLATE-BESSEL-GATE-2026-08-27.md`.
* **Unrooted all-packets theorem.**  Sum uniformly over all roots or packets.
  This is cleaner, but the extra summation discards information present in
  **PAIR(q,D)**.

A rooted theorem does not imply **PAIR(q,D)** until the reconstruction over
roots is proved.  Likewise, a peeling theorem does not imply it until the
peeled and residual pieces are recombined with their original weights.

### 1.3 Proof technologies

Motohashi/Kuznetsov, continued fractions, energy increment, capacitary
weights, peak/PAC, and theorem provers are *technologies*.  None is itself a
mathematical endpoint.  A technology is useful here only when its input and
output quantifiers match one of the two levels above.

## 2. A concrete new hierarchy

The corrected exploration gives the following hierarchy of scope:

```text
exact weighted pair endpoint (E)
        <=> all signed dyadic (A,B) cells, up to O(log^2 q)
        <=> Cartesian restricted type, up to q^o(1)
        <= uniform arbitrary-pair / full-operator theorem
        <= arbitrary two-index completion theorem.
```

The equivalences use positivity, `1<=|A|,|B|<<D`, and finite Lorentz
interpolation.  The last two implications are one-way sufficient
strengthenings.  A rootwise maximum is not on this hierarchy because it has
no valid reconstruction in general.

This hierarchy explains a recurring pattern in the previous attempts: a
calculation could make genuine progress on an intermediate theorem while
making no numerical progress on the final exponent, because the unproved
reconstruction step was doing all the remaining work.

## 3. Route A: attack the weighted pair endpoint directly

### 3.1 What the route is

Prove (1.1) without first replacing the QP weights by a worst-case family.
The existing pair-completion audit shows why this is the mathematically
closest endpoint.  The plus/minus and row/column variables enter in a
correlated way; retaining that correlation avoids an artificial supremum.

### 3.2 What this exploration learned

The pair formulation is not merely one route among equals.  It is the
acceptance criterion for every other route.  In particular:

* a theorem controlling every packet separately can still lose when the
  packets are reassembled;
* a theorem controlling an unweighted root sum can be much stronger than
  needed;
* replacing the QP-generated weights by arbitrary bounded weights can create
  examples that never occur in the original problem.

The direct route should therefore begin with a *weighted* dyadic
decomposition, not with absolute values over all pair labels.

### 3.3 What it still owes

It needs a genuine cancellation or orthogonality principle between the
QP-generated pairs.  The existing pair identities expose the structure but
do not supply this principle.  Additive energy alone is insufficient, and a
routine Cauchy--Schwarz step generally promotes the problem back to the
stronger all-packets target.

### 3.4 Preliminary rank

**Rank 1 as the theorem to target.**  It is also the least modular route:
success will probably require importing one of the analytic or geometric
mechanisms below.

## 4. Route B: peeling/PAC followed by a residual pair theorem

### 4.1 What the route is

Use exact structural rules to remove pairs whose contribution is already
controlled, and apply the hard theorem only to the residual incidence
structure.  The dynamic-CF and post-peel reports already prove useful halves
of this program.

This should be read as a proof architecture:

```text
original weighted pair family
  -> certified peeling with a charged contribution
  -> structured residual core
  -> analytic estimate on that core
  -> weighted reconstruction.                                  (4.1)
```

### 4.2 A rigorous lesson from the tests

Peeling must be charged, not merely counted.  A low-cardinality residual can
carry large weights, and a large peeled family can be harmless if it has
strong local cancellation.  Thus “most packets peel” is not a sufficient
statement.

The repository's exact peeling and dynamic-packet tests pass, but those tests
certify the arrows internal to (4.1), not the final reconstruction arrow.

### 4.3 New perspective

The valuable output of PAC/peeling is not a bound by itself.  It is a much
narrower class on which an analytic estimate may be true.  This changes the
right research question from

> can one prove a large sieve for every packet family?

to

> what invariant does every non-peelable, QP-weighted residual core possess?

Plausible invariants include bounded continued-fraction complexity,
controlled local multiplicity, or a sparse set of scale transitions.  None
has yet been proved strong enough to yield (1.1).

### 4.4 What it still owes

It must prove both:

1. a weighted accounting identity for the peeled contribution; and
2. a theorem whose hypotheses are guaranteed for every residual core.

Without both, this route only relocates the open problem.

### 4.5 Preliminary rank

**Rank 1 as an architecture, tied with the direct pair route.**  It is not a
standalone endpoint.

## 5. Route C: rooted dynamic/continued-fraction large sieve

### 5.1 What the route is

Keep a root variable long enough to expose continued-fraction packet
structure.  The dynamic-CF work proves exact local structure and shows that
translated packets saturate several natural local bounds.

The correction is that a theorem at one root is not an analytic endpoint.
It becomes useful only with one common capacitary weight or a square-function
estimate that reconstructs the sum over every root.

### 5.2 New normalization found in this pass

The safe order of operations is

```text
retain roots -> localize scale -> use packet structure
             -> prove weighted reconstruction -> sum roots,
```

not

```text
prove one root -> replace all roots by its maximum.
```

For a nonnegative incidence matrix `B`, an exact full-operator implementation
is capacitary: one common positive vector `mu` satisfying

```text
(B^*B)mu <= K*mu
```

at every root gives `||B||^2<=K`.  Allowing a different `mu` at each root,
or proving the inequality at only one root, does not assemble.

### 5.3 What it still owes

Two substantial pieces remain:

* the rooted analytic estimate itself; and
* a collective weighted reconstruction retaining the exact pair masks.

The dynamic-CF theorem proves only the geometric half.  It does not prove the
packet square-function estimate, the translate theorem, or the four-cycle
bound.

### 5.4 Preliminary rank

**Rank 3 as an implementation tool.**  Without reconstruction it is not a
candidate intermediate theorem.  Weighted packet extraction and
mask-stable packet-free unconditionality now rank ahead of it.

## 6. Route D: Motohashi/Kuznetsov specialized to the joint mask

### 6.1 What the route is

Construct a trace/reciprocity formula whose spectral side sees the joint
`(A,B)` mask and whose geometric side returns the collective weighted pair
expression.  A root may remain as an internal coordinate, but it cannot be
removed by a maximum.  The older proposal instead asked for an arbitrary
two-index formula.

### 6.2 What the audit changed

There is no proved bridge from the older arbitrary two-index theorem to the
collective Cartesian target.  The earlier formula pays for both indices
before using the QP dependency between them.  It therefore inherits a known
cubic-scale obstruction.

A viable analytic implementation must specialize earlier:

1. encode the joint determinant cell and Cartesian mask in the test function;
2. perform reciprocity/Poisson before discarding the root dependency;
3. exploit the exact QP pair dependency;
4. prove a collective square-function or participation estimate before the
   final Cauchy--Schwarz step.

This is meaningfully different from proving the old two-index statement and
specializing it afterwards.

### 6.3 What it still owes

No current report constructs that trace formula.  In particular, the
penultimate geometric expression, the admissible test-function class, and
the root-summation error terms have not been matched exactly.

### 6.4 Preliminary rank

**Rank 4 as a route; high upside, high construction risk.**  It is most
promising as the analytic engine inside Routes B--C, not as an independent
arbitrary-index theorem.

## 7. Route E: capacitary or positive weighted estimates

### 7.1 What the route is

Choose non-uniform weights on roots/packets so that expensive local pieces
receive less mass, then prove a positive operator bound.  This is analogous
to choosing a good measure before applying Schur or a square-function
estimate.

### 7.2 Useful new observation

Uniform all-root estimates are unnecessarily rigid.  Finite scans show that
simple non-uniform choices can reduce the operator norm in model families.
This supports using capacity as a *design principle* for a collective
operator theorem.

### 7.3 Why this is not yet a solution

The original pair problem does not permit arbitrary reweighting.  A
capacitary estimate helps only if one proves that its chosen measure
dominates the original pair weights with acceptable loss.  That domination
is currently absent.

For a nonnegative Gram kernel, the valid capacitary statement requires one
common positive weight satisfying every root inequality.  Root-dependent
weights do not assemble.  Thus capacity may improve a collective theorem,
but it does not bypass the weighted reconstruction problem.

### 7.4 Preliminary rank

**Rank 4 as a standalone route; rank 2 as a tool inside Route C.**

## 8. Route F: exact finite verification and theorem proving

### 8.1 What it can do

Lean and exact Python replays are well suited to:

* certify the pair identities and legal changes of variables;
* verify charge conservation in a peeling algorithm;
* check the constants in a proposed rooted reconstruction lemma;
* exhaust finite obstruction templates before analytic effort is invested.

### 8.2 What it cannot presently do

The missing statements are uniform analytic estimates.  Replaying their
finite reductions cannot establish the required `q^o(1)` bound.  A theorem
prover becomes decisive after the new cancellation lemma is stated; it is
not a source of that lemma by itself.

### 8.3 Preliminary rank

**Essential verifier, not an independent proof route.**

## 9. The corrected exact bridge and remaining candidate lemma

### 9.1 Signed two-determinant localization

For two distinct completions put

```text
A=a1*a2'-a2*a1',       B=b1*b2'-b2*b1'.             (9.1)
```

The proved generic reduction gives `1<=|A|,|B|<<D`.  Resolve both signs and
put each ordered pair in the unique cell

```text
2^j<=sigma*A<2^(j+1),
2^k<=tau*B<2^(k+1).                                 (9.2)
```

There are `O(log^2 q)` cells and their nonnegative masses partition the
exact factorial pair endpoint.  Consequently a uniform `Dq^epsilon` theorem
for every cell is equivalent, after replacing `epsilon` by `epsilon/2`, to
the full pair endpoint.  It is not a smaller arithmetic lemma.

### 9.2 Correct candidate: excess implies a charged packet

After a legal peeling step, retain the joint `(A,B)` mask and the original
rank-one color weights.  Prove

```text
polynomial all-plus excess
  => quantitatively large mergeable affine/Hankel packet
     + packet-free mask-stable S4 unconditionality.                (9.3)
```

The randomized `O(D)` theorem and the bound for an individual certified
packet are already proved.  What remains open is the weighted global
packing/participation of sparse actual-prime packets and unconditionality on
their complement.  Charges must be measured in the original factorial pair
energy, not merely in vertex or packet count.

The exact statement and its falsification criteria are recorded in
`ZETA23-QP-COLLECTIVE-DYADIC-NEXT-GATE-AND-ROUTE-AUDIT-2026-08-28.md`.

## 10. What was genuinely learned

This was not a proof, but it did produce several nontrivial corrections.

1. **The direct weighted pair theorem is the true north.**  Rooted,
   capacitary, packet, and two-index theorems are auxiliary unless their
   bridge to it is explicit.
2. **The most important mistake was high in the decision tree.**  We
   strengthened the quantifiers before exploiting the dependency among the
   pair variables.
3. **PAC is a domain-reduction mechanism, not the final estimate.**  Its
   success should be measured by the invariant forced on the residual core.
4. **Rooting alone does not reconstruct.**  It is useful only inside a
   common capacitary or square-function estimate that controls every root.
5. **Capacity is promising but subordinate.**  One common capacity can
   optimize a collective estimate; root-dependent capacities do not
   reconstruct the original weights.
6. **The old arbitrary two-index trace formula is over-scoped.**  If a
   trace-formula breakthrough exists, it should retain the collective joint
   determinant mask and the Cartesian pair weights.

## 11. Recommended next sprint

The next work should be deliberately narrow:

1. start with one exact signed `(A,B)` cell and one Cartesian color cut;
2. remove only already certified mergeable affine/Hankel packets, charging
   them in the original factorial energy;
3. test the remaining joint mask for a polynomial all-plus/randomized gap;
4. prove that any such gap yields another quantitatively large mergeable
   packet;
5. optimize the resulting two-sided weighted packet-participation loads.

The kill criterion is explicit: demote this route if packet extraction or
packing costs a fixed power of `q`, if the argument needs arbitrary-mask
unconditionality, or if it forgets the parent Cartesian masks after bicore
pruning.
