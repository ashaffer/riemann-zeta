## 0. What this document is, and is not

This is a research architecture, not a proof. Nothing in this investigation proves or disproves the Riemann Hypothesis, and no plan written by anyone can promise to. What the investigation produced is different and real: a set of working, cross-validated instruments; a body of new measurements at the current frontier of the one program (Weil positivity) where RH is equivalent to a statement being actively extended one prime at a time; a precisely posed open lemma whose truth would imply RH through that program; and today, a measured refinement of that lemma's required form. The architecture below aims every instrument at that lemma, keeps disproof channels running in parallel at all times, and attaches kill criteria and honest probability tiers so the program is falsifiable rather than aspirational.

The reader should hold two facts simultaneously. First: essentially every claimed proof of RH in history has been wrong, the problem has consumed 167 years of the strongest mathematicians, and the base rate for any new program is failure. Second: the specific gate this program targets — uniform positivity transfer across primes in the truncated Weil quadratic form — is where the field's own leading edge (Connes–Consani–Moscovici, arXiv:2310.18423; Connes–Consani, Selecta Math. 2021) currently stands, it admits finite, machine-checkable sub-statements, and the measurements reported here constrain its shape in a way that was not previously written down.

## 1. Problem state in brief

RH asserts all nontrivial zeros of ζ(s) have real part 1/2. The facts that frame this program:

**Equivalences used here.** Weil's criterion: RH holds iff a specific quadratic form (built from primes, a pole term, and an archimedean term via the explicit formula) is positive semidefinite on every compactly supported test function. Combinatorially, RH is equivalent to |M(n)| = O(n^{1/2+ε}) for the Mertens function, which is the determinant of the 0–1 Redheffer divisibility matrix. Elementarily, RH is equivalent to Robin's inequality σ(n) < e^γ n log log n for n > 5040, and to a Π₁ arithmetic sentence — so RH's *falsity*, if actual, has a finite certificate.

**Zero slack.** Rodgers–Tao proved the de Bruijn–Newman constant Λ ≥ 0; RH is equivalent to Λ ≤ 0. If RH is true, it is true with zero margin. Every phenomenon measured in this investigation re-encounters this knife-edge.

**The precedent.** RH is a *theorem* for zeta functions of curves over finite fields (Weil, Deligne), proved through an exact positivity (the Hodge index theorem on the self-product of the curve). The arithmetic analogue of that self-product does not exist yet. Ramanujan graphs realize the same positivity discretely, constructed by Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak *from* Deligne's theorem, and by Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava via interlacing families — a new positivity technology, invented in this decade, from computer science.

**Verification state.** On the order of 2×10¹³ zeros verified on the line (rigorously to 3×10¹² by Platt–Trudgian); all verification flows through one method family (Riemann–Siegel + Turing). Criterion-based checks (Li coefficients to ~3300, Maślanka/Coffey) trail this by roughly nine orders of magnitude in effective height.

**The live program.** Connes–Consani–Moscovici showed there is a property P(n), involving only Euler factors of primes below n, whose validity for all n is equivalent to RH, via Weil positivity on test functions of bounded support. *[Citation correction, July 26: the windowed-criterion lineage in print is Yoshida 1992 (RH ⟺ nondegeneracy of the windowed form for every support), Bombieri 2000/2003 (variational windows), with the CCM finite-matrix strategy per the 2602.04022 bibliography; arXiv:2310.18423 is the semilocal prolate-operator paper and does not itself state P(n). See results/agent-prior-art.md §7.3.]* Connes–Consani computed that the archimedean part of the form alone stays positive only slightly past support log 2, is rescued on [log 2, log 3) precisely by the prime 2's contribution, and that at support log 3 the positivity is destroyed if "2" is replaced by 1.9999 or 2.0005 — the form is arithmetic to a rigidity of 10⁻³. *[Sourcing flag, July 26: the 1.9999/2.0005 figures could not be located in arXiv:2006.13771 by the literature sweep; re-source before citing externally — results/agent-prior-art.md §7.3.]* A July 2026 preprint (arXiv:2607.02828) proves finite-cutoff theorems delimiting what finite numerics can certify about the truncated form. This is the gate.

## 2. Findings of this investigation

All computations below were performed and, where stated, cross-validated against independent oracles during the investigation.

**2.1 Spectral near-failures at the predicted rate.** A vectorized Riemann–Siegel scan of the first 35,644 zeros (census check: 35,643.63 predicted; deficit zero) yielded a record Lehmer-type pair at t = 17143.7865: gap 0.0353, and the function Z(t) rises to only 0.00215 between the two zeros against a typical local maximum of 2.36 — a 0.09% ascent. Five pairs were found below 7% of mean spacing. Small-gap counts run at 65–73% of the asymptotic GUE prediction at these heights, i.e., the zeros are *more* rigid than random matrices in the computable range, with the deviation itself known to be prime-structured (Bogomolny–Keating). Near-failures of RH occur at, indeed slightly below, the rate the spectral model demands: they are fingerprints of an operator, not cracks.

**2.2 The counterexample next door.** The Davenport–Heilbronn function (a twisted combination of mod-5 L-functions, satisfying a functional equation but lacking an Euler product) was constructed and its functional equation verified numerically; a zero was located at ρ = 0.808517182457 + 85.6993484854i with residual 10⁻²⁶. The functional equation alone does not imply RH; whatever makes RH true lives in the joint action of Euler product and functional equation. No known technique uses both at once.

**2.3 Combinatorial reframing and the guaranteed wildness.** The identity det(Redheffer R_n) = M(n) was verified exactly (−3, 1, 0 at n = 50, 100, 150); the R₅₀₀ spectrum shows the ±√n Perron pair and 491 eigenvalues pinned at 1. Sieving to 10⁷: max |M(n)|/√n = 0.4722. Against this, Hurst-type computations prove lim sup |M(n)|/√n exceeds ≈1.8: the RH-equivalent ratio will provably quadruple past everything in the computed range. Numerical comfort in this problem is structurally worthless — the strongest quantitative caution produced by the investigation.

**2.4 The Fourier instruments.** With prime powers weighted by Λ(n)n^{−1/2} and the pole of ζ ("the carrier") subtracted, spectral lines at known zero ordinates have heights 13.79, 13.26, 13.08, 12.90, 13.13 against log X = 13.82 at X = 10⁶, with an off-resonance floor near 0.8. A wide-band scan (t ∈ [10, 300], X from 10⁵ to 10⁶, primes only — ζ never evaluated) detected 138 lines against ~139 zeros expected, with growth ratios 1.209 ± 0.133 and maximum 1.526. This is a useful RH-consistency diagnostic independent of the Riemann–Siegel pipeline; the finite-X statistic does **not** by itself give a rigorous bound on an off-line real part β.

**2.5 The Guinand–Weil identity as a regression device.** With a Gaussian window, the two sides of the explicit formula were computed through independent code paths — left from 16 computed zeros; right from 11,000 prime powers plus the digamma integral. Window on γ₃: both sides 2.0007024351181021364, difference 1.5×10⁻²⁹. Off-window: 0.0833, difference 9×10⁻³⁰. These are strong normalization checks, not zero-exclusion certificates: an exclusion theorem would additionally need outward-rounded evaluations, rigorous prime/zero/integral tails, and a non-cancellation argument for possible off-line zero quartets.

## 3. The target lemma

**Uniform Prime Transfer (UPT), template form.** There exist κ > 0, an explicitly
**sign-preserving** normalization N_p of the truncated Weil form on the window
W_p = [2 log p, 2 log p⁺] (p and p⁺ consecutive prime-power thresholds), and
a constant c > 0 independent of p, such that for all L ∈ W_p,

  inf_{φ ∈ D_L, ‖φ‖=1} N_p[ Q_L^{(≤p)} ](φ)
      ≥ c · [dist(L, ∂W_p)]^κ.

Here sign-preserving means, for example, multiplication by a positive scalar
or congruence by an explicitly invertible map; an arbitrary "normalization"
would make the statement vacuous. On W_p, Q_L^{(≤p)} must be identified with
the full form Q_L because no later prime power meets the support.

The free parameters κ, N_p are to be *measured* by Track A below before any proof attempt; the July 2026 finite-cutoff theorems (arXiv:2607.02828) constrain which normalizations finite numerics can certify. *[Measured, July 25: the finite Galerkin ladders show no resolved threshold knife-edge — the dist^κ factor is not supported by those data — and the empirical normalization divides out the smooth envelope ln λ_m ≈ 10.2 − 1.755·e^{L/2}(L/2+4.0); see §2.15. The template is kept as originally posed for the record.]* With the sign-preserving and full-domain clauses above, UPT for all p plus continuity at window boundaries yields Weil positivity, hence RH. The analytic goal is an inequality "threshold deficit ≤ structured rescue" derived from this structure alone — via Cauchy-interlacing bounds for structured updates, finite free convolution (Marcus) for the spectral flow as primes convolve in, and MSS-style averaging over the family of forms indexed by prime subsets. This is the ensemble-to-individual bridge attempted at the one place where the "individual" (the full form) is the *limit* of the ensemble (partial prime sets) rather than a member of it — a configuration the MSS obstruction does not obviously forbid. The Redheffer spectral picture (§2.3) is the discrete shadow of the same question and serves as the toy model.

**Track D — the disproof channel.** Valid terminal witnesses include a rigorously evaluated Robin violation or a negative Rayleigh quotient for an explicit admissible test function after the full analytic form and all support-allowed prime powers are enclosed. Lehmer, LLL, and prime-side rogue scans are diagnostics unless a separate theorem converts their output into such a witness; finite-X line growth alone is not an RH kill criterion.

**Track E — the object hunt, run as experimental physics.** Candidate constructions can be compared with the finite Galerkin spectra, zero statistics, and prime-sign experiments as empirical diagnostics. The old threshold-collapse constraint is withdrawn, and the L=1.75 values are finite upper bounds rather than established operator margins. Agreement with these measurements is neither necessary nor sufficient for an RH proof.

**Track F — logical status (proof theory; independent of the numerical tracks).** RH has Π₁ formulations. Once a particular equivalence and proof predicate are formalized, a true negation would have a finite Σ₁ witness provable in weak arithmetic; hence a metatheoretic proof that ZFC cannot prove ¬RH would already imply that RH is true. This does not make an independence proof impossible: a true Π₁ sentence can be independent, but proving that would require genuine nonprovability and consistency-strength work not supplied by any finite matrix here. Concrete, appropriately scoped tasks are: formalize one Π₁ equivalence and the ZFC proof predicate; state all ambient consistency/soundness assumptions; calibrate the explicit formula and related analysis in reverse mathematics; and audit existing Turing-machine encodings. Ordinary CH-style forcing does not alter arithmetic truth in the way needed. No current Track F artifact advances a proof of independence.

## 6. Reproducibility: the normalization ledger

Everything downstream depends on conventions; these were numerically cross-checked to 29 digits (§2.5), not formally certified. For an admissible even analytic test h with transform g(u) = ∫h(r)e^{−iur}dr, the implemented explicit-formula convention is

  Σ_ρ h(γ_ρ) = h(i/2) + h(−i/2) + (1/2π)∫h(r)[Re ψ₀(1/4 + ir/2) − log π]dr − (1/π)Σ_n Λ(n) n^{−1/2} g(log n),

zeros counted with ordinates of both signs, where ρ = 1/2+iγ_ρ and γ_ρ may be complex off the critical line. For the quadratic form the analytic choice is h(z)=φ̂(z)φ̂(−z), not the non-analytic notation |φ̂(z)|². For real φ and real r these agree, and g(log n)=2πψ_φ(log n); only if the relevant zeros lie on the critical line does the zero side become a sum of nonnegative squares. The pole term is 2(∫φe^{x/2})(∫φe^{−x/2}), and the prime term is 2ΣΛ(n)n^{−1/2}ψ_φ(log n). Hat-basis closed forms: overlap of unit hats of half-width d at center distance t·d is d(2/3 − t² + t³/2) for t ≤ 1, d(2−t)³/6 for 1 ≤ t ≤ 2; ∫hat·e^{sx} = e^{sx_c}(2cosh(sd) − 2)/(s²d). Every assembled form is numerically spot-checked against the on-line-zero square sum as an RH-conditional regression oracle. Candidate negativity must be adjudicated from the prime side with rigorous enclosures and tails. Rayleigh–Ritz makes every finite λ_m an upper bound on λ(L): positive λ_m is evidence, while a rigorously bridged negative explicit test vector is a theorem.

## 7. Related work and priority caveats
