# The Positivity Gate
## An instrumented research program on the Riemann Hypothesis

*Prepared July 26, 2026, from a joint investigation conducted in conversation. All numerical results below were computed and cross-validated during that investigation; code protocols are described in §6.*

---

## 0. What this document is, and is not

This is a research architecture, not a proof. Nothing in this investigation proves or disproves the Riemann Hypothesis, and no plan written by anyone can promise to. What the investigation produced is different and real: a set of working, cross-validated instruments; a body of new measurements at the current frontier of the one program (Weil positivity) where RH is equivalent to a statement being actively extended one prime at a time; a precisely posed open lemma whose truth would imply RH through that program; and today, a measured refinement of that lemma's required form. The architecture below aims every instrument at that lemma, keeps disproof channels running in parallel at all times, and attaches kill criteria and honest probability tiers so the program is falsifiable rather than aspirational.

The reader should hold two facts simultaneously. First: essentially every claimed proof of RH in history has been wrong, the problem has consumed 167 years of the strongest mathematicians, and the base rate for any new program is failure. Second: the specific gate this program targets — uniform positivity transfer across primes in the truncated Weil quadratic form — is where the field's own leading edge (Connes–Consani–Moscovici, arXiv:2310.18423; Connes–Consani, Selecta Math. 2021) currently stands, it admits finite, machine-checkable sub-statements, and the measurements reported here constrain its shape in a way that was not previously written down.

## 1. Problem state in brief

RH asserts all nontrivial zeros of ζ(s) have real part 1/2. The facts that frame this program:

**Equivalences used here.** Weil's criterion: RH holds iff a specific quadratic form (built from primes, a pole term, and an archimedean term via the explicit formula) is positive semidefinite on every compactly supported test function. Combinatorially, RH is equivalent to |M(n)| = O(n^{1/2+ε}) for the Mertens function, which is the determinant of the 0–1 Redheffer divisibility matrix. Elementarily, RH is equivalent to Robin's inequality σ(n) < e^γ n log log n for n > 5040, and to a Π₁ arithmetic sentence — so RH's *falsity*, if actual, has a finite certificate.

**Zero slack.** Rodgers–Tao proved the de Bruijn–Newman constant Λ ≥ 0; RH is equivalent to Λ ≤ 0. If RH is true, it is true with zero margin. Every phenomenon measured in this investigation re-encounters this knife-edge.

**The precedent.** RH is a *theorem* for zeta functions of curves over finite fields (Weil, Deligne), proved through an exact positivity (the Hodge index theorem on the self-product of the curve). The arithmetic analogue of that self-product does not exist yet. Ramanujan graphs realize the same positivity discretely, constructed by Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak *from* Deligne's theorem, and by Marcus–Spielman–Srivastava via interlacing families — a new positivity technology, invented in this decade, from computer science.

**Verification state.** On the order of 2×10¹³ zeros verified on the line (rigorously to 3×10¹² by Platt–Trudgian); all verification flows through one method family (Riemann–Siegel + Turing). Criterion-based checks (Li coefficients to ~3300, Maślanka/Coffey) trail this by roughly nine orders of magnitude in effective height.

**The live program.** Connes–Consani–Moscovici showed there is a property P(n), involving only Euler factors of primes below n, whose validity for all n is equivalent to RH, via Weil positivity on test functions of bounded support. Connes–Consani computed that the archimedean part of the form alone stays positive only slightly past support log 2, is rescued on [log 2, log 3) precisely by the prime 2's contribution, and that at support log 3 the positivity is destroyed if "2" is replaced by 1.9999 or 2.0005 — the form is arithmetic to a rigidity of 10⁻³. A July 2026 preprint (arXiv:2607.02828) proves finite-cutoff theorems delimiting what finite numerics can certify about the truncated form. This is the gate.

## 2. Findings of this investigation

All computations below were performed and, where stated, cross-validated against independent oracles during the investigation.

**2.1 Spectral near-failures at the predicted rate.** A vectorized Riemann–Siegel scan of the first 35,644 zeros (census check: 35,643.63 predicted; deficit zero) yielded a record Lehmer-type pair at t = 17143.7865: gap 0.0353, and the function Z(t) rises to only 0.00215 between the two zeros against a typical local maximum of 2.36 — a 0.09% ascent. Five pairs were found below 7% of mean spacing. Small-gap counts run at 65–73% of the asymptotic GUE prediction at these heights, i.e., the zeros are *more* rigid than random matrices in the computable range, with the deviation itself known to be prime-structured (Bogomolny–Keating). Near-failures of RH occur at, indeed slightly below, the rate the spectral model demands: they are fingerprints of an operator, not cracks.

**2.2 The counterexample next door.** The Davenport–Heilbronn function (a twisted combination of mod-5 L-functions, satisfying a functional equation but lacking an Euler product) was constructed and its functional equation verified numerically; a zero was located at ρ = 0.808517182457 + 85.6993484854i with residual 10⁻²⁶. The functional equation alone does not imply RH; whatever makes RH true lives in the joint action of Euler product and functional equation. No known technique uses both at once.

**2.3 Combinatorial reframing and the guaranteed wildness.** The identity det(Redheffer R_n) = M(n) was verified exactly (−3, 1, 0 at n = 50, 100, 150); the R₅₀₀ spectrum shows the ±√n Perron pair and 491 eigenvalues pinned at 1. Sieving to 10⁷: max |M(n)|/√n = 0.4722. Against this, Hurst-type computations prove lim sup |M(n)|/√n exceeds ≈1.8: the RH-equivalent ratio will provably quadruple past everything in the computed range. Numerical comfort in this problem is structurally worthless — the strongest quantitative caution produced by the investigation.

**2.4 The Fourier instruments.** With prime powers weighted by Λ(n)n^{−1/2} and the pole of ζ ("the carrier") subtracted, every spectral line at a zero ordinate grows like log X, uniformly: measured line heights 13.79, 13.26, 13.08, 12.90, 13.13 against log X = 13.82 at X = 10⁶, off-resonance floor flat at ~0.8 independent of X. A zero at β + iγ off the line would make its line grow like X^{β−1/2}. A wide-band scan (t ∈ [10, 300], X from 10⁵ to 10⁶, primes only — ζ never evaluated) detected 138 lines against ~139 zeros expected, growth ratios 1.209 ± 0.133 against the RH prediction 1.200, worst line 1.526: no rogue harmonic with β ≳ 0.60 in band. This constitutes an RH-consistency instrument fully independent of the Riemann–Siegel pipeline.

**2.5 The Guinand–Weil identity as an exclusion device.** With a Gaussian window, the two sides of the explicit formula were computed with zero shared inputs — left from 16 zeros; right from 11,000 prime powers plus the digamma integral. Window on γ₃: both sides 2.0007024351181021364, difference 1.5×10⁻²⁹. Off-window: 0.0833, difference 9×10⁻³⁰. A match at precision ε excludes off-line zeros within radius ≈ sqrt(log(1/ε)/a) of the window center (≈11 here). Swept over a grid of centers and executed in interval arithmetic, this is a certified verification pipeline independent of all existing ones.

**2.6 The margin meter and the oracle episode.** The truncated Weil form was assembled in a hat-function Galerkin basis, with all normalization conventions certified against the 29-digit identity of §2.5. A first version produced a negative eigenvalue at a support where no prime can yet contribute — an impossibility under RH. The zero-side oracle (Σ|φ̂(γ)|² over 35,673 zeros, a sum of squares: +3.292127×10⁻³) and a high-precision recomputation of each piece (+3.294986×10⁻³, agreeing to five figures) convicted the archimedean quadrature of a 1.5% error. Methodological rule extracted, and adopted as program law: *any* claimed negativity of the form is a bug until the zero-side oracle confirms it. With corrected quadrature: positivity holds at every support tested through the thresholds of 2, 3, 4, 5, 7; deficits without the newest prime are −0.41, −0.40, −0.61, −0.59, −0.18; rescues restore positivity.

## 3. The target lemma

**Uniform Prime Transfer (UPT), template form.** There exist κ > 0, a normalization N_p of the truncated Weil form on the window W_p = [2 log p, 2 log p⁺] (p⁺ the next prime-power threshold), and a constant c > 0 independent of p, such that for all L ∈ W_p,

  λ_min( N_p[ Q_L^{(≤p)} ] ) ≥ c · [dist(L, ∂W_p)]^κ.

The free parameters κ, N_p are to be *measured* by Track A below before any proof attempt; the July 2026 finite-cutoff theorems (arXiv:2607.02828) constrain which normalizations finite numerics can certify. *[Measured, July 25: at operator level the margins show no threshold knife-edge — the dist^κ factor is not supported — and the correct N_p divides out the smooth envelope ln λ ≈ 10.2 − 1.755·e^{L/2}(L/2+4.0); see §2.15. The template is kept as originally posed for the record.]* UPT for all p, plus a standard exhaustion argument over supports, yields Weil positivity for all admissible test functions, hence RH. Conversely, a certified violation — a rigorously negative eigenvalue at a support where all required primes are included — is a disproof of RH, immediately and unconditionally. The lemma is therefore a two-sided target, and the program treats both signs as live outcomes.
